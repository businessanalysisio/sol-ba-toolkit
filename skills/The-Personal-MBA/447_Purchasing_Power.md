# Purchasing Power

## Overview

Purchasing Power is the total amount you can actually deploy right now — the sum of cash on hand plus available credit and other resources you can quickly convert to spending. Kaufman distinguishes it from profit or net worth because those can be large while your real ability to act is small: a profitable business whose money is tied up in inventory or unpaid invoices may have very little purchasing power at the moment an opportunity or emergency arrives. Purchasing power is what determines whether you can seize a bulk discount, cover an unexpected bill, or fund a promising experiment before the window closes. It's a snapshot of financial flexibility, and it fluctuates with the timing of money in and out. Maintaining healthy purchasing power — through cash reserves and access to credit — is what lets a business respond rather than freeze when circumstances change.

## Context & Usage

Track purchasing power, not just profit, when you need the ability to act quickly. Example: a retailer offered a one-time 40% discount for paying a supplier upfront can only capture it if it has the ready cash or credit — a business showing paper profit but no available funds simply watches the opportunity pass. Keep a reserve and open lines of credit so purchasing power exists before you need it, and be wary of decisions (over-buying inventory, slow-paying customers) that lock up resources and quietly drain it. Flexibility to move at the right moment is often worth more than squeezing out the last bit of return.
