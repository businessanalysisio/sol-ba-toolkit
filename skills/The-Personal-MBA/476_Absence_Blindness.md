# Absence Blindness

## Overview

We cannot perceive what isn't there. The mind registers presence — events, objects, problems — but has no sensory channel for absence, which is why prevented disasters earn no gratitude and missing elements escape notice. Kaufman's canonical illustration: the manager whose team quietly avoids crises looks less impressive than the firefighter who heroically resolves the crises a better manager would never have allowed, so organizations systematically over-reward cure and under-reward prevention.

## Context & Usage

Compensate with explicit checks for what's missing. In requirements work, the costliest defects are the unstated assumptions and absent edge cases no reviewer "sees" — use checklists and structured walkthroughs precisely because unaided perception won't flag gaps. In management, make prevention visible: track near-misses avoided, uptime maintained, churn that didn't happen, and credit the people responsible. When evaluating a pitch or plan, ask deliberately "what is not mentioned here?" — the omissions are invisible by default and that is exactly where the risk lives.
