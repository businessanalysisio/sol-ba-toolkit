# Iteration Velocity

## Overview

Iteration velocity is the speed at which you can complete full iteration cycles — how quickly you can go from trying something to learning from it and trying the next thing. Kaufman argues this is one of the most important variables in improving anything, because the total learning you accumulate is roughly the number of cycles you complete, and the faster each cycle, the more you complete in the same span of time. A team that can test a change in a day will out-learn one that takes a month, even if the slower team's individual ideas are better. The practical lever, therefore, is not just having good ideas but relentlessly shortening the loop — reducing the time to build, ship, observe, and decide.

## Context & Usage

Optimize iteration velocity whenever you are stuck improving slowly. If shipping a change to your product takes weeks of approvals and manual deployment, the highest-value work may be automating that pipeline so experiments take hours — because that multiplies every future improvement. An e-commerce team that can run and read an A/B test in three days will discover a winning checkout flow long before a competitor whose test cycle is a quarter. Look for the slowest step in your loop and attack it; that bottleneck, not idea quality, is usually what limits progress.
