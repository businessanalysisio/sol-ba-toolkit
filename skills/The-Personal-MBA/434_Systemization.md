# Systemization

## Overview

Systemization is the work of turning a process into a documented, repeatable system that produces the same result regardless of who runs it. Kaufman treats it as what separates a business from a job: if the value only gets delivered when the founder personally does it, there is no business, just a demanding self-employment. Systemizing means capturing how a task is done — the steps, standards, and decisions — into checklists, procedures, and rules that someone else (or a machine) can follow to the same standard. This is the enabler for delegation, predictability, and multiplication all at once: you can only hand off, reliably repeat, or replicate a process that has been made explicit. The effort is front-loaded — writing the system takes longer than just doing the task once — but it converts a recurring personal burden into an asset the business owns.

## Context & Usage

Systemize any task that is done repeatedly, matters to quality, and currently lives only in someone's head. Example: a founder who is the only person who knows how to onboard a new client writes that process into a step-by-step checklist, then trains a hire against it — freeing the founder and making onboarding consistent no matter who does it. Start with the highest-frequency or highest-risk processes, document them plainly enough that a newcomer could follow along, and refine the system as reality reveals gaps. The signal you need systemization is a business that stalls whenever a key person is unavailable.
