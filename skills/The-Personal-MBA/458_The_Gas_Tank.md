# The Gas Tank

## Overview

The Gas Tank is Kaufman's metaphor for the finite reserve of physical and mental energy every person runs on. Like a car, you start the day with a certain amount of fuel, every activity draws it down, and when the tank hits empty, performance collapses no matter how motivated you are — you cannot will yourself past a depleted tank. The fuel is replenished by concrete inputs: food, water, sleep, and rest. Crucially, mental and physical energy draw from the same tank, which is why a day of hard thinking leaves you physically drained and why poor sleep wrecks your decision-making. Treating energy as a manageable resource with real limits, rather than assuming you can simply push through, is the core move.

## Context & Usage

Apply this whenever output is falling and the instinct is to try harder — often the honest diagnosis is an empty tank, and the fix is refueling, not more effort. Schedule demanding cognitive work for when the tank is full (for most people, earlier in the day) and routine tasks for the low-energy trough. Protect the inputs that refill it: skimping on sleep to work longer is borrowing fuel you will repay with interest in bad judgment. A founder pulling repeated late nights and wondering why decisions keep going wrong is running on fumes; the model reframes rest and food not as indulgences but as the maintenance that keeps the whole engine running. It connects directly to Energy Cycles (501) and Stress and Recovery (502).
