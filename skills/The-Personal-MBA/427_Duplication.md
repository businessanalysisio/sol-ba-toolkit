# Duplication

## Overview

Duplication is the ability to reproduce the delivery of value at very low marginal cost. Kaufman points to it as the quiet engine behind the most scalable businesses: once you have created something duplicable — a piece of software, a recorded course, a book, a digital file — making the ten-thousandth copy costs essentially nothing beyond what the first one cost. Physical goods duplicate poorly (each unit consumes real materials and labor), while information duplicates almost perfectly. This is why value that can be encoded as information tends toward extraordinary economics: the creation cost is fixed and one-time, and every additional sale is nearly pure margin. Duplication is the foundation that multiplication and scale build on — you cannot cheaply multiply what you cannot cheaply duplicate.

## Context & Usage

Look for duplication when you want revenue to grow without a matching growth in per-unit effort. Example: a consultant capped by billable hours records their methodology as an online course; the recording is made once, then sold to thousands, breaking the one-to-one link between hours worked and income earned. When designing an offer, ask which parts of your value creation can be turned into a reproducible asset versus which must be re-performed for each customer. The more of your value you can shift into the duplicable column, the better your margins and the higher your ceiling.
