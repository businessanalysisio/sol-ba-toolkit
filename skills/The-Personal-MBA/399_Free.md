# Free

## Overview

"Free" is one of the most powerful words in marketing because it collapses the barrier to trying something — but Kaufman treats it as a double-edged tool that must be used with care. Offering something free is superb for capturing attention and earning permission to keep marketing, since it lets prospects sample value with no risk. The danger is that free also attracts people who will never pay and can devalue the offer or overwhelm you with non-buyers, so free must always sit inside a deliberate path toward a paid transaction. Used well, free is a doorway: a sample, a trial, or a genuinely useful giveaway that builds trust and moves the right people toward buying. Used carelessly, it simply trains an audience to expect everything for nothing.

## Context & Usage

Use free strategically when you need to lower resistance and start a relationship with probable purchasers. A software company offering a free tier or trial lets users experience the value firsthand, then converts the subset who need more — provided there's a clear, compelling reason to upgrade. The key questions are always: does this free thing attract the right people, and is there a designed path from free to paid? If a giveaway pulls in freebie-seekers who will never buy, or if there's no upgrade route, the free offer is a leak, not a funnel. Pair free with permission so the attention it earns becomes an ongoing marketing asset.
