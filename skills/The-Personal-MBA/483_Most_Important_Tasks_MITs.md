# Most Important Tasks (MITs)

## Overview

Not everything on the list matters equally, so each day begins by naming the two or three tasks that will create the most value — the Most Important Tasks — and doing them first, before the reactive flood arrives. The practice guarantees that even a day derailed by surprises still moved the needle, because the needle-moving happened in the protected first hours when energy and willpower were at their peak.

## Context & Usage

Use MITs as the daily bridge between strategy and calendar. A founder whose quarterly priority is landing ten pilot customers should see outreach or pilot follow-up as an MIT most mornings; if a week of MIT lists contains no trace of the stated priority, the strategy is fiction. Pick the MITs the evening before or first thing on arrival, keep them to three or fewer, and finish at least one before opening email — the inbox is other people's priorities, and it will happily consume the whole day if given the opening slot.
