# Costs: Fixed and Variable

## Overview

Costs split into two kinds that behave very differently as sales change. Fixed costs stay the same no matter how much you produce or sell — rent, salaried staff, insurance — you pay them whether you sell zero units or ten thousand. Variable costs rise and fall with each unit — raw materials, shipping, payment processing, per-unit labor — the more you make, the more you spend. Kaufman emphasizes that understanding your cost structure is essential because it governs how your business behaves at different volumes. A business heavy in fixed costs loses money badly at low volume but becomes very profitable once it clears the fixed base, since additional sales add only variable cost. A business that is mostly variable cost is safer at low volume but never enjoys much economy of scale. The mix determines your breakeven point and how each extra sale affects profit.

## Context & Usage

Classify your costs when modeling a new business, pricing, or forecasting profit at different sales levels. Example: a software company (nearly all fixed cost — build it once) bleeds money until it hits enough users, then each new user is almost pure profit; a handmade-goods maker (mostly variable cost — materials per item) earns a steady thin margin from the first sale but never sees costs melt away with scale. Use the split to compute breakeven and to understand your risk: high fixed cost is a bet on reaching volume, high variable cost is a safer but lower-ceiling structure.
