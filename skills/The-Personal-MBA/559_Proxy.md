# Proxy

## Overview

A Proxy is a measurable stand-in for something you care about but can't measure directly. Many of the things that matter most in business — customer satisfaction, employee morale, brand strength, product quality — resist direct counting, so you substitute something you can count: repeat-purchase rate for satisfaction, ticket volume for quality, hours worked for effort. Kaufman treats proxies as necessary but hazardous, because a proxy is never the real thing, only correlated with it, and the correlation can break — especially once people know the proxy is being watched. The moment a proxy becomes a target, effort flows to moving the proxy rather than improving what it was meant to represent: measure a call center on call duration and agents rush customers off the line, hitting the number while gutting the service quality the metric was standing in for.

## Context & Usage

Use proxies deliberately when the true objective is unmeasurable, but keep two habits. First, name the gap explicitly — state what the proxy actually is versus what you wish you could measure — so no one forgets it's a substitute. Second, watch the seam between proxy and reality, and periodically check the real outcome directly (talk to customers, not just read the retention number) to confirm the proxy still tracks it. Prefer proxies that are hard to game and, where you can, triangulate with more than one so a single distorted measure can't run the decision. This is the mechanism behind vanity KPIs (549) and a frequent source of the correlation-causation error (557).
