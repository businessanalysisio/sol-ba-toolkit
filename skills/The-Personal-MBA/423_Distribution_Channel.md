# Distribution Channel

## Overview

A Distribution Channel is the path your offer travels to reach the end user. Kaufman distinguishes direct channels — you sell straight to the customer (your own store, website, sales team) — from indirect channels that route through intermediaries (wholesalers, retailers, marketplaces, resellers). Each channel choice is a trade-off between reach and control: intermediaries give you access to customers and infrastructure you could never build alone, but they take a margin, own the customer relationship, and dictate terms. Selling direct preserves margin and the customer relationship but forces you to do all the work of finding and serving buyers. The same product can move through several channels at once, and a good channel isn't just about logistics — it shapes who sees your offer, at what price, and with what experience attached.

## Context & Usage

Choose channels deliberately when launching or expanding a product, and revisit the mix when margins or customer relationships feel constrained. Example: a maker selling handmade goods lists on a marketplace for instant reach (indirect), then builds a direct store to recapture the marketplace's cut and own the buyer's email for repeat sales. Weigh each channel on margin taken, control of the relationship, volume it can move, and how well it fits the buying habits of your target customer. Relying on a single powerful intermediary is a hidden risk — if it changes terms or drops you, your whole distribution collapses.
