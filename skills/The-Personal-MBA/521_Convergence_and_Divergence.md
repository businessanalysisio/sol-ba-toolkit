# Convergence and Divergence

## Overview

Two opposing social forces that continuously reshape groups. Convergence: members of a group become more alike over time — habits, vocabulary, dress, opinions drift toward the group norm, because conformity is rewarded and deviation is sanctioned. Divergence: groups actively differentiate themselves from *other* groups — when outsiders adopt a clan's markers, the clan moves on to new ones. Together they explain fashion cycles, slang turnover, and why a trend dies precisely when it goes mainstream: mass adoption erases the distinction the early clan valued.

## Context & Usage

Apply when timing markets and managing culture. In marketing: a product that spreads via an exclusive early clan will lose that clan once the mass market converges on it — plan either to ride successive waves (streetwear drops, limited editions) or to reposition for the mainstream and let the innovators go. In organizations: convergence is why a strong culture self-polices without rules, and also why homogeneous teams stop generating dissenting ideas — deliberately importing outsiders is the counterweight. Watch for divergence signals in your niche: when your early adopters start mocking your brand as "for everyone now," the cycle has turned.
