# Conservation of Energy

## Overview

Kaufman's observation that the human brain and body are tuned by evolution to spend as little energy as possible: loafing is the biological default, and people act only when the expected reward or threat clearly justifies the caloric expenditure. What looks like laziness or procrastination is ancient energy budgeting at work — for most of human history, wasting effort on non-essential activity could be fatal, so we inherited a strong bias toward the path of least resistance.

## Context & Usage

Apply it whenever behavior needs to change — yours, an employee's, or a customer's. Assume everyone will take the lowest-effort path and design accordingly: a checkout that requires creating an account before paying loses buyers not because they dislike the product but because the extra effort tips the energy calculation against acting. Reduce steps, set good defaults, and make the desired action the easy one instead of relying on exhortation. It also explains why you skip the gym after a demanding workday: the energy budget is spent, not the intention.
