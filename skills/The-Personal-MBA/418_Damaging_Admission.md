# Damaging Admission

## Overview

A Damaging Admission is the counterintuitive tactic of voluntarily disclosing a weakness or negative fact about your offer — and thereby making everything else you say more believable. Kaufman's logic is that buyers assume every seller is spinning, so a seller who openly concedes a genuine drawback signals honesty, and that credibility transfers to the seller's positive claims. Admitting "this software has a steep learning curve" or "our product costs more than the alternatives" disarms skepticism far more effectively than a flawless pitch, which reads as too good to be true. The admission also lets you frame the flaw on your own terms and pair it with a reason to buy anyway. It works precisely because it is costly to the seller in the short term, which is what makes it a trustworthy signal.

## Context & Usage

Use a damaging admission when you sense the prospect is guarded, or when your offer has an obvious weakness they will discover regardless. Example: a premium supplier tells a buyer up front, "We're the most expensive option on your list — here's why our failure rate is a third of the cheaper ones," turning the price objection into a proof point and making the comparison feel candid. Choose a real, relevant flaw rather than a trivial one, and never fabricate a fake weakness — the tactic depends on genuine honesty to earn the trust it buys.
