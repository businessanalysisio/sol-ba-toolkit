# Social Signals

## Overview

Much of what people buy is bought to be *seen*: a social signal is any visible marker that communicates status, wealth, taste, or group membership to others. A Rolex tells time no better than a $30 quartz watch; the extra $9,970 buys the signal. Kaufman's insight is that signaling value is real value — customers rationally pay premiums for products that raise their standing with the audiences they care about — and that signals only work if they are visible and hard to fake.

## Context & Usage

Use when pricing and designing products with a status dimension. Ask: who does my customer want to impress, and how does my product help them do it? Then make the signal legible — distinctive design, recognizable branding, shareable moments (unboxing, badges, "sent from" footers, conference swag). This explains otherwise-irrational patterns: why luxury brands shun discounts (cheap signals are worthless), why enterprise buyers favor prestigious vendors ("nobody gets fired for buying IBM" is a career-safety signal), and why free users display "Pro" badges. If your product is invisible in use, engineer visibility or accept competing purely on function and price.
