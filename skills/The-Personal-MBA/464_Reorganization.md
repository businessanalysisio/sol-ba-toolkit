# Reorganization

## Overview

From William T. Powers' perceptual control theory, which Kaufman builds on throughout The Human Mind: when your existing actions repeatedly fail to bring a perception to its reference level, the brain begins reorganizing — semi-random rewiring, trial-and-error at the neural level — until it stumbles onto something that works. Subjectively, reorganization feels like confusion, frustration, and anxiety; it is the uncomfortable middle stage of every genuine skill acquisition or adaptation, not a sign that you are failing.

## Context & Usage

Use this model to normalize the messy middle. A new founder learning sales, a manager promoted beyond their playbook, a business pivoting after its channel dries up — all will pass through a period where old responses stop working and new ones haven't stabilized. Expecting the discomfort prevents premature quitting: the frustration of week three learning cold outreach is the reorganization process running, not evidence you "aren't a salesperson." Persist through it deliberately, and shorten it with faster feedback.
