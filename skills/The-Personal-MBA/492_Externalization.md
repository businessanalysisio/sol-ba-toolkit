# Externalization

## Overview

Externalization is the practice of getting thoughts out of your head and into the external world — onto paper, a whiteboard, or a screen — where you can see, arrange, and work with them. Kaufman's premise is that working memory is tiny and unreliable: the mind is superb at generating ideas and terrible at holding them, and every unfinished thought you try to keep "in mind" consumes attention and creates a low hum of stress. Once externalized, an idea stops occupying mental RAM, becomes an object you can examine from outside, and can be combined with other externalized ideas in ways that are impossible while everything is swirling internally. Writing is thinking made visible, and seeing your reasoning laid out reveals gaps and errors the internal version glosses over.

## Context & Usage

Reach for this whenever you feel mentally overloaded, are wrestling with a complex decision, or cannot get traction on a problem. A "brain dump" — writing down every open loop, worry, and task without editing — reliably lowers anxiety because the brain trusts that the items are now captured and stops rehearsing them. When a decision feels tangled, externalizing the options and their pros and cons into a table almost always clarifies it, because the constraint of writing forces the fuzzy consideration into concrete form. It is the natural first step before identifying a Next Action (491) and before Self-Elicitation (493): you cannot interrogate or prioritize thoughts you have not yet gotten out of your head.
