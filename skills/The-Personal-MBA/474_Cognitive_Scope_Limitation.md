# Cognitive Scope Limitation

## Overview

Human attention and comprehension have hard caps. We can hold only a few items in working memory at once, and we can maintain only about 150 genuine relationships — Dunbar's number — before people become abstractions. Beyond these limits the mind doesn't scale up; it simplifies, chunks, and abstracts, trading fidelity for tractability. Kaufman's point is that no manager, founder, or analyst actually comprehends a large business directly — everyone operates on simplified models of it.

## Context & Usage

Design for the cap instead of pretending it isn't there. Dashboards should surface three to five decisive numbers, not forty; org structures work better as small teams of teams than as one leader "knowing everyone" past the ~150 threshold; strategy documents that require holding twelve priorities in mind produce zero priorities in practice. When a business grows beyond direct comprehension, invest in deliberate abstractions — segments, KPIs, exception reports — and stay suspicious of them, because every abstraction hides something.
