# Deconstruction

## Overview

Deconstruction is the analytical move of taking a system apart into its component processes so you can understand and improve each one on its own terms. A business looks like a single blur of activity until you break it into its actual steps — attract attention, capture a lead, convert to a sale, deliver the product, collect payment, retain the customer — at which point each step becomes a distinct thing you can measure, question, and fix. Kaufman's claim is that you cannot meaningfully analyze or improve a system you only perceive as a whole; the leverage appears once it is decomposed, because now you can see which specific stage is weak, where work piles up, and what would happen if you changed one part. A checkout process that "just feels slow" is opaque; broken into its discrete stages, the one that actually loses customers becomes visible and addressable.

## Context & Usage

Reach for deconstruction at the start of nearly any improvement effort: before optimizing a process, map its steps; before diagnosing a drop in results, break the outcome into the chain of sub-outcomes that produce it. It is what makes a funnel analysis possible — total conversion tells you little, but conversion at each step tells you exactly where the leak is. Decompose to the level where each part is something you could measure or change, and no further, since over-fragmenting buries the signal in detail. Deconstruction is the first step of analysis; Measurement (548) and Segmentation (560) act on the parts it exposes.
