# Bootstrapping

## Overview

Bootstrapping is building a business using only your own resources and the revenue it generates, avoiding outside funding altogether. Kaufman champions it as the default worth trying first because it keeps you at the top of the funding hierarchy: no debt to repay, no investors to answer to, and full ownership and control retained. The constraint of having no outside money is also a discipline — it forces you to reach profitability quickly, keep overhead low, and let real customer revenue (not a funding round) validate the business. Bootstrapping trades speed and scale for independence and resilience: you likely grow slower than a well-funded competitor, but you own everything, owe nothing, and can't be forced into decisions by a lender or board. Many durable, profitable businesses are built this way, funded entirely by customers rather than capital.

## Context & Usage

Bootstrap when independence, control, and lower risk matter more than winning a land-grab race that demands outside capital. Example: a software founder builds a product nights and weekends, launches it to paying customers, and reinvests the early revenue to grow — never raising money, never diluting, and reaching sustainable profit under their own terms. Bootstrapping fits businesses that can generate revenue early and don't need heavy upfront capital; it fits poorly where large investment must precede any income. Keep overhead minimal and let paying customers fund each next step, so the business proves itself before it grows.
