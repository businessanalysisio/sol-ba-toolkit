# Reason Why

## Overview

People comply with requests far more readily when the request comes with a reason attached. The canonical evidence Kaufman cites is Ellen Langer's Xerox-queue experiment: asking "May I use the copy machine?" won 60% compliance, while adding a justification — even the near-empty "because I have to make copies" — pushed compliance above 90%. The word "because" acts as a compliance trigger; the human mind treats a stated rationale as a signal that the request is legitimate, largely independent of how substantive the rationale actually is.

## Context & Usage

Attach a reason to every request you make of customers, colleagues, and stakeholders: why the deadline matters, why you need the data in this format, why the price is changing. In practice this costs one sentence and measurably lifts response rates — "Please review the spec by Friday because development starts Monday" outperforms the bare ask. It also works defensively: when you notice yourself agreeing to something, check whether the "because" you were given actually holds up, or whether it was Langer-empty.
