# Management

## Overview

Kaufman strips management of its mystique: it is simply the act of coordinating a group of people toward a specific goal while accounting for change and uncertainty — logistics, not charisma. His six principles of effective management: (1) recruit the smallest group of people who can do the job well; (2) communicate the desired end result, who is responsible for what, and the current state — clearly and often (Commander's Intent); (3) treat people with the Golden Trifecta of appreciation, courtesy, and respect; (4) create an environment where everyone can be productive, then get out of the way; (5) refrain from unrealistic expectations about certainty and prediction — plan conservatively; (6) measure to see whether what you're doing is working, and adapt.

## Context & Usage

Use the six principles as a standing audit whenever a team underperforms: the failure almost always maps to one of them — a bloated team (1), fuzzy goals or hidden status (2), corrosive treatment (3), a distraction-saturated environment (4), fantasy deadlines (5), or no feedback on results (6). Example: a chronically late product team is more often suffering principles 2 and 5 (unclear intent plus planning-fallacy schedules) than lack of talent. Management is a support function for the people doing the work, not a status rank above them.
