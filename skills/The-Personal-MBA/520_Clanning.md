# Clanning

## Overview

Humans instinctively organize into small groups of people who are like themselves — clans built around shared interests, identity, or circumstance — because for most of our history belonging to a group was a survival requirement. The pull is so strong that people will form clans around almost anything: a motorcycle brand, a programming language, a diet, a CrossFit box. Membership shapes what people buy, wear, say, and believe, since purchases become ways of declaring "I am one of us."

## Context & Usage

Use clanning when positioning a product or building a community: sell membership in an identity, not just an item. Harley-Davidson's riders, Apple's early "think different" faithful, and niche SaaS communities on Slack all show the pattern — the product is the badge, the clan is the value. Practically: identify which existing clan your probable purchasers belong to, speak in its language, and create gathering places (forums, events, user groups) where members reinforce each other's commitment. The warning label: courting one clan often means being rejected by its rival, so choose deliberately.
