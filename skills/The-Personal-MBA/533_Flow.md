# Flow

## Overview

Every system takes something in and puts something out, and Flow is the rate at which a resource moves across the system's boundary over time — dollars per month, units shipped per day, leads per week, energy consumed per hour. Kaufman pairs it with Stock (534): flows are the movements, stocks are the accumulations they fill or drain. What makes flow the more actionable of the pair is that it is where you intervene — you rarely change a stock directly, you change the inflows and outflows that feed it. Cash flow is the clearest example: a business with healthy accumulated cash can still fail if the outflow rate (payroll, rent, purchases) exceeds the inflow rate (collections) for long enough. Because flows are measured per unit time, they reveal trajectory that a single snapshot hides — two companies with identical cash balances can be in opposite situations depending on whether cash is flowing in faster than out.

## Context & Usage

Think in flows whenever a level is rising or falling and you want to understand or steer it. To grow a subscriber base (a stock), you work the two flows: new signups in and churn out. To fix a cash crunch you accelerate inflows (invoice sooner, take deposits) and slow outflows (stretch payables, cut burn) rather than staring at the balance. Watch for inflow/outflow imbalances that quietly compound — a hiring rate slightly above attrition swells headcount for years. Distinguishing the two flows into and out of any stock is the first analytical step in almost any system diagnosis.
