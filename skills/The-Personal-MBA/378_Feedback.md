# Feedback

## Overview

Feedback is information from the real world about how your offer actually performs, and Kaufman's central caution is to distinguish useful feedback from flattering noise. The feedback that matters comes from real prospects reacting to a concrete thing, not from friends and family reacting to a description — those close to you tend to be kind, which makes their praise nearly worthless for decision-making. He goes further: seek out negative feedback deliberately, because criticism points at what to fix while praise only tells you to keep doing what you already do. The single most valuable signal is whether people will actually pay, which is why real purchases outrank surveys and compliments as feedback.

## Context & Usage

Gather feedback continuously through the iteration cycle, but be ruthless about its quality. When testing a product idea, do not ask "Do you like this?" (which invites politeness); ask people to use it, watch where they struggle, and — best of all — ask them to buy it. A founder whose ten friends all loved the app but whose landing page got zero paid sign-ups has received the only feedback that counts. Treat enthusiastic praise from non-buyers as a warning to dig deeper, and treat a specific complaint from a real prospect as a gift.
