# Attention

## Overview

Marketing cannot begin until you have someone's attention, and attention is strictly finite — each person has only so many waking hours and so much focus to spend, and countless messages compete for it every day. Kaufman treats attention as the scarce raw material of all marketing: no matter how good your offer, if no one is looking at it, nothing else in the marketing process can happen. Getting attention is only the first step, though; the harder task is earning enough of it, from the right people, to communicate value and prompt action. Because attention is scarce, it is also valuable in its own right — which is why some entire businesses exist to capture attention and resell access to it.

## Context & Usage

Treat attention as the first gate whenever you plan how to reach customers. Before debating messaging, ask the blunter question: how will the right people even notice this exists? A brilliant product with no plan for capturing attention will simply go unbought. The scarcity also disciplines your choices — since you'll get only a sliver of anyone's focus, lead with the single most compelling thing, not a comprehensive feature list. Attention captured from the wrong people (those who will never buy) is largely wasted, which is why attention should be pursued alongside knowing your probable purchaser.
