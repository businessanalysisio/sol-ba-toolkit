# States of Being

## Overview

Some of the things people want are not achievements but ongoing qualities of experience — feeling energized, being respected by peers, having financial breathing room, staying connected to customers. Kaufman separates these states of being from goals because they have no finish line: you don't complete "calm," you maintain it. Confusing the two produces a specific pathology — chasing a state as if it were a project, arriving at the milestone, and discovering the feeling didn't come with it.

## Context & Usage

When a desire keeps resurfacing after every "win," check whether it's actually a state. The founder who believes the next revenue milestone will finally bring security hits the milestone and feels no different, because security is a state produced by daily conditions (cash buffer, predictable pipeline, sleep), not by a number crossed once. Translate wanted states into the recurring practices and environmental conditions that generate them, then monitor like a dashboard: am I experiencing this state most days? If not, adjust the conditions — don't set a bigger goal.
