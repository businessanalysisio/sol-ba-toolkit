# Goals

## Overview

A goal, in Kaufman's usage, is a defined target for a specific achievement — and well-formed goals share the PICS qualities: Positive (stated as what you want, not what you're avoiding), Immediate (actionable now, not "someday"), Concrete (you'd know it happened), and Specific (bounded in scope and time). "Get in better shape" fails all four; "walk thirty minutes every morning before work, starting tomorrow" passes. Badly formed goals aren't just unmeasurable — they give the mind nothing to steer toward.

## Context & Usage

Apply the PICS filter to every business target before committing resources. "Improve customer satisfaction" becomes "raise our post-support survey average from 3.9 to 4.4 by end of Q3"; "grow the newsletter" becomes "add 2,000 subscribers by launch, via three named channels." The rewrite exposes hidden decisions (which metric? which deadline? whose job?) that vague goals let everyone defer. Also distinguish true goals from states of being — "feel less stressed about cash" is not a goal but a state to maintain, and treating it as a finish line guarantees frustration.
