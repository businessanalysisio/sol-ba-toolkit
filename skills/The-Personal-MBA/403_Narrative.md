# Narrative

## Overview

A narrative is a story that connects the prospect to your offer, usually by tying it to their identity, values, or aspirations. Kaufman observes that people understand and remember the world through stories far more readily than through facts or feature lists, and that the most powerful narratives are ones the customer wants to be part of. When an offer is wrapped in a compelling story — about who made it, why it exists, or who the customer becomes by using it — it gains meaning beyond its functional value. Buying then becomes a way of expressing and living out that story. This is why brands invest so heavily in narrative: it transforms a transaction into an act of identity, which commands loyalty and premium prices that features alone cannot.

## Context & Usage

Craft a narrative when you want customers to connect emotionally and identify with your brand rather than merely comparing specifications. A coffee brand telling the story of the farmers it partners with, and inviting the buyer to see themselves as someone who cares about that, sells far more than one listing roast levels. Narrative works especially well alongside remarkability and controversy — a distinctive story gives people something to talk about and a side to take. When your offer feels like an interchangeable commodity, a genuine, well-told narrative is often what differentiates it, because customers buy the story they want to belong to.
