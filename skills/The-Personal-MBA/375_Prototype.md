# Prototype

## Overview

A prototype is an early, deliberately incomplete representation of an offer whose purpose is to make an idea tangible enough to react to. It does not need to work, look finished, or contain every feature — a sketch, a clickable mockup, a cardboard model, or a rough sample all qualify, so long as they turn an abstract concept into something people can see, touch, and respond to. Kaufman's point is that opinions about a described idea are nearly worthless, while reactions to a concrete prototype are informative, because people can only tell you what they truly think once there is a specific artifact in front of them. The prototype is the cheapest possible way to convert vague intentions into real feedback before you spend heavily on building.

## Context & Usage

Build a prototype the moment you have an idea worth testing, and long before you feel it is "ready." If you are considering a new app, a paper sketch of the key screen shown to five target users will reveal confusion and desire faster and cheaper than months of coding. The prototype is the first artifact in the iteration cycle — the thing you put in front of people to gather the feedback that drives the next version. Resist the urge to polish; a prototype that looks finished invites praise instead of the honest criticism you actually need.
