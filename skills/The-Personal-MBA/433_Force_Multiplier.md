# Force Multiplier

## Overview

A Force Multiplier is a tool or investment that increases the output you get from the same amount of effort. Borrowing the military term, Kaufman describes anything that lets a fixed input produce a larger result: a backhoe multiplies what one worker can dig, a well-chosen piece of software multiplies what one person can process, a good employee or a template multiplies what a founder can accomplish in a day. The defining feature is leverage over your own labor — you invest once in the multiplier, then reap amplified returns on every unit of effort afterward. Force multipliers usually cost money or time up front, and the smart move is to compare that cost against the ongoing labor they save. A well-selected multiplier pays for itself many times over; a poorly chosen one is just expense and complexity.

## Context & Usage

Look for force multipliers whenever you are effort-constrained and doing repetitive work by hand. Example: a solo operator spending hours each week manually formatting reports buys or builds a template-and-automation tool once, and every future report takes minutes — the tool multiplies their output permanently. Evaluate a potential multiplier by the effort it saves per use times the number of uses, against its cost to acquire and maintain. Beware multipliers that add more overhead than they remove; the goal is amplified output, not more tools to manage.
