# Parkinson's Law

## Overview

Parkinson's Law states that work expands to fill the time allotted for its completion. Give yourself a week for a task that genuinely needs a day, and it will somehow consume the whole week — the extra time gets absorbed by perfectionism, second-guessing, and low-value tinkering rather than better results. Kaufman highlights the practical corollary: because the effort a task takes is elastic and molds itself to the available time, deadlines are not merely administrative but productive tools. A tight, genuine constraint concentrates attention on what actually matters and strips away the padding, often producing work that is just as good as, or better than, what a luxurious schedule would have yielded, in a fraction of the time.

## Context & Usage

Apply this by deliberately setting deadlines shorter than feel comfortable, then working to hit them. If a report has ballooned across two weeks, block a single focused afternoon and commit to finishing within it — the artificial scarcity forces prioritization. It is the engine behind timeboxing and the reason short sprints outperform open-ended ones: a two-hour hard stop on writing a proposal usually gets it done, where "sometime this month" never does. Use it in reverse as a diagnostic, too — if a task is somehow taking far longer than its value warrants, suspect that a slack deadline (or no deadline) is letting the work expand. Pair with the Next Action (491) so the constrained time is spent doing rather than deliberating.
