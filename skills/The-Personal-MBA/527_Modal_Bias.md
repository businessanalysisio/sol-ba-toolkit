# Modal Bias

## Overview

The automatic assumption that your own idea, method, or preference is the best one — that *your* mode is the right mode. Everyone runs this bias by default: the engineer knows the rewrite is the answer, the marketer knows it's a positioning problem, the founder knows customers will love the feature. Kaufman's point is that the feeling of being right is not evidence; it is simply what having an idea feels like from the inside. Modal bias is why debates about untested opinions escalate — both parties are certain, and neither certainty means anything.

## Context & Usage

Apply whenever a decision is being settled by conviction rather than data. The cure is to convert opinions into testable propositions: instead of arguing which landing page is better, run both; instead of decreeing the process change, pilot it in one team. In groups, modal bias compounds with authority — the senior person's mode wins by default — so explicitly invite competing options before anyone anchors ("let's get three approaches on the table before evaluating"). Self-check: when you cannot state what evidence would change your mind, you are inside modal bias, not analysis.
