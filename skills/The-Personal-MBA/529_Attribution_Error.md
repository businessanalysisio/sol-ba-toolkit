# Attribution Error

## Overview

The fundamental attribution error: when *others* fail, we blame their character ("he's careless, she's incompetent"); when *we* fail, we blame circumstances ("I was overloaded, the brief was unclear"). We reverse the pattern for successes — ours reflect ability, theirs reflect luck. The asymmetry exists because we experience our own situational pressures directly but only observe other people's behavior, so their context is invisible to us. The result is systematically unfair judgments that poison working relationships and hide fixable process problems behind personality verdicts.

## Context & Usage

Apply whenever you catch yourself explaining someone's behavior with an adjective. The corrective question: "what circumstances would make a reasonable person do this?" A vendor who missed a deadline may be sequencing a supply shortage; a teammate who was curt may be handling an escalation. This matters operationally, not just morally: if a warehouse keeps mis-picking and you attribute it to lazy staff, you will hire and fire in a loop; if you look at circumstances, you find the confusing bin labels. Kaufman's rule of thumb — extend others the situational benefit of the doubt you automatically extend yourself.
