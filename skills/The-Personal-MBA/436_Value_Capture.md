# Value Capture

## Overview

Value Capture is the practice of keeping a portion of the value you create as profit. Kaufman's central insight is that creating value and capturing it are different acts — plenty of businesses create enormous value for customers while capturing almost none of it for themselves, and go broke doing it. The healthy zone sits between two limits: you must charge more than it costs you to deliver (or you lose money), but less than the total value the customer receives (or they won't buy). Whatever sits inside that band is what you can capture. The discipline is to capture enough to sustain and grow the business without capturing so aggressively that you strangle the value the customer perceives. A giveaway captures nothing; gouging captures a lot once and then loses the customer.

## Context & Usage

Apply value-capture thinking when your product is clearly valued but the business isn't making money, or when setting prices for a new offer. Example: an open-source project used by millions creates immense value but captures none until it adds a paid hosted tier — capturing a slice of the value from customers who'll gladly pay to avoid running it themselves. Look for value you already create but leave on the table (a free feature others would pay for, a service you bundle in for nothing). The aim is a sustainable share, not the maximum possible extraction, since over-capturing invites competitors and resentment.
