# Incremental Augmentation

## Overview

Incremental augmentation is the practice of improving an offer step by step after it's launched, guided by real customer feedback, rather than trying to build the complete, perfect version up front. You start with a Minimum Economically Viable Offer, put it in front of paying customers, and then add one improvement at a time — each one informed by what actual buyers are asking for and struggling with. Kaufman favors this because it keeps effort tightly coupled to demonstrated demand: you invest in the next feature only after the market shows it's wanted, which prevents the enormous waste of building elaborate capabilities no one uses. It's the natural sequel to shipping a MEVO and a direct application of the iteration cycle to a live product.

## Context & Usage

Use incremental augmentation once you have a working, selling offer and are deciding what to build next. Instead of guessing at a big roadmap, let customer behavior and requests dictate the order of improvements, adding features individually and watching how each lands. A software product that launches with core functionality and then adds the most-requested capability each cycle grows in exactly the directions its users value, while a competitor who spent two years building "everything" often ships features nobody wanted. Resist the temptation to batch many changes at once — small, feedback-driven steps preserve the ability to learn from each.
