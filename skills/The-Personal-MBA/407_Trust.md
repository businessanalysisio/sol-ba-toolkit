# Trust

## Overview

Trust, in Kaufman's account, is the belief that a promise will be kept — and it is the precondition for almost every transaction. Nobody buys from, invests in, or partners with someone they expect to be cheated by, so the level of trust between parties directly governs how likely a deal is to close and how large it can be. Trust accumulates slowly through kept promises and reciprocation, and it collapses fast: a single violation can destroy years of goodwill, because people weight betrayals far more heavily than favors. Reputation is trust made portable — the aggregate of what everyone who has dealt with you tells others — which is why guarding it is a commercial asset, not just an ethical nicety.

## Context & Usage

Apply this whenever you are asking someone to take a risk on you: a first-time customer, a lender, a new hire, a supplier extending credit. Because strangers start with little trust, the practical move is to substitute proof for faith — guarantees, free trials, testimonials, third-party reviews, and small reversible first steps all let a wary party experience a kept promise before committing fully. A software vendor offering a 30-day money-back guarantee is manufacturing trust by absorbing the buyer's downside. Conversely, protect the reputation you have: over-deliver on commitments, and never trade a long-term relationship for a one-time gain, since the cost of a broken promise is paid across every future transaction it poisons.
