# Reactivation

## Overview

Reactivation is the practice of winning back former customers who once bought from you but have gone quiet. Kaufman highlights it because these people are the cheapest sales you will ever make: they already know you, have paid you before, and require none of the trust-building and attention-getting that new-customer acquisition demands. A lapsed customer costs far less to reach than a stranger, so a simple, personal "we've missed you — here's what's new / here's an offer" campaign against a dormant list often produces the highest return of any marketing you can run. Reactivation is the flip side of lifetime value: instead of maximizing a single relationship's length, you restart relationships that stalled, recovering value you had already written off. The main requirement is that you kept the contact information and have a reason to reach back out.

## Context & Usage

Run reactivation on a schedule against customers who haven't purchased within a defined window. Example: a supplement brand exports everyone who hasn't ordered in six months and sends a two-touch sequence — a friendly check-in, then a limited discount — reviving a meaningful slice of the list for a fraction of the cost of acquiring the same number of new buyers. Segment by why they left where you can (price, a bad experience, simply forgot) and tailor the message. Because it is so cheap relative to acquisition, reactivation is usually the first lever to pull when revenue softens, before spending more on new-customer marketing.
