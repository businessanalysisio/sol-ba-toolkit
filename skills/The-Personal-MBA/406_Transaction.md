# Transaction

## Overview

A transaction is an exchange of value between two or more parties, and Kaufman treats it as the atomic unit of every business — the moment where Value Creation, Marketing, and Value Delivery finally pay off. For a transaction to happen, four things must be present at once: two parties, something of value each holds, a mutual desire to trade (each side must want what the other has more than what they are giving up), and a practical means to complete the exchange. When a customer hands over money for a product, both sides walk away believing they are better off than before — otherwise the trade would not close. Everything upstream in a business exists to make transactions possible; a company that creates value nobody will pay for has no business, only a hobby.

## Context & Usage

Reach for this lens when a promising offer is not converting, because a stalled sale almost always means one of the four requirements is missing. A prospect who "loves it but won't buy" may lack a means to pay (no financing, wrong currency, purchase-approval friction), or may not actually value the offer above its price. Diagnose by walking the four conditions in order: is there a genuine second party with authority, is real value on the table, does the customer want it more than their money, and can they actually complete the purchase right now. Removing whichever condition is absent — say, adding a payment plan so the "means to exchange" exists — is usually cheaper than reworking the product.
