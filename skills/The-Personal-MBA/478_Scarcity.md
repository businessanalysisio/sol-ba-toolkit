# Scarcity

## Overview

Things appear more valuable when they are limited, and the prospect of losing access converts "someday" interest into "now" action. The pull is loss aversion applied to opportunity: a closing window threatens a loss, and the mind moves to prevent it. This is why "only 3 left," enrollment deadlines, limited editions, and waitlists reliably outperform open-ended availability — without a reason to act today, most prospects defer forever.

## Context & Usage

Build honest scarcity into offers that suffer from procrastination. A course that opens twice a year, a consultancy that takes five clients per quarter, a seasonal product run — each gives genuinely interested buyers a legitimate reason to decide. The discipline is truthfulness: manufactured countdown timers that reset and "limited" stock that never runs out train customers to ignore you and corrode trust, which costs far more than the urgency ever earned. Scarcity should describe a real constraint, ideally one that also protects your quality or capacity.
