# Change (systemic)

## Overview

Systems are never static — they change continuously whether or not anyone is steering them — so Kaufman treats permanence as the illusion and change as the baseline. A configuration that works today is not guaranteed to work tomorrow, because the environment shifts, the parts wear or adapt, competitors move, and the loops inside the system keep evolving its state. This has a blunt strategic consequence: no competitive advantage, process, or product is permanent, and a business that assumes its current success will simply continue is quietly accumulating risk. Kodak's dominance in film meant nothing once the environment changed to digital. The point is not that change is bad but that it is constant and inevitable, so the relevant question is never "will things change?" but "how fast, in what direction, and are we adapting or standing still?"

## Context & Usage

Apply this whenever a plan or business model implicitly assumes the world will hold still. Build in periodic reexamination of assumptions that were true when a strategy was set, and treat any "we've always done it this way" as a flag to check whether the conditions that justified the practice still hold. Because you cannot stop change, the useful posture is to expect it and keep the capacity to adapt — retained flexibility, ongoing learning, willingness to cannibalize your own success before someone else does. It underlies why systems need continual Improvement (562, 563) rather than one-time fixes.
