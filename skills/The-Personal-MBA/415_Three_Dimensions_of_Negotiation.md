# Three Dimensions of Negotiation

## Overview

Drawing on Lax and Sebenius's 3-D Negotiation, Kaufman splits any negotiation into three distinct dimensions, and most people mistakenly work in only one. **Tactics** is the behavior at the table — how you frame offers, concede, and read the other side. **Deal design** is the structure of the agreement itself — how the terms are packaged so value is created for both parties (payment schedules, guarantees, bundled scope). **Setup** happens away from the table: making sure the right parties are involved, the right issues are on the agenda, and the sequence and alternatives are arranged in your favor before anyone speaks. The insight is that setup and deal design usually move the outcome far more than clever tactics, yet they get the least attention because they are invisible during the actual conversation. A negotiation is often won or lost by who is in the room and what is being negotiated, decided long before the haggling starts.

## Context & Usage

Reach for this model before an important negotiation to diagnose where your leverage actually lives. Example: a freelancer struggling to raise rates with a procurement contact (tactics) may win instantly by changing the setup — getting the department head who feels the pain into the conversation instead. Work the dimensions in order: fix the setup first, then design a deal that gives the other side something cheap-to-you but valuable-to-them, and only then worry about table behavior. If you find yourself relying purely on tactics, you have probably neglected the two dimensions that matter more.
