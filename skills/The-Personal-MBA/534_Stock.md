# Stock

## Overview

A Stock is the amount of a resource a system holds at a given moment — the reservoir that inflows fill and outflows drain. Cash in the bank, inventory on the shelf, active subscribers, unread tickets in a support queue, goodwill with a customer: each is a stock, a quantity you could in principle count right now. Kaufman's key point, developed alongside Flow (533), is that stocks change only through their flows and always with a lag, which is why they behave less responsively than intuition expects. A warehouse doesn't empty the instant demand spikes; a reputation doesn't recover the instant service improves. Stocks also act as buffers — the accumulated cash reserve is what lets a business survive a month when outflow outruns inflow — so the size of a stock is a direct measure of a system's resilience to shocks.

## Context & Usage

Identify the stocks whenever you model a system, because they are what you ultimately care about (cash on hand, customers retained) even though you can only act on the flows that move them. Use the buffer property deliberately: hold enough inventory, cash, or staffing headroom to absorb normal variability, but recognize that every stock has a carrying cost, so more is not always better. Watch for dangerous depletion trajectories early — because stocks drain with a lag, a runway that looks comfortable today can be committed to running out, and by the time the balance looks alarming the corrective flows may act too slowly to help.
