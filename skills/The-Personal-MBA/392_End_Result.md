# End Result

## Overview

People do not buy products or features; they buy the outcome those things produce in their lives. The classic formulation Kaufman echoes is that no one wants a quarter-inch drill — they want a quarter-inch hole, and really the shelf the hole lets them hang. The End Result is that final desired state the customer is actually paying to reach, and effective marketing communicates it directly rather than reciting specifications. Customers translate features into benefits only reluctantly and imperfectly, so the burden is on you to state the end result plainly. When you lead with the outcome — what their life looks like once the problem is solved — you speak to what they truly care about instead of leaving them to do the translation.

## Context & Usage

Frame every marketing message around the end result whenever you catch yourself listing features. Convert each feature into the concrete outcome it delivers: "256-bit encryption" becomes "your customers' data stays private, so you never face a breach headline." A mattress company sells not coil counts but waking up rested and pain-free. When a pitch feels flat, it is usually stuck describing what the product is instead of what the customer gets; rewriting it around the end result almost always sharpens it. This links to the core human drives the outcome ultimately satisfies.
