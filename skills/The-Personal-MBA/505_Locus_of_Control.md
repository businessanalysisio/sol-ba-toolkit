# Locus of Control

## Overview

Locus of Control is the psychological dimension of where you believe the causes of what happens to you reside. Someone with an internal locus attributes outcomes largely to their own choices and effort; someone with an external locus attributes them to luck, circumstances, other people, or fate. Kaufman highlights that this belief is largely self-fulfilling: people who feel they can influence their results take action, experiment, and persist, which tends to produce better outcomes, while those who feel at the mercy of external forces are more passive and give up sooner. The point is not to deny that luck and circumstance are real — they are — but to focus attention and energy on the substantial portion of any situation that genuinely is within your control.

## Context & Usage

Use this as a self-diagnostic when you feel stuck or victimized by a situation. Catch external-locus framing ("the market is impossible," "my manager won't let me," "customers just don't get it") and deliberately ask the internal-locus question: given these constraints, what is actually within my control here, and what is the next action I can take on it? A founder blaming a flat month on "the economy" cedes all agency; the same founder asking "what can I test this week to improve conversion?" reclaims it. The healthiest stance is a realistic internal locus — owning your response and your controllable inputs without pretending you command outcomes you don't. It pairs with Attachment (506), since releasing attachment to outcomes frees you to focus on the inputs you actually govern.
