# Incentive-Caused Bias

## Overview

Charlie Munger's observation, adopted by Kaufman: people's perceptions and judgments bend — honestly and unconsciously — toward whatever their incentives reward. The commissioned broker genuinely believes the product suits you; the surgeon genuinely believes surgery is indicated; the consultant genuinely believes the engagement should be extended. "Whose bread I eat, his song I sing." The bias is dangerous precisely because it does not feel like dishonesty from the inside: incentives shape what people notice, how they weigh evidence, and what conclusions feel obviously right.

## Context & Usage

Two applications. Receiving advice: before weighing any recommendation, ask what the adviser gains if you follow it — and discount accordingly, or seek advice from someone paid regardless of your choice (fee-only advisers, independent auditors). Designing incentives: assume people will do exactly what you pay them to do, including the perverse versions — pay support reps per ticket closed and tickets get closed unresolved; pay salespeople on bookings alone and unprofitable deals flood in. Wells Fargo's fake-accounts scandal is the canonical modern case of quota incentives manufacturing the measured behavior. Audit every metric-linked reward for the behavior it *actually* purchases.
