# Sufficiency

## Overview

Sufficiency is knowing how much is enough — the amount of revenue, profit, or growth actually required to meet your goals, beyond which more is not automatically better. Kaufman raises it as a deliberate counterweight to the reflex that a business should always maximize everything. Defining sufficiency means naming the concrete target: the profit needed to pay yourself well, cover the business, and fund the life you want. Once you know that number, you can make cleaner decisions — you can decline growth that would cost you your time, autonomy, or sanity for money you don't need, and you can recognize when the business has already succeeded on its own terms. Without a sufficiency figure, "more" becomes an infinite treadmill where no amount of success ever feels like arrival.

## Context & Usage

Define sufficiency early, and revisit it when facing a growth-versus-quality-of-life trade-off. Example: a freelancer calculates that $8,000 a month covers their needs and goals comfortably, so instead of chasing every client they cap their workload at that level and reclaim their time — a rational choice only visible once "enough" is named. Use the number to filter opportunities: growth that moves you toward the target is worth pursuing; growth beyond it should be justified by something you actually want, not pursued by default. Sufficiency turns "how do I get more?" into the sharper question "what am I actually trying to reach?"
