# Alternatives

## Overview

Your offer never competes in a vacuum — every customer weighs it against alternatives, and the most important alternative is almost always doing nothing at all. Kaufman stresses that prospects are not choosing between your product and a direct competitor's; they are choosing among all the ways they could spend that money and attention, including keeping both. This means the real bar you must clear is not "better than the rival" but "worth changing from the current situation," which is a higher and often overlooked bar because inertia is powerful and free. Understanding the full set of alternatives a customer considers tells you what you are truly up against and what objections you must overcome.

## Context & Usage

Map the alternatives explicitly whenever sales are slow despite a good product. Often the culprit is not a competitor but the status quo — customers who would rather tolerate a familiar annoyance than take on the effort and risk of switching. A project-management tool competes less with rival tools than with the spreadsheet-and-email habit teams already use for free. Once you name the real alternatives, you can position against them directly: reduce switching cost, dramatize the cost of doing nothing, and show why the change is worth the disruption.
