# Trade-offs

## Overview

No offer can be best on every dimension at once — cheapest, fastest, highest quality, most flexible, and most reliable all together is not a real option. Kaufman frames trade-offs as the deliberate choice of which dimensions to maximize and which to sacrifice, and insists this choice be made consciously rather than by default. A business that tries to be everything to everyone ends up mediocre on all fronts, while one that openly optimizes for a few things — say, low price and speed at the expense of customization — becomes clearly the right choice for the customers who share those priorities. The strategic act is deciding what you will be bad at on purpose, because that is what lets you be genuinely excellent at what matters most to your market.

## Context & Usage

Confront trade-offs when defining positioning or resolving internal debates where every stakeholder wants to improve a different attribute. A budget airline that chooses low fares accepts no assigned seats and paid extras — and wins the price-sensitive traveler precisely because it refuses to chase the comfort-seeker. When a team is stuck trying to make a product both premium and cheap, naming the trade-off breaks the deadlock: pick the dimensions your target customer cares about, and let the others go. Match the trade-offs you choose to the economic values (381) your market weighs most heavily.
