# Call-to-Action (CTA)

## Overview

Every marketing message should ask the prospect to take one specific, clear next step — that request is the call-to-action. Kaufman stresses that marketing without an explicit CTA squanders the attention it captured: people who are interested but not told exactly what to do usually do nothing. A good CTA is unambiguous and singular ("Buy now," "Enter your email for the free guide," "Call to schedule"), because a confused or overloaded prospect defaults to inaction. Kaufman notes CTAs generally serve one of two goals — get the prospect to buy immediately, or get them to grant permission so you can continue marketing. A crucial side benefit is measurability: because a CTA prompts a trackable action, it lets you measure exactly how well a message performs.

## Context & Usage

Include a clear CTA in every marketing piece, and make it the one action you most want taken. A blog post that delivers value but never invites the reader to subscribe wastes the goodwill it built; adding a single, prominent "get updates" prompt captures it. Avoid offering multiple competing actions — one strong CTA outperforms three weak ones. Because the response to a CTA is measurable, use it to run iteration cycles on your marketing: change the wording, placement, or offer and watch the conversion rate move. Match the CTA to the prospect's readiness — ask for permission when they're not yet ready to buy, and for the sale when they are.
