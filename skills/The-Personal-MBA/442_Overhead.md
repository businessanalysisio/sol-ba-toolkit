# Overhead

## Overview

Overhead is the ongoing cost of simply keeping the business running, regardless of how much you sell — rent, salaries, software subscriptions, insurance, utilities. Kaufman flags it as the baseline you must cover every single month before you earn a cent of profit, which makes it the quiet determinant of how much pressure a business is under. High overhead sets a high bar: you have to sell a lot just to stand still, and a slow month becomes a crisis. Low overhead is a form of resilience and freedom — a lean business survives downturns, can experiment cheaply, and reaches profitability faster, because there is less it must cover before the money it makes is genuinely its own. Overhead tends to creep upward invisibly as small recurring commitments accumulate, so it rewards periodic pruning.

## Context & Usage

Watch overhead when starting out (keep it minimal to shorten the runway to profit) and review it periodically as it creeps. Example: two founders with identical revenue fare very differently — the one paying for a large office and a big team must sell hard just to break even, while the lean one paying almost nothing keeps most of what it earns and sleeps easily through a slow quarter. Audit recurring expenses regularly and ask which actually contribute to value delivery versus which are habit or comfort. Lowering overhead is one of the fastest routes to profitability because every dollar cut drops straight to the bottom line.
