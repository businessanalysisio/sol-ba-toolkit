# Uncertainty

## Overview

The future is fundamentally unknowable, and Uncertainty is Kaufman's insistence that you build that fact into your thinking rather than papering over it with false precision. He separates it from risk in the narrow sense: risk is a known distribution of outcomes you can price, while uncertainty is the deeper condition where you don't even know the full range of what might happen. Elaborate five-year forecasts and single-number projections give the comforting illusion of control, but they are guesses dressed as facts — the more detailed and confident a long-range plan, the more suspect it should be. The honest response is not paralysis; it is to expect surprise as the normal case and to prefer decisions and structures that stay robust across many possible futures instead of ones that only pay off if a specific prediction comes true.

## Context & Usage

Invoke this whenever a decision leans on a confident forecast of what will happen. Replace point predictions with ranges and scenarios, favor reversible bets and small experiments over irreversible all-in commitments, and keep Slack (535) and reserves precisely because you cannot see what is coming. Treat plans as living hypotheses to be revised as reality reports back, not as contracts with the future. It pairs naturally with Change (542): uncertainty is why you keep some flexibility in hand rather than optimizing every resource for a single expected outcome.
