# Threat Lockdown

## Overview

When the mind perceives a threat, it locks attention onto that threat and suppresses deliberate, creative thinking until the danger is resolved or reinterpreted as harmless. The mechanism is ancient — the midbrain hijacking the system to deal with the predator — but it fires just as hard on modern non-lethal threats: a rumor of layoffs, an angry client email, a public criticism. While lockdown persists, people cannot plan, absorb nuance, or hear solutions; they can only fixate.

## Context & Usage

Defuse the threat before attempting reason. An enraged customer must feel acknowledged ("you're right, this shouldn't have happened, here's what I'm doing about it") before any explanation can land. A team that has heard restructuring rumors will produce nothing until leadership addresses the fear explicitly — silence keeps every mind in lockdown. Personally, notice when you are looping on a perceived threat: write down the worst case and your response to it, which reinterprets the ambiguous danger into a bounded, handled problem.
