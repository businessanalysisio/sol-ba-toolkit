# Loss Aversion

## Overview

People feel losses roughly twice as intensely as equivalent gains — losing $100 hurts about twice as much as finding $100 pleases (Kahneman and Tversky's foundational finding). The asymmetry explains a whole family of business behavior: customers who won't switch from a mediocre vendor, employees who fight reorganizations that would benefit them, investors who hold losing positions, and buyers frozen at checkout by the fear of a bad purchase.

## Context & Usage

Apply it on both sides of the transaction. Selling: neutralize the prospect's feared loss before amplifying the promised gain — money-back guarantees, free trials, and pilots work precisely because they transfer the loss risk to the seller. Analyzing: when stakeholders resist a clearly beneficial change, look for what they perceive they might lose (status, competence, control), because that hidden loss outweighs the advertised gain in their calculus. Address the loss directly rather than repeating the benefits louder.
