# Permission

## Overview

Permission, a concept Kaufman draws from Seth Godin, is a prospect's explicit agreement to receive ongoing messages from you — the classic example being an email subscription. Its power is that it converts a one-time flash of attention into a durable asset: instead of having to recapture strangers' attention repeatedly at rising cost, you can communicate with a receptive, opted-in audience again and again over time. Permission is granted willingly, usually in exchange for something valuable (useful content, a free resource), and it is precious precisely because it is voluntary — people who chose to hear from you are far more receptive than those you interrupt. A permission asset compounds in value as it grows, becoming one of the most reliable engines a business can own.

## Context & Usage

Build a permission asset whenever you'd otherwise be paying repeatedly to reach the same people. Rather than relying solely on ads that must be re-bought for every campaign, offer something worth opting in for, then market to that list over time — a newsletter that captures interested readers lets you launch future products to a warm audience at near-zero marginal cost. Guard permission carefully: sending irrelevant or excessive messages erodes it, and once people unsubscribe or tune out, the asset shrinks. This is the natural complement to "free," which is often what earns the permission in the first place.
