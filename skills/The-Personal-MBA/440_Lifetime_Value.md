# Lifetime Value

## Overview

Lifetime Value (LTV) is the total profit a customer generates over the entire span of their relationship with you — not just the first purchase but every repeat purchase, upsell, and referral, minus the cost of serving them. Kaufman stresses that thinking in single transactions badly undervalues customers: a coffee drinker who spends $4 a day is not a $4 customer but potentially a several-thousand-dollar one over the years they keep coming back. LTV reframes acquisition and retention decisions, because it tells you what a customer is actually worth over time rather than today. It rises when you increase how much customers spend per visit, how often they buy, and how long they stay — so retention, reactivation, and repeat-purchase design all feed directly into it. LTV is also the number that sets the ceiling on what you can afford to spend to acquire a customer.

## Context & Usage

Estimate LTV before setting marketing budgets or judging whether a customer relationship is worth investing in. Example: a subscription box calculates that the average subscriber stays 14 months at a $12 monthly profit — a ~$168 LTV — which then justifies spending far more to acquire each subscriber than the first month's payment alone would suggest. Use LTV to prioritize retention (extending the relationship compounds directly into the figure) and to compare customer segments, since high-LTV segments deserve more acquisition spend and attention. Pair it with allowable acquisition cost, which uses LTV to cap what you can profitably pay per new customer.
