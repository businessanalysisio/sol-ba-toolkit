# Safety

## Overview

Kaufman's foundation for all productive collaboration: people can only do their best work when they do not feel threatened. Because the brain's threat-detection circuitry (the "onion brain") reacts to social dangers — loss of status, public criticism, arbitrary punishment, job insecurity — the same way it reacts to physical danger, a person who feels unsafe drops into Threat Lockdown and loses access to creative, deliberate thinking. Establishing safety means making it clear, through consistent behavior, that you will not use your power to harm the other person: no humiliation, no surprise reprisals, no punishing honest mistakes or bad news.

## Context & Usage

Apply whenever you hold power over others — as a manager, client, or senior stakeholder — and need candor or creativity from them. If your team stops surfacing problems, sandbagging estimates, or admitting errors, the root cause is usually a safety failure: someone was punished for honesty. A practical example: a project lead who thanks the engineer who reports a slipped milestone early (and helps re-plan) gets early warnings forever after; one who explodes gets discovered-late disasters instead. Safety is also why blameless post-mortems outperform fault-finding reviews.
