# Opportunity Cost

## Overview

Opportunity Cost is the value of the next-best option you give up whenever you commit a resource to one choice. Kaufman frames it as the hidden price tag on every decision: because time, money, and attention are finite, saying yes to one thing is automatically saying no to everything else you could have done with those resources. The true cost of a project isn't just its cash and hours — it's the best alternative use of those same cash and hours that you forgo. This is why a "profitable" project can still be a bad decision: if it earns 5% while an available alternative would have earned 20%, the opportunity cost of choosing it is that lost 15%. Making opportunity cost explicit turns vague busyness into deliberate prioritization, because it forces you to compare each option against what else you could be doing rather than judging it in isolation.

## Context & Usage

Weigh opportunity cost when allocating scarce time or capital, especially when several options all look worthwhile. Example: a founder deciding whether to spend a month building a feature should compare it not to doing nothing but to the highest-value alternative that month — a marketing push that might earn far more, making the feature's real cost the growth it displaced. Ask of every commitment, "what is the best thing I'm giving up to do this?" and prefer options whose return clearly beats their alternatives. It is the antidote to filling time with activities that are good in isolation but poor compared to what else you could pursue.
