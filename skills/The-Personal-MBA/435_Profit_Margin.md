# Profit Margin

## Overview

Profit Margin is the difference between what you charge for something and what it costs you to provide, expressed as a percentage of the price: (revenue − cost) ÷ revenue. Kaufman treats it as one of the most revealing numbers in a business because it measures how much breathing room every sale gives you. Sell a product for $100 that costs $40 to deliver and you have a 60% margin; sell it for $100 that costs $95 and your 5% margin leaves almost nothing for overhead, marketing, or error. High margins forgive mistakes, fund growth, and let you weather downturns; thin margins make a business fragile, since a small rise in costs or a small discount can wipe out the profit entirely. The number matters more than raw revenue — a smaller high-margin business often keeps more money than a larger one grinding on thin margins.

## Context & Usage

Check profit margin before celebrating revenue growth and before cutting prices. Example: a shop owner proud of doubling sales discovers the extra volume came from discounting that halved the margin, so more work produced barely more profit. Use margin to compare products (drop or reprice the thin-margin lines), to sanity-check discounts (a 20% discount on a 30% margin gives away two-thirds of the profit), and to judge whether a business is worth scaling at all. When margins are thin, the highest-leverage moves are usually raising price or cutting cost per unit, not selling more.
