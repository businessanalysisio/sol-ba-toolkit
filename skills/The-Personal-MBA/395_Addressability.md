# Addressability

## Overview

Addressability is how easily you can actually reach your probable purchasers with a message. A market can be full of eager buyers, but if there's no practical channel to get your message in front of them, it's very hard to market to profitably. Kaufman treats addressability as a make-or-break factor often overlooked in the excitement over a promising audience: a highly addressable market has clear, affordable ways to reach it — a mailing list, a search term people use, a publication they read, a community they gather in. A poorly addressable market may want your offer badly yet be scattered, hidden, or reachable only at ruinous cost, which quietly kills otherwise attractive opportunities.

## Context & Usage

Assess addressability early, while evaluating whether a market is worth entering. Ask concretely: where do these people already congregate, and how much will it cost to get a message to them? A product for left-handed classical guitarists may face a real, motivated audience that is nonetheless expensive to reach because they're rare and dispersed. Conversely, a niche with a dedicated forum, newsletter, or well-defined search behavior is cheaply addressable and often a better bet even if smaller. Weak addressability shows up in the "cost of customer acquisition" factor when scoring a market, and should temper enthusiasm for a large but unreachable audience.
