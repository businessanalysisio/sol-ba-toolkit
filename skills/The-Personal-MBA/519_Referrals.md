# Referrals

## Overview

People strongly prefer to buy from, hire, and work with those recommended by someone they already trust — a referral transfers accumulated trust from the referrer to you, collapsing the long, expensive process of establishing credibility from scratch. Kaufman's point is that referrals are not luck; they are a system input you can influence: deliver notably good work, make it easy for satisfied customers to tell others, and ask directly at the moment of highest satisfaction.

## Context & Usage

Treat referrals as a designed acquisition channel, not a byproduct. Concretely: after a successful delivery, ask "who else do you know facing this problem?"; give customers a specific, low-friction way to introduce you (a forwardable one-paragraph description beats "tell your friends"); and reward referrers with appreciation or reciprocal value. For a freelancer or agency, referral-sourced clients typically close faster, negotiate less, and churn less than cold leads — which is why tracking "percentage of new business from referrals" is a leading indicator of service quality. Neglecting the ask is the most common failure: satisfied customers rarely refer spontaneously.
