# Communication Overhead

## Overview

Communication Overhead is the time and effort a team loses to coordinating with itself rather than doing the actual work — and Kaufman's key observation is that it grows disproportionately fast as a team gets bigger. The number of possible communication channels between people rises roughly as n(n-1)/2, so a team of 5 has 10 pairwise links while a team of 10 has 45; doubling the headcount more than quadruples the coordination burden. Past a point, adding people slows a project down instead of speeding it up, because each newcomer multiplies the meetings, updates, and clarifications everyone else must absorb. This is why small teams often ship faster than large ones, and why throwing more bodies at a late project (Brooks's Law) tends to make it later.

## Context & Usage

Use this whenever you are sizing a team or wondering why a growing group feels increasingly bogged down. Keep working teams as small as the task allows, and when scale is unavoidable, structure it to cut channels: break a large group into small autonomous units with clear ownership so most communication stays local rather than everyone talking to everyone. Reduce overhead per link with good asynchronous documentation, clear interfaces between teams, and Commander's Intent so people can act without constant checking-in. A five-person team that suddenly grows to fifteen without restructuring will spend a startling share of its day in status meetings; the model tells you that is arithmetic, not bad attitude. It complements Comparative Advantage (510) — clean division of labor is also clean division of communication.
