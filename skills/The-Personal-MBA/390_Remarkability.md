# Remarkability

## Overview

Something is remarkable when it is literally worth making a remark about — interesting or unexpected enough that people spontaneously tell others. Borrowing Seth Godin's Purple Cow idea, Kaufman argues that remarkability is the engine of free, word-of-mouth marketing: ordinary offers get ignored because there's nothing to say about them, while remarkable ones spread on their own because customers do the spreading. The implication is that remarkability should be built into the offer itself, not bolted on as advertising afterward — a truly novel product markets itself, whereas an unremarkable one requires expensive, constant promotion just to be noticed. Being remarkable usually means being different in a way people care about, not merely better on a dimension no one talks about.

## Context & Usage

Pursue remarkability when you want growth that doesn't depend on ever-increasing ad spend. Before launch, ask: what about this would make a customer stop and tell a friend, unprompted? A restaurant with a genuinely unusual signature dish generates conversation and referrals that a merely competent one never will. This connects to trade-offs — being remarkable often means taking a distinctive stance that some people dislike, since an offer inoffensive to everyone is usually memorable to no one. Design the remarkable element in deliberately rather than hoping it emerges.
