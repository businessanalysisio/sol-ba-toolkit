# Confirmation Bias

## Overview

Confirmation Bias is the tendency to seek out, notice, and give extra weight to evidence that supports what we already believe, while overlooking, discounting, or explaining away evidence that contradicts it. Kaufman treats it as one of the most pervasive threats to clear thinking, because it operates automatically and invisibly: once you hold a belief, your attention filters the world in its favor, so the belief feels increasingly well-founded even when reality is telling a different story. A manager who has decided a new hire is a star will interpret ambiguous behavior charitably and forget the misses; one who has written them off does the reverse with the same underlying facts. The bias is not stupidity — it is the mind's efficiency mechanism misfiring, defending existing conclusions instead of updating them.

## Context & Usage

Guard against it hardest when the stakes are high and you already have a strong opinion — a launch decision, a strategic bet, an assessment of why a project failed. The practical defenses are deliberate: actively look for the strongest case against your position, ask "what evidence would change my mind?" before gathering data, and assign someone to argue the opposite. When reviewing metrics, resist cherry-picking the numbers that flatter your thesis; pre-commit to which metric decides the question. A founder testing a pricing hypothesis should watch especially for the temptation to dismiss the customers who said no as "not the target market." Pair it with Counterfactual Simulation (494) and real testing (503), since imagined and cherry-picked evidence both feed the bias rather than checking it.
