# Cash Flow Cycle

## Overview

The Cash Flow Cycle is the timing of money moving in and out of a business — when cash actually arrives versus when it must go out. Kaufman's crucial point is that timing, not just totals, determines survival: a profitable business can go bankrupt if the cash it's owed arrives after the bills it owes are due. The cycle traces the gap between paying for inputs (buying inventory, paying staff, covering rent) and collecting from customers. When you pay suppliers immediately but customers pay you 60 days later, you must fund that gap out of your own reserves, and a growing business can find that the faster it grows, the more cash the gap consumes. Managing the cycle means shortening the time to get paid, sensibly lengthening the time to pay out, and holding enough reserve to cover the gap. Profit is an accounting result; cash flow is what keeps the doors open.

## Context & Usage

Map your cash flow cycle whenever payment timing is mismatched or when growth is straining reserves. Example: a wholesaler pays for goods on delivery but invoices retailers net-60, so every new order deepens a two-month cash hole — solved by requiring deposits, invoicing faster, and negotiating longer supplier terms to close the gap. Monitor when cash is actually available (not just monthly profit) and stress-test the cycle against a slow-paying month. Because a business dies from running out of cash rather than from a bad P&L, keeping the cycle healthy often matters more day to day than maximizing profit.
