# Critically Important Assumptions (CIAs)

## Overview

Every business plan rests on a handful of beliefs that must be true for the venture to work — Kaufman calls these Critically Important Assumptions. A CIA is not just any assumption but one whose failure would sink the whole enterprise: that customers will pay a certain price, that you can acquire them for less than they're worth, that the product can be delivered at the assumed cost. The discipline is to surface these load-bearing beliefs explicitly, separate them from the many minor assumptions, and test the riskiest ones first — before committing large resources. Most business plans quietly bury their CIAs inside optimistic projections; the value of naming them is that you can then design cheap experiments to check whether reality agrees before you bet the company on them.

## Context & Usage

Identify your CIAs at the start of any new venture or major initiative, then rank them by how damaging it would be if each turned out false and how uncertain each is. Attack the most dangerous, most uncertain one immediately with the cheapest possible test. If your entire plan assumes people will pay $50 a month for a service, that assumption is a CIA worth testing with a shadow test or pre-sale before you build anything — because if it's wrong, no amount of good execution elsewhere saves you. Retiring CIAs one at a time turns a risky leap into a series of survivable steps.
