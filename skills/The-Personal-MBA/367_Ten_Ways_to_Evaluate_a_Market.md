# Ten Ways to Evaluate a Market

## Overview

Kaufman offers a ten-factor scorecard for sizing up any market before you commit to it: (1) urgency — how badly do people want this right now; (2) market size — how many people are actively buying; (3) pricing potential — the highest price a typical buyer would accept; (4) cost of customer acquisition — how easily and cheaply you can find buyers; (5) cost of value delivery — how much it costs to create and deliver; (6) uniqueness of offer — how hard it is for competitors to copy; (7) speed to market — how quickly you can start selling; (8) up-front investment — what you must spend before earning; (9) upsell potential — whether you can sell more to the same customers; and (10) evergreen potential — whether the offer keeps selling with little added effort. Score each from 0 to 10; Kaufman's rule of thumb is that a total below 50 means walk away, while 75 and above signals a promising market worth pursuing.

## Context & Usage

Run this checklist when choosing between several business ideas or deciding whether an opportunity is worth entering at all. It forces you to confront weak spots honestly — a market can score high on size and urgency but crater on cost of acquisition or up-front investment, and the low scores are exactly what sink ventures. Scoring three candidate ideas side by side turns a gut hunch into a comparison you can defend, and often reveals that the most exciting idea is not the most viable one.
