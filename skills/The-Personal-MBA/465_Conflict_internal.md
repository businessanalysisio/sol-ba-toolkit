# Conflict (internal)

## Overview

In perceptual-control terms, internal conflict is two control systems inside the same mind demanding incompatible perceptions at the same time — you want to grow the business aggressively AND you want never to risk your savings; you want to fire the underperformer AND you want to be liked. Because satisfying one reference level violates the other, the result is oscillation, second-guessing, and paralysis. No amount of extra willpower resolves it; only changing one of the conflicting reference levels does.

## Context & Usage

Diagnose with this model whenever you (or a stakeholder) keep circling a decision without acting. Chronic procrastination on a specific choice is usually not laziness but an unresolved collision of desires — surface both sides explicitly, then consciously demote or reframe one. A founder torn between "keep full control" and "raise capital to grow" must decide which perception matters more before any financing conversation can be productive. Naming the conflict out loud is often enough to break the stall.
