# Receptivity

## Overview

Receptivity is how open a person is to hearing your message at the moment you deliver it. The identical message can succeed or fail depending entirely on whether the recipient is in a frame of mind to receive it — Kaufman's point is that timing and context often matter more than the content of the message itself. An advertisement for pizza is far more effective delivered to hungry people at dinnertime than to the same people right after a large meal. Receptivity depends on relevance (does this connect to what they care about right now) and on the absence of resistance (are they in a mindset that welcomes rather than resents the message). Marketing to receptive audiences is dramatically more efficient than trying to force a message on people who aren't ready for it.

## Context & Usage

Consider receptivity whenever you choose where, when, and to whom you deliver a message. A weight-loss program advertised in early January meets far higher receptivity than the same ad in mid-December. Rather than blasting a message widely and hoping, position it where your audience is already thinking about the relevant problem — a moving-supplies ad served to someone who just searched for apartments reaches a highly receptive mind. When response rates disappoint, the problem is often not the message but the moment; fix the timing and context before rewriting the copy.
