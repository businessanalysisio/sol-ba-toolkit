# Habits

## Overview

Habits are actions that run on autopilot once triggered by a cue — and because they execute without drawing on willpower, they are the only sustainable way to make a beneficial behavior permanent. The installation cost is front-loaded: the first weeks of a new habit demand conscious inhibition and structure, after which the behavior becomes nearly free to maintain. Small habits compound; a daily fifteen-minute pipeline review is trivial on any given day and transformative over a year.

## Context & Usage

Install one habit at a time, anchored to an existing cue. "After I pour the first coffee, I write the day's three MITs" borrows the reliability of an established routine; a habit with no anchor depends on remembering, which is exactly what fails. The same logic runs organizations: weekly retrospectives, Friday invoice runs, and Monday metrics reviews are institutional habits — once cued by the calendar and made routine, they happen without heroics or reminders. When a good practice keeps lapsing, the diagnosis is usually a missing cue or a structure that makes the habit inconvenient, not weak commitment.
