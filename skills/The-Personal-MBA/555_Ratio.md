# Ratio

## Overview

A Ratio expresses one quantity in terms of another, and Kaufman prizes it because the relationship between two numbers usually carries more meaning than either number alone. Raw totals are hard to interpret and easy to be misled by; putting one over another builds comparison directly into the figure. "We spent $50,000 on marketing" says little until you divide by the customers it produced to get a $50 cost per acquisition, which you can then set against the $200 each customer is worth. Ratios turn scattered absolutes into comparable, decision-ready measures — conversion rate (buyers ÷ visitors), revenue per employee, gross margin (profit ÷ revenue), customer lifetime value over acquisition cost. Because they normalize away size, ratios let you compare a big operation against a small one, this month against last, one channel against another, on equal footing.

## Context & Usage

Whenever you're handed an absolute number, look for the ratio that gives it meaning — per customer, per dollar spent, per unit of time, as a share of the whole. The most useful business ratios pair a result with what it cost to produce, exposing efficiency rather than just volume: a channel driving huge traffic can be your worst on cost-per-sale. Track key ratios over time, since their movement is often a leading signal that raw totals mask (rising revenue with a falling margin is trouble hiding inside a good headline). Ratios are how Context (552) gets built into a metric, and many of the most important KPIs (549) are ratios rather than counts.
