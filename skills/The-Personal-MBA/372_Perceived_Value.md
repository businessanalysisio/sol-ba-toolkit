# Perceived Value

## Overview

Customers never pay for what something actually cost you to make; they pay for what they believe it is worth to them. Perceived value is that subjective estimate, and it is the real determinant of price. Two coffees with identical ingredients command wildly different prices depending on the cup, the setting, the brand, and the story around them — the beans did not change, the perception did. Because perceived value lives in the customer's mind, it can be raised without raising costs: through better presentation, stronger association with a desired end result, social proof, guarantees that reduce risk, and framing that connects the offer to what the buyer already wants. This is why Kaufman treats value creation and marketing as inseparable — value that is not perceived is value that is not paid for.

## Context & Usage

Reach for this whenever you are setting prices or feel your offer is "worth more than customers will pay." The problem is usually not the value delivered but the value perceived, and the fix is communication, not deeper features. A consultant who reframes a report as "a decision that will save you $200k" rather than "40 pages of analysis" changes nothing about the work while multiplying its perceived worth. Audit every touchpoint — packaging, first impression, testimonials — for where perception can be lifted.
