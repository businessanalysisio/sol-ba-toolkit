# Probable Purchaser

## Overview

A probable purchaser is someone who is genuinely likely to buy what you're selling — and Kaufman's insistence is that marketing should be aimed at these people, not at "everyone." Trying to appeal to the entire world dilutes your message into blandness and wastes resources chasing people who will never buy. The probable purchaser has identifiable characteristics: a specific problem your offer solves, the money to pay for it, and the authority to make the purchase. Concentrating attention, message, and spend on this narrow group is far more effective than broad, unfocused reach, because a message crafted precisely for a well-defined buyer resonates in a way a generic message never can.

## Context & Usage

Define your probable purchaser before spending a dollar on marketing. The more precisely you can describe who is most likely to buy — their situation, their pain, their budget — the more sharply you can target where you advertise and what you say. A company selling premium ergonomic office chairs wastes money marketing to students but thrives targeting remote knowledge workers with back pain and disposable income. When marketing underperforms, a frequent cause is aiming too broadly; narrowing to the true probable purchaser usually lifts response more than any change in creative. This pairs directly with addressability — you must be able to reach the probable purchasers you identify.
