# Minimum Economically Viable Offer (MEVO)

## Overview

The Minimum Economically Viable Offer is the simplest version of your offer that people will actually pay for. It is the smallest thing that delivers enough real value to justify a purchase — no more. Kaufman's emphasis on "economically viable," as distinct from the more familiar minimum viable product, is deliberate: the bar is not "usable" or "demo-able" but "someone hands over money for it." Getting to a MEVO fast matters because it starts the flow of real revenue and, more importantly, real paying-customer feedback — the only feedback that reliably tells you whether you're building something valuable. Everything beyond the MEVO can be added later, once customers are already buying and telling you what they actually need next.

## Context & Usage

Aim for a MEVO whenever you're tempted to perfect an offer before releasing it. Rather than spending a year building the full-featured version, identify the one core benefit customers will pay for and sell that first — a consultant might launch with a single well-defined service rather than a whole menu. Selling the minimum viable version immediately does two things: it proves the market with real transactions, and it funds and directs the incremental augmentation that follows. If you can't get anyone to buy even the minimum, that's crucial information you'd rather learn in a month than a year.
