# Four Methods to Increase Revenue

## Overview

Kaufman reduces the seemingly endless list of growth tactics to just four levers, and every revenue-boosting idea is really one of them: increase the number of customers, increase the average size of each transaction, increase the frequency of transactions per customer, or raise your prices. That is the complete set. A restaurant can grow by seating more diners (customers), by selling appetizers and wine alongside entrées (transaction size), by turning occasional visitors into weekly regulars via a loyalty program (frequency), or by charging more per dish (price). Knowing there are only four moves stops the scattershot pursuit of every marketing fad and forces a direct question: which of these four are we actually trying to move, and does this initiative move it?

## Context & Usage

Use this as a diagnostic and prioritization filter whenever revenue needs to grow. Map any proposed tactic to one of the four levers; if it maps to none, it is not a revenue initiative. Then weigh cost and leverage — acquiring new customers is usually the most expensive lever, while raising prices (see Pricing Uncertainty Principle, 409) and increasing transaction size (via cross-sells and bundles) often flow almost straight to the bottom line because they add little cost. A software company chasing endless new signups might find that a modest price increase and an upsell to existing users produces more profit than a costly acquisition campaign. The model's value is forcing that comparison instead of defaulting to "get more customers."
