# The Mercenary Rule

## Overview

The Mercenary Rule is Kaufman's warning against starting a business purely to make money. Ventures almost always take longer, cost more, and demand more grinding persistence than expected, and money alone is a thin fuel for that grind. When the only motivation is profit, the founder tends to abandon ship the moment the returns look slow or a shinier opportunity appears — which is precisely when most businesses would have turned a corner if the owner had stayed. The rule does not say money is unimportant; it says money is insufficient as the primary reason, because a purely financial calculation rarely survives contact with the difficulty of actually building something.

## Context & Usage

Apply this as a self-check before launching a venture, especially one chosen because it "looks lucrative." If you cannot summon genuine interest in the customers, the problem, or the craft of the work itself, expect to quit before it pays off. Kaufman pairs it with the Crusader Rule (370): the durable middle ground is a business you both believe in and can make economically viable. A useful test — imagine the venture takes three hard years to become profitable; if the money is the only thing keeping you in, choose a different idea.
