# Performance Load

## Overview

Performance Load is the total amount of effort — mental and physical — required to accomplish a task, and Kaufman's key claim is that the higher that load, the less likely the task is to get done, well or at all. Every extra step, decision, form field, or bit of friction between a person and their goal drains energy and raises the odds they give up partway. Load comes in two flavors: cognitive load (how much you must think, remember, and decide) and kinematic load (how much you must physically do). Reducing load is one of the most reliable ways to improve both your own follow-through and other people's behavior, because it works with human limits rather than demanding more willpower to overcome them.

## Context & Usage

Use this as a design principle anywhere you want a behavior to happen reliably — your own habits, a customer's checkout, an employee's process. If a desirable action keeps not happening, suspect excessive load before suspecting laziness: a checkout that asks for fifteen fields loses buyers a one-click purchase would keep, and a personal habit like "go to the gym" is likelier to stick if you sleep in your workout clothes and pick the closest gym. Strip steps, pre-make decisions, provide defaults, and remove friction from the path you want taken. Inversely, you can deliberately raise the load on behaviors you want to discourage — deleting a distracting app so it takes effort to reinstall. It pairs with Parkinson's Law (495) and the finite Gas Tank (458): low load conserves the limited energy each day provides.
