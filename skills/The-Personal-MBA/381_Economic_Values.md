# Economic Values

## Overview

Kaufman identifies nine dimensions people use to judge how much an offer is worth to them: **efficacy** (how well it works), **speed** (how fast it works), **reliability** (whether it works consistently), **ease of use** (how little effort it demands), **flexibility** (how many things it can do), **status** (how it makes the owner look to others), **aesthetic appeal** (how attractive or pleasing it is), **emotion** (how it makes the buyer feel), and **cost** (how little they must give up to get it). Any offer can be evaluated and improved along these nine axes, and different customers weight them differently. Recognizing which economic values your buyers actually care about tells you where to invest in improvement and which selling points to emphasize — and it interacts directly with trade-offs, since strengthening one value often costs another.

## Context & Usage

Use the nine economic values as a checklist when improving an offer or crafting its pitch. If a product feels undifferentiated, run it through the list and ask which dimension your target segment prizes and where you could visibly lead — a tool that competitors match on efficacy might win decisively on ease of use or aesthetic appeal. A luxury watch is sold almost entirely on status, emotion, and aesthetics, not efficacy, so its marketing rightly ignores timekeeping accuracy. Pair this with relative importance testing (382) to find out which values your specific customers rank highest instead of guessing.
