# Golden Trifecta

## Overview

Kaufman's upgrade of the Golden Rule for working relationships: treat every person with **appreciation**, **courtesy**, and **respect** — the three things everyone wants to receive in every interaction. Appreciation means acknowledging what the other person contributes; courtesy means basic politeness and consideration even under pressure; respect means honoring their status and treating them as capable adults. People gravitate toward, and go out of their way for, those who consistently deliver all three — which is why the trifecta compounds into influence that formal authority cannot buy.

## Context & Usage

Use as a default operating posture with colleagues, reports, vendors, and customers — and as a diagnostic when a relationship sours. If a supplier keeps deprioritizing your orders or a stakeholder has turned obstructive, audit recent interactions against the three elements; the missing one is usually obvious (a dashed-off demand with no thanks, a public correction that cost them face). Example: a business analyst who opens a requirements workshop by crediting the ops team's workarounds gets richer elicitation than one who opens with a list of process defects.
