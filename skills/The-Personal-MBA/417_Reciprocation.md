# Reciprocation

## Overview

Reciprocation is the deep, near-universal human tendency to return favors — when someone gives us something, we feel an obligation to give something back. Kaufman treats it as one of the most reliable social forces in business: by providing genuine value first, with no strings attached, you create goodwill that people naturally want to repay with attention, trust, or a purchase. The free sample at a store, the useful article given away before any pitch, the restaurant mint left with the check that measurably raises tips — all work because receiving triggers a felt debt. The mechanism holds only when the initial gift feels sincere rather than a transparent lever; manipulative giving ("I gave you this, now buy") breaks the effect and can curdle into resentment. Reciprocation is why so much modern marketing leads with free content, tools, or trials.

## Context & Usage

Apply reciprocation early in a relationship, before you ask for anything. Example: a consultant who publishes a genuinely helpful teardown of a prospect's website, freely and with no sales ask, finds that prospect far more inclined to hire them later — the free work created an unspoken obligation and demonstrated competence at once. The rule is give first and give real value; treat the return as a likely consequence, not a guaranteed transaction. Over-asking relative to what you gave, or making the debt explicit, destroys the goodwill you built.
