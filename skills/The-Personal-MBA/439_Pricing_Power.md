# Pricing Power

## Overview

Pricing Power is the ability to raise your prices without losing a meaningful number of customers. Kaufman treats it as a direct readout of how strong a business really is: if you can increase prices and demand barely moves, you have a genuinely differentiated offer, a loyal base, or a weak set of substitutes — and raising the price flows almost entirely to profit, since it changes no costs. If even a small increase sends customers fleeing to competitors, you have little pricing power and are effectively a commodity competing on price alone. Pricing power comes from things like a trusted brand, switching costs, unique capability, or emotional attachment. It is one of the most valuable properties a business can have, because a price increase is the single most efficient way to raise profit when the market will bear it.

## Context & Usage

Test your pricing power periodically, and build it deliberately if you have little. Example: a premium brand raises prices 10% and sees sales dip only 2% — net profit rises sharply because loyal customers absorb it, revealing strong pricing power. Signs you lack it include customers who shop purely on price and immediate defections whenever you nudge prices up; the fix is differentiation, brand, or lock-in rather than another discount. Because a price change touches profit directly with no added cost, testing whether the market will accept a higher price is often the highest-return experiment a business can run.
