# Self-Elicitation

## Overview

Self-Elicitation is the technique of deliberately asking yourself questions to draw out knowledge and ideas you already possess but have not yet articulated. Kaufman's observation is that the brain is a relentless question-answering machine — pose it a question and it will keep working on it, consciously and in the background, until it produces something. Most people never exploit this because they never ask themselves precise questions; they wait for insight to arrive. By feeding your mind well-formed queries ("What would make this offer irresistible?", "What is the real reason this deadline keeps slipping?"), you direct that answering machinery at the problem you actually care about, and answers surface that were latent all along. The quality of the answer is bounded by the quality of the question.

## Context & Usage

Use it when you are stuck, planning, or trying to reach a decision, and pair it with Externalization (492) — write the question down, then write whatever the mind offers, without censoring. Open, generative questions work best: "What am I missing here?" or "If this had to succeed, what would I do first?" A founder unsure how to differentiate might ask nightly, "What does my best customer wish existed that no one offers?" and find the answer assembled by morning. The method also inoculates against passivity: rather than hoping the answer comes, you take charge of the questions your mind chews on. Beware questions that presuppose failure ("Why can't I ever finish anything?"), since the machine will dutifully generate reasons; frame questions toward what you want to find.
