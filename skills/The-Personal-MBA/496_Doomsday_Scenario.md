# Doomsday Scenario

## Overview

Fear paralyses action because the mind treats a vague threat as far worse than any concrete outcome. The Doomsday Scenario is Kaufman's antidote: instead of suppressing the anxiety, deliberately play the worst realistic case all the way through — the deal collapses, the launch flops, you lose the money — and then ask what you would actually do next. Almost always the honest answer is that you would survive, recover, and have options. Because the brain's Threat Lockdown response fires hardest at the unknown, naming the specific downside drains most of its power; the fear that felt infinite turns out to be finite, bounded, and usually recoverable. The model is a decision-clearing tool, not fatalism: once the true floor is visible, you can weigh it against the upside and act instead of freezing.

## Context & Usage

Reach for this whenever fear of a bad outcome is stopping a decision that the numbers otherwise support — quitting a job to start a business, spending on a first ad campaign, pitching a much larger client. Concretely: a founder terrified to leave salaried work writes down the actual worst case (savings gone in nine months, then back to a similar job) and realises it is survivable, freeing the decision. Pair it with an honest reserve-and-runway check so the "doomsday" you rehearse is the real one, not a catastrophised fantasy.
