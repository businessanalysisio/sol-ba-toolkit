# Three Universal Currencies

## Overview

Kaufman argues that in any negotiation there are only three things you can actually trade: resources, time, and flexibility. Resources are tangible assets — money, equipment, people, materials. Time is the hours and attention a party must commit. Flexibility is freedom of action: the right to choose how, when, and with whom something is done. Every concession and every demand can be expressed in these terms, and crucially the three are convertible — you can trade away flexibility to preserve resources (a lower price in exchange for a long exclusive contract), or spend time to save resources (doing work yourself instead of hiring out). Seeing a deal as an exchange across these three currencies reveals options that a single-axis fight over money completely hides.

## Context & Usage

Use this when a negotiation stalls on one dimension, because the currencies give you levers to widen the Common Ground (408). If a client balks at your fee (resources), you can offer a lower number in return for a longer timeline (time) or fewer revision rounds (flexibility) — trading currencies you value less for ones you value more. A cash-poor startup pays a key hire in equity and autonomy (flexibility) rather than salary (resources). Ask, for each party, which currency is scarce and which is abundant: the strongest deals move a currency from someone who values it little to someone who values it a lot, creating value on both sides rather than just splitting a fixed sum of money.
