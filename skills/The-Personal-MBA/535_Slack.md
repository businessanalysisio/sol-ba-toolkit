# Slack

## Overview

Slack is the reserve capacity a system carries but is not currently using — the unbooked hours in a schedule, the cash beyond this month's bills, the staffing above the minimum needed to run. It looks like waste to an efficiency-minded eye, and that is exactly the trap Kaufman warns against: slack is what absorbs variability and shocks, and a system run at 100% utilization has no way to accommodate the surprises that arrive on every real timeline. A calendar booked solid means one delayed meeting cascades into a ruined day; a team with no bench means one resignation stalls a project; a supply chain with no buffer stock stops the line the first time a shipment is late. Some slack is the price of resilience, adaptability, and the ability to seize unexpected opportunities.

## Context & Usage

Deliberately build in slack wherever variability is high and the cost of failure is real — leave gaps between meetings, keep a cash cushion beyond forecast, don't schedule every engineer to full capacity on committed work. Treat any drive to eliminate all slack in the name of efficiency as a red flag: it optimizes for the average case and guarantees fragility in the tail. The balance is judgment — too much slack is genuine waste and too little is brittleness — so size the reserve to the volatility you actually face. Slack is the practical counterweight to Optimization (562), which, pushed too far, strips out exactly this protective margin.
