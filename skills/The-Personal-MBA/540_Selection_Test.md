# Selection Test

## Overview

A Selection Test is any criterion that decides what passes into or through a system and what gets filtered out — and whatever passes the test is what propagates. Kaufman's insight is that the character of a system over time is set by its selection tests, often more than by any deliberate plan: change the filter and you change everything downstream. A hiring bar determines the kind of people a company accumulates; an editorial standard shapes what a publication becomes; the ranking criteria of a search engine decide what content the whole web produces. The test does not need to be conscious or written down to have this effect — an informal norm about "who fits here" is a selection test, quietly steering who joins and stays. Whatever survives repeated exposure to the test comes to define the system.

## Context & Usage

When you want to shape what a system produces over the long run, work on its selection tests rather than trying to correct outputs one at a time. To raise the quality of a team, tighten the hiring and retention filter; to change what customers you attract, change the offer and price that select them; to improve a portfolio of projects, sharpen the criterion that decides which projects get funded. Make implicit tests explicit so you can inspect and improve them, and check for tests that select for the wrong thing — a metric-based filter that rewards activity over results will reliably accumulate busy people who accomplish little. The environment (539) is what enforces the test.
