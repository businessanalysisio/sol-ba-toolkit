# Interpretation and Reinterpretation

## Overview

Memories are not recordings; they are reconstructions, and every reconstruction carries an interpretation — a layer of meaning the mind attaches to what happened. Because the meaning is added at recall time rather than fixed at the event, it can be deliberately revised. The failed product launch that "proved I'm not cut out for this" can be legitimately reinterpreted as the cheapest market research you ever bought. The facts stay constant; the story, and therefore the emotional charge and the behavior it drives, can change.

## Context & Usage

Use reinterpretation when a past event is constraining present action. A salesperson gun-shy after a brutal rejection, a founder avoiding partnerships after one bad deal, a team demoralized by a cancelled project — in each case the interpretation, not the event, is doing the damage. Ask: what else could this experience mean? What did it teach that nothing cheaper could have? This is not self-deception; it is choosing among equally valid readings the one that keeps you operating.
