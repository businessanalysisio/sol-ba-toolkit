# Field Testing

## Overview

Field testing means using your own offer under real conditions, the way an actual customer would, before and after you release it — the discipline often summed up as "eat your own cooking." Controlled demos and lab checks miss the friction, edge cases, and annoyances that only surface in genuine use, so Kaufman urges creators to live with their offer in the real environment. When you rely on the thing you've built to do a real job, its shortcomings become impossible to ignore, and you experience them exactly as your customers will. Field testing turns you into your own most demanding user, catching problems that no amount of internal review or customer survey would reveal.

## Context & Usage

Field-test whenever an offer will be used in conditions you can't fully replicate in development. A team building expense-tracking software should run the company's actual expenses through it; a chef designing a menu should cook and eat it during a real dinner rush, not just taste-test in a quiet kitchen. The gap between "works in the demo" and "works in the field" is where reputations are lost, so closing it before customers do is cheap insurance. Combine this with real customer feedback — your own experience surfaces some issues, but customers in their own contexts will find others you can't.
