# Value-Based Selling

## Overview

Value-Based Selling means anchoring the entire sale on the value the offer creates for this specific buyer, rather than on its features, your costs, or the competition's price. Kaufman's point is that people buy outcomes, not attributes — a customer does not want a drill, they want a hole, and beneath that a shelf, and beneath that the feeling of a tidy home. The seller's job is to understand what the prospect actually values, then frame the offer as the most direct route to that end state, ideally quantified in the buyer's own terms. Because it prices against value delivered (the highest of the Four Pricing Methods, 410), value-based selling supports far larger prices than feature-based or cost-plus approaches, and it makes the price feel like a bargain relative to the payoff.

## Context & Usage

Use it whenever you can connect your offer to a measurable or emotionally significant result for the buyer — most powerful in B2B and high-consideration purchases. The discipline is to research the customer's situation before pitching, then lead with their world: for a consultant, "this process will recover roughly $200,000 a year in lost margin" lands far harder than "I have fifteen years of experience." Anchor the price against that recovered value ($40,000 to recover $200,000 is obviously worth it) rather than against hours worked. It fails when you cannot articulate a concrete benefit, which is itself a useful signal that either the offer or your understanding of the buyer needs work.
