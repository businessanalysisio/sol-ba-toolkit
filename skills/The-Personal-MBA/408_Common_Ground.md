# Common Ground

## Overview

Common Ground is the overlap between what you want and what the other party wants — the region in which a mutually acceptable deal actually exists. Kaufman frames negotiation not as a battle to be won but as a search for this shared territory: if there is no overlap between your acceptable terms and theirs, no amount of persuasion produces a transaction, and the honest move is to walk away. The larger you can make the overlap — by uncovering interests beyond the single number on the table, such as timing, risk, volume, or future work — the more room both sides have to construct an agreement each genuinely prefers to no deal.

## Context & Usage

Use this before and during any negotiation to redirect the conversation from positions ("I want $10,000") to interests ("I need predictable cash flow this quarter"), because interests reveal overlap that fixed positions hide. When a talk deadlocks on price, the productive question is what else each party values — a buyer who cannot move on price may happily concede a longer contract, faster payment terms, or a testimonial, any of which widens the common ground. A freelancer and client stuck $2,000 apart on fee might close by trading a reduced rate for a case-study reference and a three-month retainer. If, after genuinely exploring interests, no overlap appears, the model tells you the deal is not there and your energy belongs elsewhere.
