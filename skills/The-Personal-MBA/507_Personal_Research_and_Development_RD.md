# Personal Research and Development (R&D)

## Overview

Personal Research and Development is the practice of deliberately investing in your own knowledge and skills, the way a company reinvests profits into R&D to stay competitive. Kaufman's framing is that your capabilities are your most important asset, and like any asset they either appreciate through investment or quietly depreciate as the world moves on. The investment is time, attention, and often a little money spent on learning something genuinely new — a skill, a field, a tool — with the expectation that it compounds into future capacity you cannot yet fully foresee. Because the payoff is uncertain and deferred, personal R&D is easy to postpone indefinitely under the pressure of urgent work, which is precisely why treating it as a standing commitment rather than a someday-luxury matters.

## Context & Usage

Budget for it explicitly: reserve regular hours for learning that is not tied to an immediate deliverable, and choose skills adjacent to what you already do so they combine into rare, valuable capability (see Comparative Advantage, 510). The most productive personal R&D is active rather than passive — building a small project, running an experiment (503), or teaching what you're learning cements far more than reading alone. A developer who spends a few hours a week learning to write and sell, or a marketer learning basic data analysis, is manufacturing future options. Watch the common failure mode of consuming endless content that never becomes capability; tie learning to application so the "development" half actually happens. It compounds with The Growth Mind-set (508), which supplies the belief that the investment will pay off.
