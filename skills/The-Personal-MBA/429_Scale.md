# Scale

## Overview

Scale is the measure of how well a system holds up as the quantity it must handle increases. Kaufman's key distinction is that some things scale and some do not: a software product can serve one user or ten million with roughly the same core system, whereas a massage therapist cannot serve more clients without proportionally more of their own hours. A business that scales can grow output dramatically without a matching growth in cost or effort; a business that doesn't scale hits a hard ceiling set by a resource that must grow one-for-one with demand — usually human time. Understanding where a business sits on this spectrum tells you its realistic size and its economics: high-scale models get cheaper per unit as they grow, while low-scale models stay expensive no matter how large they get.

## Context & Usage

Assess scalability before betting on growth, and design for it early if a large business is the goal. Example: a founder choosing between one-on-one coaching (revenue capped by their available hours) and a self-serve app (revenue capable of growing while they sleep) is really choosing how much their model can scale. When something won't scale, the fix is usually to shift the value into a scalable form — productizing a service, automating a manual step, or duplicating the deliverable. Watch for the resource that must grow with demand; that resource is your scaling constraint.
