# Multiplication

## Overview

Multiplication is taking something you can duplicate and applying it across many units, locations, or markets to multiply total output. Where duplication copies a single thing cheaply, multiplication repeats a whole working system many times over. Kaufman's clearest example is the franchise: McDonald's did not just perfect one restaurant, it created a documented, repeatable system for running a restaurant and then stamped that system out thousands of times, each new outlet reproducing the same operation. Mass production does the same with a manufacturing process, and a media company does it by pushing one piece of content across many channels. Multiplication turns a proven small success into a large one by replication rather than reinvention — which is why the hard and valuable work is getting the repeatable unit right before you multiply it.

## Context & Usage

Reach for multiplication once you have one instance of something that reliably works and want its impact many times larger. Example: a restaurateur with one profitable location documents every process into an operations manual, then opens or franchises additional locations that each reproduce the proven model, instead of designing each new restaurant from scratch. Do not multiply prematurely: replicating an unprofitable or unsystemized unit just multiplies the problems. The prerequisites are a duplicable core and a systemized process, so that each new copy performs like the original without you personally running it.
