# Pygmalion Effect

## Overview

People tend to perform to the level others expect of them: raise the expectation and performance rises; lower it and performance falls. The classic demonstration is Rosenthal and Jacobson's classroom study, where teachers told that randomly selected students were "bloomers" saw exactly those students improve — the expectation changed the teachers' behavior (more attention, more challenge, more patience), which changed the results. Expectations leak through countless small signals, so they function as self-fulfilling prophecies in both directions.

## Context & Usage

For anyone leading people: communicate genuinely high expectations paired with support, and audit where you have quietly written someone off — your micro-behaviors (assigning them only routine work, double-checking everything they touch) are actively producing the mediocrity you expect. Example: giving a junior analyst a stretch assignment with the framing "I'm giving you this because I think you can handle it" reliably outperforms giving them the same work as damage-limitation. The effect also runs upward and outward: teams live up (or down) to how leadership talks about them, and customers treated as intelligent adults behave better than customers treated as suspects.
