# Autocatalysis

## Overview

Autocatalysis is a reinforcing loop that feeds itself: the output of a process becomes the fuel for more of that same process, so once it starts it accelerates on its own. Kaufman borrows the chemistry term — a reaction whose product catalyzes further reaction — as the systems name for a self-sustaining flywheel. The classic business version is profit reinvested to generate more profit, or the loop Amazon is famous for: lower prices draw more customers, more customers attract more sellers, more selection and volume drive costs down further, enabling still lower prices. Referrals that produce customers who themselves refer are autocatalytic; so is content that ranks, draws readers who link to it, and thereby ranks higher. The signature is compounding: growth that comes from the system's own past output rather than from ever-larger fresh inputs.

## Context & Usage

Hunt deliberately for a place where you can wire a business's output back into its own growth, because an autocatalytic loop is the difference between growth you have to keep pushing and growth that builds momentum by itself. The starting problem is that these loops are weak and fragile at the beginning — a trickle of referrals catalyzes almost nothing — so the practical work is getting the loop past the threshold where its own output sustains it, often by subsidizing the early turns (seeding reviews, funding first referrals, reinvesting all early profit). Once ignited, protect the loop above almost anything else, and watch that the same mechanism can run in reverse into a destructive spiral.
