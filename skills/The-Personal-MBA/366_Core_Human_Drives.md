# Core Human Drives

## Overview

Drawing on Lawrence and Nohria's research in *Driven*, Kaufman identifies five fundamental drives that motivate nearly all human behavior: the drive to acquire (to obtain and collect things, status, influence), the drive to bond (to belong and connect with others), the drive to learn (to satisfy curiosity and master skills), the drive to defend (to protect ourselves, our property, and our loved ones), and the drive to feel (to seek emotional experiences — pleasure, excitement, comfort). Every successful offer appeals to one or more of these drives, and the strongest offers stack several at once. A luxury car appeals to acquire and feel; a gym membership to defend and feel; a university degree to learn, acquire, and bond. Because these drives are wired into people, they do not need to be created — only recognized and served.

## Context & Usage

Use this when defining an offer's positioning or diagnosing why something isn't selling. If your product connects to no core drive, no clever marketing will rescue it. When you can name the drives your offer serves, you know which benefits to emphasize and which words will resonate. For example, a home security system sells poorly on feature lists but powerfully on the drive to defend — so lead with protection of family, not sensor specifications.
