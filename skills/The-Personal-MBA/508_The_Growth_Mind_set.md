# The Growth Mind-set

## Overview

The Growth Mind-set, drawn from Carol Dweck's research, is the belief that your abilities are not fixed traits you were born with but capacities you can develop through effort, practice, and learning. Its opposite, the fixed mind-set, treats intelligence and talent as static — you either have it or you don't. Kaufman stresses that which belief you hold quietly shapes everything you attempt: someone with a growth mind-set treats a hard problem or a failure as information and an opportunity to improve, so they persist and get better; someone with a fixed mind-set experiences the same failure as proof of their limits, so they avoid challenges that might expose them and stop trying. The mind-set is partly self-fulfilling, because the person who believes they can improve does the practice that actually produces improvement.

## Context & Usage

Cultivate this in yourself and the people you lead, especially around setbacks and unfamiliar skills. Catch fixed-mind-set language — "I'm just not a numbers person," "I could never sell" — and reframe it toward development: "I haven't learned that yet." Praise and self-talk that focus on effort, strategy, and progress reinforce growth, while praise fixated on innate talent quietly installs the fixed frame and a fear of ever looking untalented. A founder who treats a failed launch as a lesson in what customers don't want will iterate toward something that works; one who reads it as evidence they lack business instinct may quit. It is the belief that makes Personal R&D (507) worth doing and that dissolves the disabling Mystique (504) around successful people.
