# The Crusader Rule

## Overview

The Crusader Rule is the constructive counterpart to the Mercenary Rule: it is always better to build a business you genuinely believe in — one you would be proud to describe to your family and happy to be associated with. Belief in the work is not sentimentality; it is what sustains the years of effort a real business demands and what keeps you honest with customers. Kaufman's caution, though, is that being a crusader is not a license to ignore economics. A cause you care about deeply but that no one will pay to support is not a business — it is a hobby or a charity. The rule points to a balance: pursue work meaningful enough to carry you through hardship, while insisting it also stands on a viable market.

## Context & Usage

Use this alongside the Mercenary Rule (369) when deciding what to build. If money alone is dangerous fuel, so is pure mission divorced from viability — many passionate founders burn out defending an offer the market never wanted. The healthy zone is the overlap: something you'd be genuinely glad to spend years on that also passes a market test. Before committing, ask both questions — "Would I be proud of this?" and "Will enough people pay for it to sustain me?" — and require a yes to each.
