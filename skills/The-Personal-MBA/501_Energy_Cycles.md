# Energy Cycles

## Overview

Energy Cycles are the natural, recurring rises and falls in physical and mental energy that everyone moves through — across the day, and in shorter waves of roughly 90 to 120 minutes of focus followed by a need to rest. Kaufman's point is that these cycles are biological facts, not signs of weakness, and that human energy is fundamentally rhythmic rather than a flat line you can hold at maximum by sheer will. Most people have a daily peak (often mid-morning) when concentration and judgment are sharpest, and predictable troughs (commonly early afternoon) when pushing hard yields little. Fighting the cycles — grinding through a trough on caffeine and willpower — produces poor work and drains the Gas Tank (458) faster; working with them multiplies output.

## Context & Usage

Use this to schedule work by energy rather than just by time. Reserve your peak hours for the most demanding, highest-value cognitive work — strategy, writing, hard decisions — and park email, admin, and routine tasks in the low-energy troughs where they do no harm. Honor the shorter waves too: after 60–90 minutes of deep focus, a genuine break (movement, food, rest) restores far more than trying to push through the fog. A writer who does creative work first thing and answers messages after lunch will outproduce one who lets the morning peak leak away on inbox triage. Track your own pattern for a week to find your personal peaks and troughs, then defend the peaks fiercely. It connects directly to The Gas Tank (458) and Stress and Recovery (502).
