# Key Performance Indicator

## Overview

A Key Performance Indicator is a measure that tracks the health of a critical process — the vital signs of a business, in Kaufman's framing. The emphasis is on key: of the hundreds of things you could measure, only a handful genuinely reflect whether the system is succeeding, and those are the ones worth watching continuously. A subscription business lives or dies on a small set — new signups, churn rate, revenue per customer, cash runway — and staring at those tells you more about survival than a dashboard of fifty metrics ever could. Kaufman's practical test is that a real KPI is one you would act on: if it moved sharply, you would change something. Numbers that are interesting but wouldn't alter any decision are vanity metrics, and cluttering attention with them dilutes the signal from the few that matter. Good KPIs are also chosen to resist gaming, since people optimize whatever you make them accountable for.

## Context & Usage

Choose KPIs by working backward from the outcomes that actually determine success, then picking the smallest set of measures that reveal movement toward or away from them — typically a handful, reviewed on a regular cadence, not a sprawling report. Watch for the trap where a KPI becomes a target and people optimize the number instead of the underlying reality (calls made instead of problems solved), which is where the Proxy (559) and Selection Test (540) cautions apply. Pair leading indicators that predict with lagging ones that confirm. This is the same discipline BABOK treats as Metrics and KPIs (57), applied at the level of whole-system health.
