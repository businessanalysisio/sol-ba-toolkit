# Next Best Alternative

## Overview

Your Next Best Alternative is the option you fall back on if the current deal collapses — the negotiation literature's BATNA (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement). Kaufman's insight is that the real source of bargaining power is not tactics or bluster but the quality of each side's alternative: whoever needs the deal least, because they have a strong fallback, can hold firm and walk away, while whoever has no alternative is negotiating from weakness. A deal only makes sense when its terms beat your next best alternative; the instant the offer on the table is worse than what you could get elsewhere, walking away is the rational choice, and knowing that line keeps you from being talked into a bad agreement.

## Context & Usage

Before any negotiation, do two things: strengthen your own alternative and estimate the other party's. A job candidate with a second written offer negotiates salary from a completely different footing than one with none, so lining up alternatives beforehand is itself a negotiating move. Gauging the counterparty's alternative tells you how hard you can push — a supplier with a full order book will not discount, while one with idle capacity has a weak fallback and more room to move. The practical rule: never enter a negotiation you cannot afford to lose, because a visible willingness to walk, backed by a genuine alternative, is the most persuasive position available. It also bounds Common Ground (408) — no overlap exists below either party's alternative.
