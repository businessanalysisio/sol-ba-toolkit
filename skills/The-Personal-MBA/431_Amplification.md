# Amplification

## Overview

Amplification is increasing the output of a system by adding energy or leverage to it — magnifying the effect of what you already do. Kaufman's image is the megaphone: the same voice, amplified, reaches a far larger audience. In business, amplification means finding an existing source of value and pouring more resources through the same channel to multiply its result — spending more on an ad that already converts profitably, giving a productive employee better tools, or promoting proven content harder. The critical caveat is that amplification magnifies whatever it acts on, good or bad: amplify a profitable process and you get more profit, but amplify a flawed or unprofitable one and you get bigger losses, faster. So amplification is a lever you apply after you have proven something works, not a way to fix something that doesn't.

## Context & Usage

Use amplification to pour fuel on things already demonstrated to work, once you have evidence of a positive return. Example: a marketer finds one ad campaign returning three dollars for every one spent, so they amplify it by increasing the budget on that specific campaign rather than launching untested new ones. Before amplifying, confirm the underlying unit is genuinely net-positive at small scale; scaling spend on an unprofitable funnel just multiplies the bleed. Watch for diminishing returns — amplification often works until the channel saturates, at which point the extra energy stops converting.
