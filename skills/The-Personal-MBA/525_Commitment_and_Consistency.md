# Commitment and Consistency

## Overview

Cialdini's principle that people feel strong internal pressure to act consistently with what they have previously said and done — especially commitments that were public, voluntary, and effortful. Once someone declares "I'm the kind of person who X," contradicting it costs identity, so they keep honoring the old commitment even when circumstances change. This drives the foot-in-the-door effect: a small yes (sign a petition, accept a free trial, agree the problem matters) measurably raises the odds of a later large yes (donate, subscribe, approve the project).

## Context & Usage

In sales and change management, sequence asks from small to large: get stakeholders to agree on the problem statement before proposing the solution; get a pilot before the rollout; get a verbal "this would solve it" before the contract. Make commitments active and public — a customer who writes down a goal or says it in a meeting follows through far more often. The defensive use matters equally: notice when *you* are persisting with a failing plan mainly because you announced it (consistency pressure plus sunk cost), and give others face-saving ways to change positions, or they will defend wrong ones forever.
