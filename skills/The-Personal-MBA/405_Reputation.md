# Reputation

## Overview

Reputation is what people expect of you based on your past actions — it precedes you into every interaction and quietly shapes how much attention, trust, and money others are willing to give. Kaufman treats reputation as an asset built slowly through consistent behavior and lost quickly through a single betrayal, which is why it demands ongoing, deliberate care. A strong reputation lowers the cost of everything: prospects arrive already trusting you, sales close faster because less proof is required, and you can command premium prices because buyers assume you'll deliver. Because reputation is an expectation formed over time, it can't be bought or faked for long; it is earned by repeatedly doing what you say you'll do.

## Context & Usage

Manage reputation as a long-term investment in every decision that touches customers. When weighing a short-term gain against how it will look and what it signals, remember that reputation compounds — years of reliable delivery create a moat competitors can't easily copy, while one publicized failure to honor a promise can undo it. A company known for standing behind its products can charge more and market less, because its reputation does the persuading. Reputation also makes points of market entry and word-of-mouth work in your favor, since people entering the market ask others whom to trust. Protect it by treating every transaction as a deposit into it.
