# Bystander Apathy

## Overview

The counterintuitive finding from Darley and Latané's emergency experiments: the more people who witness a problem, the *less* likely any individual is to act, because responsibility diffuses across the group — everyone assumes someone else will handle it. In business the same mechanism means a task assigned to "the team" is assigned to no one. An email sent to eight people asking "can someone look into this?" reliably produces eight people waiting for one of the other seven.

## Context & Usage

Counter it by making responsibility singular and explicit: every task gets exactly one named owner and a deadline, even when several people contribute. In meetings, close each agenda item with "who owns this, by when?" — the two questions that convert discussion into action. Diagnostically, when something important has sat untouched for weeks despite everyone agreeing it matters, look for diffused ownership before questioning anyone's competence. A shared inbox with no rotation, a risk log with no owners column, a group chat "FYI" — all are bystander apathy generators.
