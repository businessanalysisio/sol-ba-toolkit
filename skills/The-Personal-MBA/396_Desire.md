# Desire

## Overview

Marketing works by connecting an offer to desires people already have — it does not, and cannot, manufacture desire from nothing. Kaufman is explicit that you can't make people want something they fundamentally don't; what you can do is identify existing desires (rooted in the core human drives) and show how your offer satisfies them. This reframes the marketer's job from persuasion-by-force to accurate connection: find the deep want that already exists in your probable purchaser, then demonstrate credibly that your offer is the path to it. Attempting to sell against genuine indifference is exhausting and usually futile, while channeling a pre-existing desire feels almost effortless because the wanting is already there.

## Context & Usage

Anchor your marketing in real, existing desire whenever you're crafting a message or choosing what to build. If people don't already want the underlying outcome, no clever campaign will save the offer — the fix is to find an audience whose desire is genuine, not to shout louder. A fitness app succeeds by tapping the existing desire to feel healthy and look good (drives to defend and feel), not by trying to make sedentary people care about exercise for the first time. When something won't sell, test whether you've correctly identified and connected to a true desire, or whether you're pushing against indifference.
