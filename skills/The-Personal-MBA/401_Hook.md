# Hook

## Overview

A hook is a single, memorable phrase that captures the primary benefit of your offer and grabs attention instantly. Because attention is scarce and fleeting, you often get only one line to make a prospect care, and the hook is that line — a compressed distillation of the end result and what makes the offer remarkable. Kaufman points to how a strong hook does the heavy lifting of marketing in a few words: it communicates the core value so clearly and compellingly that people want to know more. Think of famous positioning lines that instantly convey a promise; the hook is what makes an offer sticky and repeatable, easy to remember and easy to pass along.

## Context & Usage

Craft a hook whenever you need to lead a marketing message, headline, or elevator pitch. The discipline is ruthless compression: what is the one thing about this offer that, said in a sentence, makes the right person lean in? A meal-kit service's "home-cooked dinners without the grocery run" hooks by naming the end result and the pain removed at once. Test several hooks and keep the one that draws the most response — a weak hook wastes the attention you fought to get, while a strong one turns a glance into engagement. The hook should marry the end result (392) with what makes you remarkable (390).
