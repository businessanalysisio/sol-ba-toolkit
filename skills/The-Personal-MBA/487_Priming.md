# Priming

## Overview

Priming is deliberately programming your attention to notice specific information. The mind filters most of what it perceives, but whatever you have recently focused on gets flagged: buy a particular car and suddenly the road is full of them — the cars were always there; your filter changed. Kaufman's applied version: skim a book's table of contents and write down the questions you want answered before reading, and your brain will surface the answers as you go.

## Context & Usage

Prime before every information-dense activity. Before a stakeholder interview, list the three assumptions you most need to test, and the conversation will yield evidence you'd otherwise talk past. Before reviewing a contract, name the specific risks that worry you (termination terms, IP ownership, payment timing) so the relevant clauses jump out of the boilerplate. Before a trade show, define what a valuable contact looks like. Unprimed attention wanders and returns with generic impressions; primed attention returns with exactly what you sent it to find — which also means priming on the wrong questions blinds you, so revisit the list.
