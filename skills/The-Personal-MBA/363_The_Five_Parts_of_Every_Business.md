# The Five Parts of Every Business

## Overview

Kaufman argues that every functioning business, from a corner bakery to a global manufacturer, is made of exactly five interdependent processes: value creation (discovering what people need and building it), marketing (attracting attention and demand), sales (turning interested prospects into paying customers), value delivery (giving customers what was promised and keeping them satisfied), and finance (bringing in enough money to keep going and make the effort worthwhile). The test is subtractive: remove any one of the five and you no longer have a business. Something that creates and markets but never closes a sale is a hobby; something that sells but cannot deliver is a scam; something that does everything but never turns a profit is a charity or a money-losing venture. The model is a diagnostic map — when a venture is struggling, the first useful question is which of the five parts is broken.

## Context & Usage

Reach for this whenever you are evaluating a business idea, auditing a struggling operation, or deciding where your attention is most needed. A founder pouring energy into a beautiful product (value creation) while ignoring how anyone will hear about it (marketing) has a predictable, locatable failure. Walk the five parts in order and ask of each: does this exist, and does it work? The part that fails the test is where the leverage is. It also clarifies hiring and skill-building — every economically valuable role maps to one of these five functions.
