# Modularity

## Overview

Modularity is the practice of building an offer out of separable, interchangeable components rather than as one fused whole. When the pieces are independent, you can recombine them into many different offers, improve one part without rebuilding everything, and let customers assemble the configuration they want. Kaufman points to how a modular design multiplies value from the same underlying work: a training course broken into standalone lessons can be sold whole, sold as individual modules, repackaged into a shorter workshop, or updated one lesson at a time. Modularity also lowers risk — a defect or improvement is contained to a single module instead of forcing a costly overhaul of the entire product.

## Context & Usage

Design for modularity when you expect to serve varied customers, iterate frequently, or spin one body of work into multiple offers. A software team that builds features as loosely coupled services can ship, price, and improve each independently, and later bundle or unbundle them as the market demands. The discipline pays off most when combined with bundling and unbundling: modular parts are the raw material those strategies rearrange. Ask of any large offer — could this be built as swappable pieces, and what new offers would that make possible?
