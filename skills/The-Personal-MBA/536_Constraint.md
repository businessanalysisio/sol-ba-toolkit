# Constraint

## Overview

In any system there is a single factor that limits total throughput more than all the others — the Constraint, or bottleneck — and the system can produce no faster than that one step allows. Kaufman draws directly on Eliyahu Goldratt's Theory of Constraints: because output is capped at the weakest link, effort spent improving any step that is not the constraint produces no gain in throughput and often just piles up inventory in front of the true limit. The discipline is a sequence: identify the constraint, exploit it (get the most from it without new investment), subordinate everything else to keeping it fed and running, then elevate it (add capacity) if needed — and when it is no longer the limit, find the next one, because there is always a new constraint. A factory line moves at the speed of its slowest machine; adding a second faster machine upstream just grows the queue at the slow one.

## Context & Usage

Use this whenever you want more output from a system and are tempted to improve everything at once. Find the one place where work backs up — the overloaded person, the approval that everything waits on, the sales step where deals stall — and pour your attention there, because an hour saved at the constraint is an hour of throughput gained for the whole system, while an hour saved anywhere else is wasted motion. It is the sharpest antidote to scattershot optimization: before improving a process, ask what is actually limiting it, and resist upgrading non-constraints that only feel productive.
