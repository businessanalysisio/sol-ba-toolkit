# Breakeven

## Overview

Breakeven is the point at which total revenue exactly equals total costs — the business makes neither profit nor loss. Below breakeven you are losing money; above it, each additional sale contributes to profit. Kaufman presents it as a fundamental orientation check: knowing your breakeven tells you the minimum you must sell to survive and how far your current sales sit from safety. In unit terms it is computed as fixed costs divided by the contribution margin per unit (the price of a unit minus its variable cost) — if fixed costs are $10,000 a month and each unit contributes $25 after its variable cost, you break even at 400 units. Breakeven ties the earlier finance models together: fixed and variable costs determine it, and pricing and margin move it. It is one of the first numbers to compute for any new venture or major cost change.

## Context & Usage

Calculate breakeven before launching, before taking on new fixed costs, and when setting sales targets. Example: a café with $8,000 monthly fixed costs and a $4 contribution per cup must sell 2,000 cups a month just to break even — a concrete target that instantly tells the owner whether the location's foot traffic makes the business viable. Use it to test decisions: raising price lifts contribution per unit and lowers the breakeven quantity, while adding fixed costs raises it. If breakeven volume looks unreachable given realistic demand, the model needs to change before, not after, you commit.
