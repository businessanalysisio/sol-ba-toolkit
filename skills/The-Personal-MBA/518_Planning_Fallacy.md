# Planning Fallacy

## Overview

The systematic human tendency — documented by Kahneman and Tversky — to underestimate how long tasks will take and how much they will cost, even when we know that similar past projects overran. The mechanism: we plan from the inside view, mentally simulating the work going smoothly, and fail to budget for the interruptions, dependencies, and surprises that always occur but are individually unpredictable. More people and more interdependent tasks amplify the error, because coordination overhead grows faster than headcount.

## Context & Usage

Assume every estimate you make or receive is optimistic, and correct with the outside view: base forecasts on how long *comparable past projects actually took*, not on how long this one feels like it should take. Practical moves: multiply gut estimates by a historical overrun factor, add explicit buffer to commitments (promise the padded date, work to the raw one), and break big efforts into small deliverables so slippage surfaces early. Example: a "two-week" systems migration that history says runs six — quote six externally, and investigate at week three, not week five.
