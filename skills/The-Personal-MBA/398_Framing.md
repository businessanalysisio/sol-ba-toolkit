# Framing

## Overview

Framing is the choice of which details to emphasize and which context to present, and it changes how the same underlying facts are perceived. Kaufman's canonical illustration is ground beef labeled "90% lean" versus "10% fat" — chemically identical, yet the first framing sells far better because it foregrounds a positive while the second foregrounds a negative. Framing is not deception; it is the unavoidable reality that every message presents information from some angle, and choosing that angle deliberately shapes interpretation and response. Because people react to how information is presented as much as to the information itself, thoughtful framing can make a genuine benefit land with full force or let it fall flat.

## Context & Usage

Apply framing whenever you decide how to present a price, a feature, or a comparison. A gym membership framed as "just $1 a day" feels trivial where "$365 a year" feels steep, though the amounts are equal. A software plan framed as "save 20 hours a month" lands harder than "automates report generation," because it foregrounds the valued outcome. When testing marketing messages, vary the frame — positive versus negative, per-day versus per-year, gain versus loss avoided — and measure which resonates, since the right frame often moves response more than any change in the underlying offer. Keep framing honest; a frame that misrepresents facts destroys trust once discovered.
