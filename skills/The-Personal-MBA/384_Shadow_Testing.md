# Shadow Testing

## Overview

Shadow testing is selling an offer before it actually exists to find out whether real demand is there. Instead of building a product and hoping people buy, you create the appearance of the offer — a sales page, a description, a way to place an order — and see whether people will commit real money or a real order. If they do, you have proof of demand and can then build; if they don't, you've saved yourself the cost of building something no one wanted. Kaufman treats this as the sharpest way to test a Critically Important Assumption about demand, because it measures actual buying behavior rather than opinions. The one ethical rule is that you must honor or cleanly refund every order — a shadow test collects genuine intent, and betraying it is fraud, not research.

## Context & Usage

Run a shadow test before investing in building whenever your riskiest assumption is "will anyone buy this?" A common form is a landing page describing the not-yet-built product with a "Buy Now" button that, on click, explains it's launching soon and offers to notify or pre-order the buyer. If a thousand targeted visitors yield zero attempts to purchase, the market has spoken far more cheaply than a finished product would have. This is the demand-side complement to the prototype, which tests usability rather than willingness to pay.
