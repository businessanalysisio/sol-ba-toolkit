# Five-Fold Why

## Overview

Ask "why do I want this?" of any desire, then ask "why?" of the answer, and repeat about five times: the chain almost always bottoms out in a root desire quite different from the surface want. Kaufman's example is "I want a million dollars" — five whys later it typically resolves to freedom, security, or status, each of which has cheaper and faster routes than a million dollars. Surface wants are strategies; root desires are the actual requirements, and optimizing a strategy without knowing the requirement wastes years.

## Context & Usage

Run the chain on your own goals before committing serious resources — the founder who discovers their "build a big company" want is really "control my own schedule" can stop pursuing venture scale that would destroy the actual goal. It works identically on stakeholders: a client demanding "a mobile app" asked why a few times often reveals the need is "field staff can update orders without a laptop," which a responsive web page satisfies in a quarter of the time. The technique is the personal twin of root-cause analysis: keep digging past the stated request until you hit the thing that, if satisfied, dissolves the request.
