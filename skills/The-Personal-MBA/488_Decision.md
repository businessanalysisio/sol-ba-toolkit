# Decision

## Overview

The word comes from the Latin decidere — "to cut off." A real decision severs the alternatives and commits to a single course; everything short of that cut is deliberation, however decisive it feels. Kaufman's point is that many stalled businesses and careers aren't short on analysis, they're short on the cut: options are kept open indefinitely because closing them feels like loss, and the resulting half-commitments starve every path of the resources any one of them needed.

## Context & Usage

Watch for deliberation masquerading as diligence. If a choice has been "under review" through three meetings with no new information arriving, more analysis is procrastination — set a decision deadline, name who decides, and cut. Distinguish reversible from irreversible calls: a reversible decision (trial pricing, a channel test) deserves speed, since acting generates better information than pondering; an irreversible one (co-founder, brand name, lease) justifies slower deliberation. After the cut, stop relitigating — a decision revisited daily was never actually made, and the reopened alternatives resume their tax on attention.
