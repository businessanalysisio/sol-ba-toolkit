# Feedback Loop

## Overview

A Feedback Loop exists when a system's output is routed back and becomes part of its own input, so the system responds to its own behavior. Kaufman distinguishes two kinds. A reinforcing (positive) loop amplifies whatever direction it starts in — more feeds more, less feeds less — producing exponential growth or collapse: word of mouth that brings customers who generate more word of mouth, or a bank run where withdrawals trigger further withdrawals. A balancing (negative) loop counteracts change and pushes the system toward a target, the way a thermostat cuts heat as the room warms and restores it as the room cools, holding a stock near a set point. Real systems are webs of both, and the surprising, hard-to-predict behavior of a business over time usually comes from loops interacting — often with delays that make cause and effect hard to connect.

## Context & Usage

Look for loops whenever a metric grows or shrinks faster than the inputs seem to justify, or refuses to move no matter how hard you push. If growth is compounding, find the reinforcing loop and protect it; if a number stubbornly returns to the same level after every intervention, find the balancing loop that is resisting you and address that mechanism rather than pushing harder. Deliberately engineering reinforcing loops — referral programs, network effects, reinvested profit — is how durable growth is built, while balancing loops are how you keep a system stable. Watch for delays in the loop, which cause overcorrection and oscillation.
