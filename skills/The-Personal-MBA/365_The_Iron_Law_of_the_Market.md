# The Iron Law of the Market

## Overview

The market always wins. Kaufman's Iron Law states that no amount of talent, effort, funding, or brilliance in execution can overcome the absence of a market that actually wants what you are selling. A mediocre offer aimed at a hungry, paying audience beats a flawless offer aimed at no one, every time. Kaufman treats a nonexistent or indifferent market as the single most common cause of business failure — founders fall in love with a product, build it beautifully, and only afterward discover that too few people care enough to pay. The law reorders the usual startup sequence: before investing in building, you confirm that a group of people already has an urgent, expensive problem and the willingness to spend money solving it.

## Context & Usage

Apply this before committing serious time or capital to any idea. The classic trap is the technically dazzling product with no buyers — a founder who spends two years perfecting software for a workflow no one is desperate to change. The Iron Law says: prove demand first, cheaply, through pre-sales, sign-up interest, or direct conversations with prospects, and only then build. If you cannot find people who want it before it exists, building it will not conjure them into existence.
