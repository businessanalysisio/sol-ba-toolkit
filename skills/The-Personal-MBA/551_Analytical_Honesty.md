# Analytical Honesty

## Overview

Analytical Honesty is the discipline of drawing only the conclusions your data actually supports — and, harder, accepting conclusions you don't like. Kaufman leans on Richard Feynman's warning that the first principle is that you must not fool yourself, and that you are the easiest person to fool. The threat is not usually deliberate deception but motivated reasoning: we go looking for numbers that confirm what we already believe, quietly discard the ones that don't, and stop analyzing the moment the data agrees with us. A founder convinced a product is working will fixate on the one metric that's up and explain away the three that are down. Analytical honesty means holding yourself to the standard of what the evidence shows regardless of what you hoped it would show, and treating a disconfirming result as information rather than an inconvenience to be argued around.

## Context & Usage

Practice this whenever you have a stake in the answer — which is nearly always. Decide in advance what result would change your mind and what would count as failure, so you can't move the goalposts after seeing the data. Actively seek disconfirming evidence rather than only supporting evidence, ask what you'd conclude if a rival had produced these numbers, and be suspicious when analysis conveniently vindicates your preferred plan. It is the interpretive counterpart to Garbage In, Garbage Out (550): honest inputs are wasted if the reading of them is biased. Analytical honesty is what makes KPIs (549) and measurements (548) trustworthy rather than decorative.
