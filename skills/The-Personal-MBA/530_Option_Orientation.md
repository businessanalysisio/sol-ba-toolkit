# Option Orientation

## Overview

When something goes wrong, redirect attention from the problem to the options: instead of dwelling on what happened, who is at fault, or how bad it is, ask "what can we do about it *now*?" Kaufman frames this as the fastest way out of unproductive group states — blame spirals, venting loops, and analysis paralysis all dissolve when the conversation is forced onto available next actions. The past is fixed input; options are the only lever that still moves.

## Context & Usage

Use as a facilitation move the moment a discussion turns retrospective and circular. The key phrase is literal: "Okay — what are our options?" A production outage post-incident call, a lost deal debrief, a blown budget review: in each, allocate a bounded slice to understanding what happened (root cause still matters for prevention), then pivot hard to enumerating moves — including imperfect ones. Listing three or more options also breaks false binaries ("ship late or ship broken") that stress manufactures. Pairs naturally with assigning owners: options without an owner and a next action are just a longer complaint.
