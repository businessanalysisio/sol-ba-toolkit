# Twelve Standard Forms of Value (Product, Service, Shared Resource, Subscription, Resale, Lease, Agency, Audience Aggregation, Loan, Option, Insurance, Capital)

## Overview

Kaufman catalogs twelve standard ways any business can package and monetize the value it creates: a **product** (create something tangible, sell it once), a **service** (provide help or skill for a fee), a **shared resource** (build something expensive once and let many people use it — a gym, a museum), a **subscription** (recurring payment for ongoing value — magazines, SaaS), **resale** (buy wholesale, sell at markup — retail), a **lease** (grant temporary use of an asset you retain — car rental, real estate), **agency** (connect buyer and seller, take a commission — literary agents, brokers), **audience aggregation** (assemble attention and sell access to it — advertising, media), a **loan** (lend money, collect interest), an **option** (charge for the right to do something later — tickets, gift cards), **insurance** (assume a defined risk in exchange for premiums), and **capital** (buy ownership in a business in exchange for a share of profits). Most real businesses combine several forms — a software company may sell a subscription, offer paid services, and aggregate an audience.

## Context & Usage

Use this menu when designing or reinventing how you charge. If your one-time product sales are volatile, the list prompts an obvious question: could this become a subscription, or generate recurring service revenue? A photographer selling shoots (service) might add prints (product), licensing (option), and a members' resource library (subscription) from the same underlying skill. Running an offer through all twelve forms is a fast idea-generation exercise for new revenue lines and often surfaces a more durable model than the one you started with.
