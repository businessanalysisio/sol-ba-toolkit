# Research-question formulation

## Overview

Writing the specific, answerable questions that the analysis will address, each phrased so that a data-derived answer is possible and its usefulness is clear. A good research question names the population, the variables, and the timeframe, avoids embedding a presumed cause, and is scoped to what available data can actually support. Questions typically map to an analytics type — "what is happening" (descriptive), "why" (diagnostic), "what will happen" (predictive), "what should we do" (prescriptive) — which in turn signals the method the analysis will need.

## Context & Usage

The pivotal Identify-domain activity that converts problem and objective into an analytical brief and directly drives sourcing (identify → source → analyze → interpret & report → use). Concrete example: the premium-churn problem becomes questions like "Which premium sub-segments had the largest drop in repeat purchase rate over the last two quarters?" (descriptive) and "Which service and delivery factors most strongly predict premium churn?" (predictive) — each precise enough to tell you exactly which tables to source and which technique to run.
