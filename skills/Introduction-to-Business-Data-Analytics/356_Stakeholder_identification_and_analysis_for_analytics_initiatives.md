# Stakeholder identification and analysis for analytics initiatives

## Overview

Systematically identifying everyone who has a stake in an analytics effort and characterizing each so the work is scoped, sourced, and communicated to fit them. It catalogues sponsors, decision-makers who will act on the findings, data owners and stewards who control the sources, subject-matter experts who validate meaning, and those affected by any resulting change — then assesses each on influence, interest, attitude, and information needs (often via a power/interest grid). This analysis determines who must approve the research questions, who grants data access, and who must be persuaded for results to be adopted.

## Context & Usage

Applied at the start of and throughout an analytics initiative, connecting the Identify domain (whose question is this?) to reporting and use (who acts?) (identify → source → analyze → interpret & report → use). Concrete example: for the churn initiative, stakeholder analysis names the VP of Customer Success as sponsor and decision-maker, the data-platform team as owners of the delivery-events source, operations leads as SMEs for validation, and the fulfillment team as those affected by a new shipping SLA — clarifying early whose sign-off and whose data the effort depends on.
