# Analytics-approach planning (descriptive / diagnostic / predictive / prescriptive)

## Overview

Choosing which of the four analytics types each research question calls for, and therefore what data, methods, and effort the work will require. Descriptive analytics summarizes what happened (aggregates, distributions, trends); diagnostic analytics explains why (drill-down, correlation, root-cause); predictive analytics estimates what is likely (regression, classification, forecasting); prescriptive analytics recommends what to do (optimization, simulation, decision models). The four form a ladder of increasing value and difficulty, and a single initiative often climbs several rungs as questions get sharper.

## Context & Usage

The final Identify-domain activity, bridging into sourcing and analysis by fixing the method before data is acquired (identify → source → analyze → interpret & report → use). Concrete example: for premium churn, "how much did repeat purchases fall?" is planned as descriptive; "why did they fall?" as diagnostic; "who will churn next quarter?" as predictive (a classification model); and "which retention offer maximizes saved revenue per dollar spent?" as prescriptive (an optimization) — each choice determining the data volume and modeling skill needed.
