# Simulation

## Overview

A technique that models a system or process and runs it many times under varying inputs to see the range of outcomes that could result — used to answer "what if" and "what should we do" questions where closed-form calculation is impractical. Monte Carlo simulation draws inputs from probability distributions to produce a distribution of results (with confidence intervals) rather than a single point estimate; discrete-event and agent-based simulations reproduce process dynamics over time. Because it quantifies uncertainty and lets analysts test scenarios and interventions before committing real resources, simulation is a workhorse of prescriptive analytics.

## Context & Usage

An Analyze-domain technique reached for when a decision hinges on uncertainty and interacting variables, feeding directly into option recommendation (identify → source → analyze → interpret & report → use). Concrete example: before choosing a retention action, the churn team simulates expedited premium shipping — sampling delivery-time and cost distributions across thousands of runs — to estimate the range of retained revenue net of added logistics cost, revealing not just an expected gain but the probability the initiative loses money, informing whether it is worth doing.
