# Interpret and Report Results

## Overview

The fourth domain of the lifecycle: turning analytical output into meaning a business audience can act on. It covers deriving insights (separating signal from noise, stating what the numbers imply and how confident that implication is), assessing stakeholder communication needs, choosing visualizations that fit the message, and constructing a data-driven narrative that links finding to consequence to recommended action. Interpretation is where a coefficient or cluster becomes a business statement — and where the analyst must be explicit about assumptions, limitations, and uncertainty so decisions are not built on overstated results.

## Context & Usage

Sits fourth in the cycle (identify → source → analyze → interpret & report → use), taking validated analysis results and preparing them for the people who will decide. Concrete example: the regression showed delivery delays over three days roughly double churn probability in the premium segment. Interpretation translates that into "our slowest-shipping segment is also our most valuable and most at-risk," pairs it with a chart contrasting churn against delivery time, and frames the recommendation — rather than dumping the model's raw coefficients on an executive audience.
