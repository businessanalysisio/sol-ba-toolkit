# Recommending solution options from analytics results

## Overview

Translating what the analysis found into a set of actionable options a decision-maker can choose among, each weighed on expected value, cost, feasibility, risk, and time to impact. Rather than presenting a single answer, good practice frames two or more viable courses of action, traces each explicitly back to the evidence that supports it, states its assumptions and downsides, and often recommends one while explaining why. This is where analytical insight becomes a business proposal — the bridge from "here is what the data says" to "here is what I suggest we do about it."

## Context & Usage

The opening activity of the Use domain, consuming the reported findings and feeding implementation planning (identify → source → analyze → interpret & report → use). Concrete example: from the churn finding, the analyst presents three options — expedite premium-segment shipping, proactively credit customers on delayed orders, or renegotiate the slow carrier — each with modeled retained-revenue upside, cost, and risk (drawing on the simulation), and recommends expedited shipping as the best value-to-effort option while noting its logistics-capacity dependency.
