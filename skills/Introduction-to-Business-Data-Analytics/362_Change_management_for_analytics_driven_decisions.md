# Change management for analytics-driven decisions

## Overview

Managing the human and organizational side of acting on analytics so that a data-supported decision is actually adopted and sustained rather than resisted or quietly ignored. It addresses communicating the rationale and the evidence, engaging the people whose work will change, building trust in the data and the method (especially where findings challenge intuition or established practice), providing training and support, and reinforcing new behaviors until they stick. Analytics initiatives fail less often on the math than on adoption — this discipline is what converts a sound recommendation into a durable change in how the business operates.

## Context & Usage

A Use-domain discipline that runs in parallel with implementation and often loops back as monitoring reveals adoption gaps (identify → source → analyze → interpret & report → use). Concrete example: rolling out expedited premium shipping means the fulfillment team must trust that the delay-churn link is real, not a spreadsheet artifact; change management here means walking them through the evidence, addressing their concern that speed will hurt accuracy, adjusting incentives to the new SLA, and tracking compliance — so the operational change endures beyond the launch memo.
