# Identify the Research Questions

## Overview

The first domain of business data analytics: framing the business problem or opportunity as a set of specific, answerable research questions before any data is touched. It covers defining the problem, assessing the current state, describing a measurable future state, formulating questions, and planning the analytics approach (descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, or prescriptive). A well-framed research question anchors scope, guides data sourcing, and defines what a "useful answer" looks like.

## Context & Usage

Applied at the very start of the analytics lifecycle — it is the entry point of the cycle (identify research questions → source data → analyze → interpret & report → use results operationally). A business data analyst uses this domain when stakeholders present symptoms ("sales are down") rather than analyzable questions ("which customer segments reduced repeat purchases in the last two quarters, and why?"). Getting this right prevents the most expensive analytics failure: answering the wrong question precisely.
