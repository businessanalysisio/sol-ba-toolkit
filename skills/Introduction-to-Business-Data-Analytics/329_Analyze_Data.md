# Analyze Data

## Overview

The third domain of the lifecycle: applying analytical methods to prepared data to produce findings that answer the research questions. It runs across the four analytics types — descriptive (what happened), diagnostic (why it happened), predictive (what is likely to happen), and prescriptive (what to do about it) — and covers building an analysis plan, preparing data through joins, cleansing, normalization and weighting, exploring it, and selecting techniques such as regression, clustering, classification trees, association rules, or simulation. It also demands assessing integrity, validity, reliability, and bias so conclusions are defensible.

## Context & Usage

Sits third in the cycle (identify → source → analyze → interpret & report → use), consuming staged data from sourcing and feeding results into interpretation. Concrete example: for the repeat-purchase question, analysis might segment customers with k-means clustering, run a regression to identify which factors (discount frequency, delivery delay, category) most predict churn, then validate the model on a holdout period before any finding is reported — the analyst is answering "why" and "what is likely," not just describing volumes.
