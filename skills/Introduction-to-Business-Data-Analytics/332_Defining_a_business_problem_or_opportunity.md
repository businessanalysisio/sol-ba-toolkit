# Defining a business problem or opportunity

## Overview

The opening activity within the Identify domain: articulating, in business terms, the gap or upside that makes analytics worth doing before any question is formulated. It means separating a symptom from the underlying problem, stating who is affected and what the cost of inaction is, and distinguishing a problem (something is going wrong that must be fixed) from an opportunity (something could be gained that is not being captured). A crisp problem statement — the situation, its impact, and the desired change — is the seed from which measurable objectives and research questions grow.

## Context & Usage

The first step of the first lifecycle domain (identify → source → analyze → interpret & report → use). Concrete example: leadership says "our churn is too high." Defining the problem sharpens that into "premium customers are churning at 18% quarterly versus a 10% target, costing an estimated $2M in annual recurring revenue, and we do not know which factors drive it" — a statement with scope, magnitude, and a stated unknown, which is analyzable, versus a vague complaint, which is not.
