# Stakeholder analysis

## Overview

Carkenord treats stakeholder analysis as the entry ticket to "know your audience": before any elicitation, build a stakeholder list that captures, per person or group, their role on the project (sponsor, SME, product owner, developer, tester, vendor, end customer), their department and physical location, their availability, their attitude toward the project (champion, neutral, threatened), and — critically — their authority over requirements decisions. Then decide two things for each: how much involvement you need from them (approve, contribute, review, inform) and which elicitation and communication approach fits them. The list is never finished; every session tends to surface a name nobody mentioned, and an unfound stakeholder discovered at UAT is the classic source of late "new" requirements.

## Context & Usage

Do it in the first days of an initiative and revisit at every phase gate or scope change. The immediate consumers are the elicitation plan (who to interview, who belongs in workshops) and the communication plan (who gets which deliverable view). Watch for stakeholders defined only as departments — "Finance" cannot answer a question; a named person in Finance can. Also distinguish the person who will use the solution from the manager who speaks for them: you usually need both.
