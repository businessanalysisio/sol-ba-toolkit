# Active listening

## Overview

Listening as deliberate analytical work rather than passive reception: paraphrasing what the stakeholder said and playing it back for confirmation, summarizing at intervals, withholding judgment and solutions until the speaker has finished, and noticing what is conspicuously *not* said. Carkenord highlights it because elicitation quality is capped by listening quality — a BA who is mentally drafting the next question, or already designing the solution, hears only fragments. The paraphrase step doubles as validation: "so an order can never ship before payment clears — did I get that right?" surfaces misunderstandings in seconds instead of at UAT.

## Context & Usage

Core to interviews (274) and facilitated sessions (277), but equally relevant in hallway conversations where requirements often first appear. Balance note-taking against engagement — a head buried in a laptop kills the non-verbal channel; consider a scribe for important sessions so you can stay fully present. When a stakeholder rambles, active listening is also the polite steering mechanism: summarize the relevant thread and ask the next question about it.
