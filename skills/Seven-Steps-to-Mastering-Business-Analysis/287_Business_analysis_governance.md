# Business analysis governance

## Overview

Governance answers the questions that turn requirements from opinions into commitments: who has authority to approve each class of requirement (the sponsor for business requirements, product owner for stories, architect for technical constraints — and named individuals, not roles-in-the-abstract), through what mechanism changes are decided after baseline (change control board, product-owner triage, or sponsor fiat — any of these works; "whoever emails the BA last" does not), who holds prioritization decision rights when stakeholders deadlock, where the escalation path leads and how fast it moves, and what standards deliverables must meet before review. Without these agreements, sign-off is theater — the executive who "approved" the BRD relitigates it at UAT because nothing established that approval bound anyone. Carkenord's angle is preventive: negotiate the decision rules while everyone is still friendly, because inventing a change process during the first scope fight means the process itself becomes the fight.

## Context & Usage

Establish during planning (286), record in a page the sponsor endorses, and then actually enforce it — a governance model that bends for the first executive who bypasses it teaches everyone to bypass it. Scale to context: a heavyweight CCB on a two-week enhancement is bureaucracy, no change control on a regulatory program is malpractice. The BA typically administers the machinery (impact analysis for each change request, decision log, requirement status tracking) while pointedly not owning the decisions themselves.
