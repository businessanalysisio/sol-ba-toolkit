# Elicitation technique: Facilitated Sessions

## Overview

Putting the stakeholders who disagree in one room and working the disagreement live — the JAD-style workshop that compresses weeks of serial interviews and email reconciliation into hours, because conflicts surface and get resolved in front of everyone instead of being discovered later between interview notes. What separates a facilitated session from a meeting is engineering: a stated objective and agenda circulated in advance, ground rules agreed at the start, the right people (decision-makers or their delegated authority, not just messengers), a neutral facilitator who owns the process while staying out of the content, a scribe so the facilitator never turns their back to write, and a parking lot for issues that would hijack the agenda. Carkenord treats facilitation as a first-rank BA skill precisely because the failure modes — the loud architect dominating, the silent SME agreeing by exhaustion, scope debates disguised as wording debates — are all recoverable with technique: round-robins, breakout pairs, timeboxed decisions, and naming the impasse out loud.

## Context & Usage

The best tool for scope definition, cross-functional process design, prioritization, and requirement conflicts that shuttle diplomacy has failed to resolve. Expensive per hour of stakeholder time, so reserve it for topics needing group agreement and do discovery beforehand by interview and document analysis — a workshop that starts from a strawman moves three times faster than one starting from a blank wall. Send the documented decisions and open items to participants within a day.
