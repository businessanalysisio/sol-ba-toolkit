# Working with dispersed/distributed teams (time zone, cultural, and language differences)

## Overview

Distance strips out the non-verbal channel that BAs rely on, so distributed elicitation must compensate deliberately. Carkenord's practical adjustments: rotate meeting times so the pain of odd hours is shared rather than always landing on one region; follow every verbal session with a written summary for confirmation, because "yes" on a call may mean "I heard you," not "I agree" — in some cultures disagreeing with authority out loud simply is not done; lean on visual models (process flows, context diagrams, prototypes) that cross language barriers better than prose; and speak slowly, avoid idioms, and check understanding by asking people to restate decisions rather than asking "any questions?". Budget more elapsed time per review cycle — a question that takes two minutes across a desk takes a day across twelve time zones.

## Context & Usage

Applies to offshore development, multi-country rollouts, vendor teams, and post-merger projects. Front-load relationship building: one early face-to-face or camera-on working session measurably changes how honestly remote stakeholders answer later. When requirements pass through a local coordinator to offshore developers, insist on direct access for clarification questions — every relay layer is a defect generator. Document more formally than you would co-located; hallway repair of misunderstandings is not available.
