# Elicitation technique: Observation

## Overview

Sitting beside a worker while they do the real job, because people cannot accurately describe their own work: they forget steps that have become muscle memory, omit the workarounds (the sticky notes, the shadow spreadsheet, the re-keying between systems) that are precisely the improvement opportunities, and describe the official process rather than the one they perform. Carkenord distinguishes passive observation — watch silently, save questions for afterwards, minimum disruption — from active observation, where you interrupt to ask "why did you do that?" the moment something surprises you, trading realism for insight. Either way, expect the Hawthorne effect: watched people follow rules they normally skip, so observe multiple people, at busy and quiet times, and treat the first hour of any session as theater.

## Context & Usage

Strongest for high-volume, repeatable operational work — order entry, claims processing, call centers — and weak for knowledge work whose substance is invisible thinking. Use it to verify what interviews and documents claim: the triangle of "what the manual says, what the SME says, what you saw" locates the truth. Ask permission properly and say why you are there; a BA mistaken for a time-and-motion auditor poisons the whole department. Capture volumes, timings, interruptions, and exception frequency — numbers you cannot get by asking.
