# Probing/questioning discipline (asking good, detailed follow-up questions)

## Overview

First answers are rarely complete answers, so the craft lies in the follow-up: "what happens if the customer cancels after shipping?", "who else touches this record?", "always, or are there exceptions?". The discipline combines question types (open questions to explore, closed questions to confirm, "why" chains to reach root cause) with preparation — arriving with a structured question set per topic — and the flexibility to abandon the script when an answer opens a more important thread. Carkenord treats this as the engine of elicitation: probing is how implicit rules, exceptions, and workarounds become explicit requirements instead of production defects.

## Context & Usage

Deploy in every elicitation encounter, but calibrate: relentless "why?" can feel like interrogation, so soften phrasing ("help me understand what drives that") and explain that the detail protects the stakeholder's own outcome. Probe hardest around boundaries and exceptions — the happy path is usually well known; the edge cases are where analysis earns its keep. Note unanswered probes explicitly as open questions rather than letting them evaporate.
