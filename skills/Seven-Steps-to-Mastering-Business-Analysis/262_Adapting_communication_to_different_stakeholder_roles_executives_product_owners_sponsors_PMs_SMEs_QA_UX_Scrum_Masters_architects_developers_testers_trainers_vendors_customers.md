# Adapting communication to different stakeholder roles (executives, product owners, sponsors, PMs, SMEs, QA, UX, Scrum Masters, architects, developers, testers, trainers, vendors, customers)

## Overview

The heart of Carkenord's "know your audience" step: each role hears the same requirement through a different filter, so the BA re-packages the message — vocabulary, level of detail, medium, and formality — per recipient. An executive sponsor gets the business impact in two minutes and one page; a developer gets unambiguous rules, data definitions, and edge cases; a tester gets verifiable acceptance conditions; an SME gets their operational reality reflected back in their own terms; a vendor gets contractual precision. Concretely this means maintaining one requirements repository but generating several views of it: an objectives summary for the sponsor, use cases or stories for the build team, scenarios for QA, process flows for trainers. Sending everyone the same 80-page document is not communication — it guarantees that nobody reads the part meant for them.

## Context & Usage

Decide the packaging whenever you schedule an elicitation session, circulate a deliverable for review, or report status: ask first "what does this person need in order to act, and what will they ignore?" It matters most at review and sign-off time, when a mismatch (asking executives to approve field-level detail, or handing developers vision statements) stalls approval. Watch for role-specific danger zones Carkenord flags — developers who want design decisions early, PMs who want dates before scope is stable, SMEs who describe the current system as the requirement.
