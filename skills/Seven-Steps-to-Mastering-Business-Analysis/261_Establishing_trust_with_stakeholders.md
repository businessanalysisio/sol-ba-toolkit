# Establishing trust with stakeholders

## Overview

Candid requirements only flow across a trusted relationship: stakeholders will not admit workarounds, political constraints, or their own knowledge gaps to an analyst they see as an auditor or as management's messenger. Trust is built through small, repeated proofs — doing what you said by when you said it, sending accurate meeting notes, protecting sensitive remarks shared in confidence, admitting what you don't know, and being honest when a requested feature is unlikely to survive prioritization rather than nodding it into the backlog. Carkenord treats it as the precondition for every elicitation technique: the technique gets you the meeting; trust gets you the truth.

## Context & Usage

Invest earliest and heaviest in stakeholders whose cooperation the project cannot survive without, and in those who show signs of skepticism from prior failed projects. Trust also carries a boundary obligation: never weaponize what a stakeholder confided, and never promise scope to win rapport — a friendly BA who overcommits destroys more trust than a candid one who says "I'll take it to prioritization, and it may not make the cut."
