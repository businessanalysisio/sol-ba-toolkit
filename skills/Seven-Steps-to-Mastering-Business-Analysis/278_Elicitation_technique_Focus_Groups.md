# Elicitation technique: Focus Groups

## Overview

Borrowed from market research: a moderated conversation among roughly six to twelve pre-qualified participants — customers, prospective users, or a specific user segment — following a prepared script of prompts, run to capture attitudes, perceptions, and reactions rather than to make decisions. That distinction is what separates it from a facilitated session (277): a workshop produces agreed requirements; a focus group produces raw opinion data that the BA analyzes afterwards for themes. Composition drives honesty — keep each session homogeneous (do not mix managers with their staff, or expert users with novices, because one silences the other) and run several sessions to see which reactions repeat. The moderator draws out quiet participants, blocks dominant ones, and probes reactions ("you frowned at that screen — say more") without ever defending the product under discussion.

## Context & Usage

Best when the voice you need belongs to people outside the project — evaluating a product concept, testing reactions to a prototype or pricing change, understanding why customers leave. Weak for precision: group settings breed performative agreement, and what people say they would do diverges famously from what they do, so pair focus-group findings with behavioral data or observation before betting requirements on them. Record (with consent), transcribe, and code the themes; the analysis is where the value is created.
