# Strategic planning awareness (portfolio/program management, enterprise/business/information/technology architecture)

## Overview

A project is one move in a larger game, and Carkenord expects the BA to know the board: portfolio management ranks all candidate investments against strategy and decides which get funded (which explains why your project can be paused for reasons that have nothing to do with its merits); program management coordinates related projects toward one outcome, meaning some of "your" requirements are actually owned by a sibling project; and enterprise architecture describes the organization in linked layers — business architecture (capabilities and processes), information architecture (what data exists and who masters it), and technology architecture (platforms and standards). The BA uses these artifacts as inputs, not decoration: check proposed requirements against architectural standards, reuse canonical data definitions instead of inventing new ones, and flag solution options that fight the target architecture.

## Context & Usage

Consult portfolio and architecture context during initiation and again at solution-option evaluation. It is the fastest route to spotting duplicated effort ("another program is already building that interface") and doomed options ("that platform is being retired"). For a BA joining a large enterprise, an hour with an enterprise architect yields more usable constraints than a week of interviews. If no formal architecture exists, the strategic plan and the IT roadmap are the poor-man's substitute.
