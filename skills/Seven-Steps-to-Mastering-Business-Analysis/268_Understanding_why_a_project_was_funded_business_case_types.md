# Understanding why a project was funded (business case types)

## Overview

Carkenord insists the first question on any assignment is "why did someone write a check for this?" — and she groups the answers into recurring business case types: increase revenue, decrease cost, comply with a regulation or mandate, improve customer service or satisfaction, and replace aging or unsupported technology. The type governs everything downstream. On a compliance project the deadline and the regulator's wording are the requirements backbone and gold-plating is waste; on a revenue project time-to-market can beat completeness; on a cost-reduction project every requirement should trace to hours or dollars saved. Ask to read the actual business case; if none exists or it is a slogan ("modernize the platform"), reconstruct the justification with the sponsor before eliciting a single detailed requirement, because you cannot prioritize against a reason you do not know.

## Context & Usage

Do this before kickoff and reread the business case whenever scope disputes arise — it is the tiebreaker for competing stakeholder needs (264) and the yardstick for change requests. Be alert to funded-reason drift: projects approved for reason A quietly become vehicles for reason B, and the requirements set splits along that fault line. If the measurable benefit in the case cannot survive scrutiny, raise it early; the BA is often the first person to notice a business case that does not add up.
