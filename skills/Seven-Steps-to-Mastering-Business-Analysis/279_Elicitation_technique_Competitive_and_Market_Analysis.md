# Elicitation technique: Competitive and Market Analysis

## Overview

Requirements do not all come from inside the building: when a solution faces external customers, what competitors offer is itself a requirements source. The method is systematic comparison — inventory competitor products feature by feature (their websites, demos, trial accounts, user reviews, analyst reports, trade press), track market trends and entrants, and sort findings into two piles Carkenord considers strategically distinct: parity features, which customers assume and whose absence loses deals (these become baseline requirements almost automatically), and differentiators, where the organization chooses to win (these deserve the innovation budget). Benchmarking extends the same logic to practices and performance levels — if competitors ship orders in two days, "acceptable fulfillment time" has been defined for you by the market, not by internal stakeholders.

## Context & Usage

Use for customer-facing product decisions, build-versus-buy evaluations, and business cases that must argue "we are falling behind." Pair with public-materials research (271) and feed results into prioritization — a feature matrix showing three competitors already have something ends debates that internal opinion cannot. Two cautions: copying a competitor's feature without understanding the need behind it imports their mistakes, and everything must be gathered ethically from public sources — a line the BA never crosses.
