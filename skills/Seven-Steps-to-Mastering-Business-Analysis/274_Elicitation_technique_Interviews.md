# Elicitation technique: Interviews

## Overview

The workhorse elicitation technique, and Carkenord breaks it into a prepare / conduct / confirm cycle. Prepare: research the interviewee's role and history with the project, decide structured (fixed question script, comparable answers across interviewees) versus unstructured (themes only, follow where the conversation leads), and sequence questions from broad to specific. Conduct: open with purpose and how notes will be used; run mostly open questions ("walk me through what happens when an order fails") to discover, then closed questions ("can a customer have two active contracts?") to pin facts; probe with follow-ups instead of accepting first answers; listen more than you talk. Confirm: write up the notes within a day and send them back for correction — the interview is not evidence until the interviewee has validated your record of it. One-on-one format buys candor that groups suppress, at the price of hearing one perspective at a time.

## Context & Usage

Use for depth with individuals — sensitive topics, expert knowledge, executives who will not attend workshops — and to reconcile conflicts found by other techniques. Its serial nature is its weakness: ten interviews yield ten partly contradictory views that a facilitated session (277) would have reconciled live, so plan interviews as input to group confirmation, not as a substitute for it. Two BAs per important interview (one asks, one scribes) protects the listening.
