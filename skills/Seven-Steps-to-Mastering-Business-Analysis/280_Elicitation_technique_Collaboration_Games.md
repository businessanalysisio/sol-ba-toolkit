# Elicitation technique: Collaboration Games

## Overview

Structured play that gets past the answers stakeholders think they are supposed to give. Classic examples: Product Box — teams design the retail packaging for the solution, and the benefits they print on the box are the value proposition they actually care about; Speedboat/Sailboat — anchors dragging the boat are pains with the current state, wind in the sails is what must be preserved; buy-a-feature — participants spend a limited play budget across candidate features, turning polite "everything is important" into forced-choice prioritization; affinity mapping — silent brainstorming on stickies, then group clustering, which gives introverts equal airtime. Games work because the artifact absorbs the risk: criticizing "the anchor on the poster" is safe in a way that criticizing the sponsor's process is not, and hierarchy flattens when everyone is holding a marker. The non-negotiable step is the debrief — walking the artifacts and converting them into recorded needs, pains, and priorities — without which the session was team-building, not elicitation.

## Context & Usage

Insert into facilitated sessions (277) when energy is low, when the group is stuck in solution-speak, when participants do not know each other, or when the topic (frustrations with the current system) needs licensed candor. Match game to goal — divergent games to generate, convergent games like buy-a-feature to decide — and brief the rules crisply; a confused game is worse than none. Expect skepticism from formal cultures: name the purpose up front and keep it short.
