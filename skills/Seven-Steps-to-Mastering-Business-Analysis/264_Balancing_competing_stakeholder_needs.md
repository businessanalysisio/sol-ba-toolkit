# Balancing competing stakeholder needs

## Overview

When Sales wants speed, Compliance wants controls, and Operations wants nothing to change, the BA's arbitration tool is not diplomacy but traceability: pull each competing demand back to the business objective in the business case and ask which one actually advances it. Carkenord's core move is separating positions from underlying interests — "we must keep the fax workflow" usually decodes to "we cannot lose the audit trail," which the new solution can satisfy differently. Where a genuine trade-off remains, the BA does not pick the winner; the BA frames options with cost, benefit, and risk for each and takes them to the sponsor or a structured prioritization session, so the decision is made by people with the authority to own it and is recorded with its rationale.

## Context & Usage

Constant background work, but it peaks during prioritization, scope negotiation, and conflicting review feedback on the same requirement. Run MoSCoW or ranked prioritization with the conflicting parties in one room rather than shuttling between them — shuttle diplomacy lets each side believe they won. A warning sign Carkenord flags: a requirements set where every stakeholder got everything is a scope, budget, and timeline problem wearing a consensus costume.
