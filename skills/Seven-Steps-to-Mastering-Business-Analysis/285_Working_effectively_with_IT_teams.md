# Working effectively with IT teams

## Overview

Developers, architects, DBAs, and testers are stakeholders too, and Carkenord's prescription for them inverts the usual handoff model: requirements transfer is a conversation, not a document thrown over a wall. Concretely — bring architects and senior developers into elicitation early, both for feasibility reality-checks and because implementers who heard the business's pain firsthand build better solutions than ones decoding prose; always deliver the why alongside the what, since a developer who knows the purpose can fill small gaps sensibly while one who does not fills them arbitrarily; learn the team's methodology and ceremonies so requirements arrive in the shape and rhythm they consume (stories groomed before sprint planning beat a BRD delivered mid-sprint); and treat their constraints as requirements-grade facts rather than obstruction. Respect runs on competence signals: keeping commitments, clean unambiguous specifications, and technical literacy (284) buy the BA a seat in design discussions.

## Context & Usage

Daily during build phases; decisive at the requirements-to-design transition and during defect triage, where "is it a bug or a requirement gap?" gets negotiated. Watch two developer patterns Carkenord flags: solutioning during elicitation (capture the idea, park it, re-anchor on the need) and the quiet assumption — a developer who stops asking questions is not out of questions, they are answering them alone. Regular informal contact, not just ceremonies, is what surfaces those silent decisions in time.
