# Curiosity / continuous learning orientation

## Overview

Carkenord treats habitual "why?" asking as the trait that separates strong BAs from requirement scribes: every project drops the analyst into an unfamiliar domain, and genuine interest in how the business actually works is what drives digging beneath the first answer. The skill has two halves — in-the-moment inquisitiveness (wondering why a process step exists, why a rule has an exception, why two departments do the same task differently) and a career-long learning habit (new industries, techniques, and technologies). A BA who stops learning starts recycling yesterday's solutions onto today's problems.

## Context & Usage

Most visible at the start of an engagement, when nothing about the domain can be assumed, but it should never switch off — stale assumptions from a previous project are a common source of wrong requirements. Cultivate it deliberately: read the industry press for the sector you serve, ask SMEs to walk you through work you don't understand yet, and treat every "that's just how we do it" as an invitation to investigate rather than a closed door.
