# Detail orientation (holding the big picture and the details simultaneously)

## Overview

Requirements fail in the fine print — a date format, a rounding rule, an exception path nobody mentioned — so the BA must be able to descend to field-level precision without losing the thread of why the project exists. Carkenord frames this as a dual-focus discipline rather than mere fussiness: the analyst is often the only person on the team tracking both altitudes at once, since sponsors think in outcomes and developers think in specifics. The practical test is catching contradictions across levels, such as a detailed business rule that quietly undermines a stated project objective.

## Context & Usage

Apply hardest during requirements documentation and review, where vague words ("timely", "user-friendly", "etc.") must be hunted down and replaced with testable statements. Also apply when decomposing scope: functional decomposition only works if each level stays consistent with the one above it. Watch the failure mode of perfectionism on low-value details while high-risk areas stay shallow — let risk decide where the microscope goes.
