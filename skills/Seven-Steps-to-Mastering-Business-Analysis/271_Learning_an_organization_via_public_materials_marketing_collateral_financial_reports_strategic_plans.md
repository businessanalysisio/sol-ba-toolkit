# Learning an organization via public materials (marketing collateral, financial reports, strategic plans)

## Overview

Before spending a minute of stakeholder time, Carkenord sends the BA to sources that cost nothing: the annual report or 10-K (how the company actually makes money, segment by segment, and what management lists as risks), the website and marketing collateral (how it wants customers to see it — and the vocabulary it uses for its own products), press releases (recent acquisitions, launches, leadership changes), the published mission and strategic plan (what "alignment" will be measured against), and industry analyst or trade-press coverage (how outsiders rate it against competitors). Twenty minutes with the revenue breakdown tells you which business unit's requirements will win a priority fight. The reading also arms elicitation: interview questions that use the organization's own product names and strategy language get taken seriously; generic ones get generic answers.

## Context & Usage

Standard homework when joining a new client, employer, or domain, and worth refreshing annually even in a long engagement — strategies shift. Note the gap between public story and internal reality when you find it; the distance between the two is often exactly where the project sits. For non-public organizations, substitute internal newsletters, intranet strategy pages, and regulatory filings. Treat marketing claims as aspiration, financial statements as fact.
