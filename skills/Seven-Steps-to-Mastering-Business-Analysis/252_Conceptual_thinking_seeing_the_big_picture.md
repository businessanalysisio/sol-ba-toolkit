# Conceptual thinking (seeing the big picture)

## Overview

The ability to step back from a pile of individual stakeholder statements and see the pattern they form: how a requested screen change connects to a business process, how that process serves an enterprise goal, and where the project's scope boundary really sits. Carkenord singles this out because stakeholders naturally speak in fragments — a field here, a report there — and someone must assemble the fragments into a coherent whole before anyone can judge completeness. Conceptual thinkers also spot when two seemingly unrelated requests are actually the same underlying need expressed by different departments.

## Context & Usage

Use it when framing scope at project start, when synthesizing elicitation results into models, and whenever a stakeholder request seems disconnected from the stated business objective — the disconnect is either a scope problem or a missed insight. It pairs with, and pulls against, detail orientation (253); mature BAs consciously shift altitude between the two rather than living at one level.
