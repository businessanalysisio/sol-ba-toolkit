# Verbal and non-verbal communication

## Overview

Two channels operate in every elicitation conversation, and the BA must manage both: the words chosen (plain business language, no unexplained acronyms, questions phrased without leading the witness) and everything around the words — tone, pace, posture, eye contact, silence. Carkenord stresses reading the non-verbal channel in stakeholders: crossed arms after a scope statement, hesitation before agreeing, a glance between two SMEs that says a rule is contested. Just as important is congruence in your own signals — impatient body language while saying "take your time" teaches stakeholders to stop talking.

## Context & Usage

Most consequential in interviews and workshops, where a missed non-verbal cue means a missed requirement or an unspoken objection that resurfaces at sign-off. Remote work strips much of this channel away, so on video calls compensate deliberately: watch faces rather than your notes, name what you think you observed ("you seem unsure about that rule — is it?"), and follow up one-on-one when someone goes quiet.
