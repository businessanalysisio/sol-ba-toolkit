# Navigating organizational politics

## Overview

Every project redistributes something — budget, headcount, control over a process, visibility with executives — so some stakeholders rationally oppose it even while nodding in meetings. Carkenord's guidance: map the informal power structure alongside the org chart (who actually gets decisions made, whose opinion the sponsor asks for last), identify who loses if the project succeeds, and expect their resistance to arrive disguised as scheduling problems, endless review comments, or "additional requirements." The BA's protection is deliberate neutrality: advocate for the business objective, never for a faction; keep analysis evidence-based so recommendations are attackable only on facts; route genuinely political conflicts to the sponsor for air cover instead of trying to out-maneuver a director. And never carry gossip between camps — trust, once spent, does not come back at par.

## Context & Usage

Most acute on projects that centralize, automate, or outsource work, and during vendor selection where internal teams compete with external options. Read the politics during stakeholder analysis, before the first workshop: a threatened SME handled with respect early becomes an ally; discovered late, a saboteur. If you find the project's stated objective and its political objective diverge, that is a conversation with the sponsor, not something to quietly absorb into requirements.
