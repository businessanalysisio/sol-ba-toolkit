# Organizing requirements on iterative/incremental projects

## Overview

When delivery is sliced into iterations, the monolithic BRD gives way to a living backlog — and the organizing principles invert. Slice by user-valued feature, not by document chapter: an increment ships "customer can reset password end-to-end," never "all the validation rules." Elaborate progressively: items near the top of the backlog carry full detail and acceptance criteria (a working definition of ready), items further down stay coarse epics on purpose, because detailing work that may be cut or reshaped is inventory that spoils. The BA's distinctive iterative-era job is keeping the connective tissue that a pile of stories loses: a big-picture view (context diagram, feature map, lightweight domain model) that shows how slices compose, traceability from each story up to its epic and business objective so the backlog cannot silently drift off-case, and the cross-cutting rules and non-functional requirements that belong to no single story yet constrain all of them. Carkenord's warning for BAs raised on waterfall: "we'll detail it when we get there" is discipline, not procrastination — but only if someone is guarding the whole while everyone else works the parts.

## Context & Usage

The operating mode on Scrum, Kanban, and any phased or MVP-first delivery. Run analysis one step ahead of build — refining iteration n+1's items while n is in flight — and use refinement sessions as the recurring elicitation venue. Watch two failure patterns: the backlog as junk drawer (hundreds of stale unranked items; prune ruthlessly) and increment-local thinking that optimizes each slice while the seams between slices — shared data, sequencing dependencies — go unowned. Prioritization is continuous here, not a one-time workshop.
