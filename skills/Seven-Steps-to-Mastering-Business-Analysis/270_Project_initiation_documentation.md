# Project initiation documentation

## Overview

The initiation package — charter, statement of purpose, scope statement, business case reference, initial assumptions, constraints, and risks, plus a named sponsor — is the BA's contract with reality, and Carkenord has BAs both help draft it and audit it. The audit checklist: are the objectives measurable (a number and a date, not "improve efficiency")? Is scope stated as both in-scope AND out-of-scope lists — the out-of-scope list being the one that prevents arguments later? Is the sponsor a person with budget authority, or a committee (a committee sponsor is a risk to log)? Do the constraints distinguish real ones (regulatory deadline) from preferences wearing constraint costumes (a favored vendor)? A weak charter is not a formality problem: every ambiguity in it resurfaces months later as a scope dispute the BA must referee without a text to cite.

## Context & Usage

Engage during or immediately after project approval, before detailed elicitation begins. Use a context diagram (275) to make the scope statement visual and force the "is this interface in or out?" conversations early. When you inherit a running project with no initiation documents, writing a one-page retroactive scope statement and getting the sponsor to sign it is usually the highest-value week you will spend. Keep the charter visible — it is the reference document for every subsequent change discussion.
