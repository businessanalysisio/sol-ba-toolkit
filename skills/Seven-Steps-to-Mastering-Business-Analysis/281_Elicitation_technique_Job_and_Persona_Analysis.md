# Elicitation technique: Job and Persona Analysis

## Overview

Two lenses on the same question — who will actually use this? Job analysis decomposes a role into its tasks, decision points, information needs, tools, and required competencies, usually from job descriptions plus interviews and observation; it tells you what the solution must let this role accomplish and what training the transition will require. Persona analysis compresses a user class into a fictional but evidence-based archetype — a name, a photo, goals, skill level, environment, and frustrations ("Marta, branch teller, 11 years' tenure, keyboard-only, judged on queue length") — that the team can design and argue for when real users cannot be in the room. The persona's power is anti-abstraction: "would Marta find this in a submenu?" is a decidable question where "is this user-friendly?" is not. Personas also expose coverage gaps — writing them forces the discovery that "the user" is actually four different jobs with conflicting needs.

## Context & Usage

Use when the user population is large, external, or unavailable for direct elicitation, and early in design so UX and requirements share the same archetypes. Ground every persona in data (interviews, observation, support tickets, analytics); an invented persona just launders the team's assumptions into a character. In agile settings the personas slot straight into story form — "as Marta, I want..." — and job analysis feeds transition requirements (289) such as retraining and role redesign.
