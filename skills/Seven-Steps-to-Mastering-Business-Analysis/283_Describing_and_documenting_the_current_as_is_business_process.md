# Describing and documenting the current (as-is) business process

## Overview

You cannot measure a gap from a state you never captured. The as-is record baselines today's operation — actors and roles, triggering events, the step sequence, the business rules applied at each decision, the exceptions and their frequency, the systems and forms touched, and the volumes and cycle times that make it quantitative — so that gap analysis, impact assessment, and the business case's "before" numbers all have something to stand on. Carkenord pairs the technique with a warning she considers as important as the technique itself: the current state is a bottomless pit. Analysis paralysis lives here — teams that spend six months lovingly documenting a process the project exists to abolish. Her rule of proportionality: detail only what the change will touch, sketch the rest, and time-box the whole exercise with an explicit exit criterion ("enough to identify impacted roles and interfaces") agreed before starting.

## Context & Usage

Essential for process-improvement and system-replacement projects, for regulatory work where today's controls must be evidenced, and for transition planning — training and cutover requirements are all deltas from the as-is. Cross-verify sources, because three versions of the process always coexist: the documented one, the described one, and the observed one; where they differ, the observed one is running the business. Explicitly capture why odd steps exist — some workarounds encode forgotten regulatory requirements, and abolishing them naively recreates the incident that caused them.
