# Leadership in meetings (running sessions, encouraging contribution)

## Overview

BAs lead without formal authority, and meetings are where that leadership is visible: publishing a purpose and agenda in advance, inviting only people who can contribute to the stated outcome, starting and ending on time, and steering discussion back when it wanders. The "encouraging contribution" half is the harder half — deliberately soliciting input from junior or quiet attendees ("Maria, you process these claims daily; does this match reality?"), preventing the loudest voice from becoming the de facto requirement source, and making it safe to disagree with the sponsor in the room. Every session ends with decisions, owners, and next steps read aloud.

## Context & Usage

Applies to every session a BA convenes: elicitation workshops, requirement reviews, walkthroughs, and prioritization meetings. It matters most when attendance is expensive — a workshop with eight stakeholders burns a person-day per hour, so wasted meetings destroy the BA's credibility and future access. If a meeting has no decision or information objective that requires a group, cancel it and use asynchronous channels instead.
