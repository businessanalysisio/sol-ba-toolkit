# Reviews

## Overview

Sessions used to demonstrate and inspect an increment of the solution with stakeholders, eliciting feedback to determine whether what is being developed aligns with the need. The Guide's central POA argument is that reviewing a working product increment is more relatable to stakeholders — and elicits better feedback — than evaluating PBIs, user stories, or other product documentation; outcomes include a shared understanding of the evolving product, validation of alignment with customer needs, identification of defects and possible resolutions, and determination of next steps or priority changes. For smaller incremental work, such as one or a few user stories, the Developer, QA, and POA Practitioner form a triad that evaluates the stories' progress from planning to delivery — what the Guide calls the Three Amigos review.

## Context & Usage

Reviews in POA cover more than working software: work products under review include product strategies, roadmaps, team structure, and product concepts, and rather than deciding whether a work product is right or wrong, reviews invite discussion and feedback. Since reviews are often internal to a product team without customer representation, the POA practitioner presents the customer perspective (with dedicated customer reviews used to uncover nuances of customer needs), and must ensure the whole team and all key stakeholders are represented — the three amigos can stand in for those perspectives when time is short. The Guide sets a cadence floor: reviews must be conducted at least for each iteration of product increment, so feedback can be actioned through the backlog, de-risking the product from major surprises at customer release. The value discipline is pointed — focus on the value the work product delivers, not the effort spent, the development process, or cross-dependencies that digress the review from the value discussion.
