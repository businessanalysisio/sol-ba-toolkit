# Focus Groups

## Overview

A moderated, interactive group session for eliciting ideas, impressions, preferences, and needs about a specific product or opportunity. In the POA reading of the technique, focus group studies belong to the product management function's customer research, but the POA Practitioner plays a defined role: collaborating on selection of the target customer group, the focus group objectives, and the questions participants must answer, then assessing how the outcomes relate to the product. The Guide is specific about outputs — focus group results can drive modifications to the product strategy, the roadmap, planned features, and release plan scope — and about discipline: the moderator (who may be the POA Practitioner) must not bias feedback with their own opinion.

## Context & Usage

Planned around important events in the product lifecycle — MVP releases, significant feature releases, or failed launches — because gains must be balanced against the effort, scheduling, and participant incentives involved. Questions must be relevant, concise, and "empathetic yet neutral," in a setting comfortable enough for participants to share reactions and motivations openly. As a Learn Fast tool, the subjective opinions gathered should be corroborated with product metrics, and the practitioner should probe for clarification whenever feedback is ambiguous so the elicited information genuinely assesses value.
