# Kano Analysis

## Overview

A model for understanding which product characteristics will differentiate a product in the marketplace and drive customer satisfaction, by sorting them into three categories the Guide defines by impact profile: excitement characteristics (delighters, with significant short-term impact), performance characteristics (which generate long-term impact), and threshold characteristics (basics that, if missed, negatively affect product acceptance no matter how many exciting features exist). The Guide calls it "extremely powerful" in POA specifically because early in product building — when most features are being deliberated — there is a general lack of data, KPIs, or metrics, and Kano offers a way to analyze characteristics before anything is released.

## Context & Usage

Used with elicitation techniques to assess the impact of a new feature or discuss modifications, to evaluate direct customer input (or, if customer feedback is unavailable, to have the team analyze features), and to compose releases: depending on strategy and roadmap, each release should carry a deliberate mix of performance and excitement characteristics. A modified Kano can expose gaps between current offerings and satisfaction, seeding new product ideas. The Guide's value warning is specific: teams chasing maximum value may over-select excitement characteristics and under-invest in threshold ones, causing product-market-fit misalignment — Kano restores the balance. Measures like NPS and Customer Effort Score can quantify the categories for iteration planning.
