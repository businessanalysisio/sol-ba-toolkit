# Persona

## Overview

An archetype representing how typical users or a user group will interact with a product, used to empathize with customers, align solutions with needs, and make future needs feel real to the people designing and building. The Guide captures the technique's distinctive effect: "something magical happens" when a customer becomes "Shay the shoe-buyer," "Sarah the Sales Representative," or "Rachel the Realtor" — teams start referring to personas by name in discussions ("How will Rachel access property comparison data quickly and accurately?"), igniting passion to build better solutions. Personas challenge ideas and assumptions so the team can make more effective strategic product decisions, and they enhance the value of stakeholder maps.

## Context & Usage

POA Practitioners typically lead persona creation — without doing all the work — by incorporating direct customer observation, interviews, workshops with market trends, data analytics, and other discovery activities. The Guide's engagement practices: have the team build a personal or team persona as a warm-up; invite contributions to the persona canvas; use WH questions, the Johari window, and the 5 Whys for deeper insights; draft personas from what is known and assumed, then validate with actual customers to expose gaps; keep personas visible in a shared space and bring them up during user story discussions; keep them current; and use them to plan market analytics and meaningful metrics. Motivations partly shift over time — post-release opinion changes should be reflected back into the persona.
