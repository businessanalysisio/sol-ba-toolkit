# Human-Centred Design for Products

## Overview

A practical, repeatable problem-solving approach premised on the idea that the customers facing the problem hold the key to how the product should solve it. The Guide anchors it in the ISO tenets — active customer involvement (practitioner and customer co-create the product), an iterative and non-linear journey, experimentation and feedback driving the roadmap (from rudimentary prototypes to A/B tests), and adaptive processes that serve specific needs rather than templated mass-market features — and in the IDEO-popularized three-phase process: Inspiration (frame the problem, build an interdisciplinary team, research customers), Ideation (make sense of data, generate and evaluate ideas, build tangible prototypes), and Implementation (partnerships, business model refinement, pilots, and evolution as needs change). Five mindsets underpin it: creative confidence, experiment, empathy, embrace ambiguity, and optimism.

## Context & Usage

Chosen when a team needs innovative products "planted in the customers' actual needs" and wants to de-risk failure by involving customers from the start. Its strength is evidence over speculation — as prototypes are tested by real customers, details get evaluated and feedback grows more accurate. The Guide's cautions: interviews should be discussions about the customer's experience rather than interrogations, and over-reliance on customers' current problems can limit the product to incremental innovation within existing processes and technologies.
