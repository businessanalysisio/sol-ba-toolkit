# Backlog Refinement

## Overview

A technique derived from lean practices in which product backlog items (PBIs) are developed in just enough detail that the product team agrees the set is "ready" for build activities in an upcoming iteration. The Guide's POA perspective extends the standard agile notion — refinement depth decided by team consensus — with domain-specific practices: elaborating PBIs from the user's viewpoint grounded in empathy (quoting real customer examples), right-sizing by decomposing oversized items into smaller stories, and refining just-in-time so effort is not wasted on items that may never be built. Refinement can also surface elements that affect product alignment across the strategy, initiative, or delivery horizons, which must be fed back into the backlog to conform to the product vision.

## Context & Usage

A recurring whole-team activity — the Guide is explicit that refinement sessions are not the sole responsibility of the Product Owner, and that the POA Practitioner must weigh the whole team's opinion on what makes a PBI "ready." Used ahead of iteration planning to select and shape the stories relevant to the iteration's theme or release. Team understanding of "how much refinement is enough" matures over iterations: initially the team collectively sets readiness parameters for PBIs, epics, and user stories, then reassesses every iteration until shared understanding stabilizes.
