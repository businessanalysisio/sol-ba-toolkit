# Product Backlog Management

## Overview

Recording, tracking, and ordering the remaining work items so the team delivers the most value with the least risk. The Guide leans on two specifics: the Pareto principle applied to product delivery — 80% of the value sits in 20% of the features, and the trick is finding that elusive 20% — and the 2011 Scrum Guide terminology change from "prioritized" to "ordered" backlog, a change frequently misread as meaning prioritization is no longer needed (it grants flexibility, not exemption). It catalogues product-specific ordering techniques beyond MoSCoW: Cost of Delay and Weighted Shortest Job First (cost of delay divided by job size), cost vs. benefit scoring, RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence over Effort), buy-a-feature, opportunity scoring (importance vs. current satisfaction), value vs. complexity, and the Eisenhower matrix — with a selection grid that plots techniques by required customer interaction against subjective vs. quantitative information.

## Context & Usage

Continuous through the delivery horizon: ordering at the feature level tracks customer and business strategy, while ordering PBIs within features incorporates delivery-team input on what makes sense technically. Two Guide heuristics stand out: the backlog "is not a dumping ground" for every request or issue, and too many "must have" items signals an unclear product vision and customer need — worth a root cause analysis. The value/effort "sweet spot" balances alignment to vision, customer value, business impact, revenue, speed to deliver, and managed risk; rules of engagement for each technique must be enforced, or stakeholders will dodge the difficult decisions.
