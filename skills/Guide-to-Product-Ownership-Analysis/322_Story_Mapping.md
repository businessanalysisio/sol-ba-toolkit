# Story Mapping

## Overview

Creates a shared understanding of product functionality and the flow of usage while assisting with prioritizing product delivery — the map gives a top-down view of PBIs, epics, stories, and steps within a delivery horizon, capturing customer activities and goals as relatable stories so pain points surface naturally. POA practitioners elevate it beyond a workshop artifact: the Guide positions the story map as an input to iteration and release planning, a way to plan desired outcomes across different agile horizons (as a component of the product roadmap or when planning the MVP), and a device for discovering dependencies and critical paths among product features. Its distinctive move is reorganization — flipping the drill-down of PBIs into a sequence of user stories for one specific user, which shows exactly how that user would traverse the product and reveals gaps in the flow.

## Context & Usage

Treat the map as a fluid, living visual: delivery priorities, schedule, and budget change often, and keeping it current gives the team standing visibility into product progress. During iteration planning, release planning, and team discussions it works as a visual anchor — teams track progress on it and get a sense of accomplishment when a story is delivered — while gaps in the flow of PBIs cue the practitioner to course-correct and adapt iterations or releases when story development becomes distressed. Summarized upward, the map ties product goals and objectives to business and customer goals; read across personas, the Guide calls it an excellent tool to visualize value expected by each customer persona versus value actually delivered.
