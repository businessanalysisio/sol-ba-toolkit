# Obsess about Value

## Overview

The seventh POA domain, grounded in the BABOK definition it quotes — value is "the worth, importance, or usefulness of something to a stakeholder within a context," tangible or intangible, potential or realized, and possibly negative (losses, risks, costs). From the customer's perspective, value is a comparison between a product's benefits and its cost — price paid or effort saved — and the Guide observes that industry has spent far more energy managing price than understanding value, which evolves as expectations change. The domain prescribes two critical steps throughout the life of a product: Evolve the Product (assess strategic alignment, market alignment — regulations, trends, competition — and financial viability) and Optimize Value Delivery (assess team capability, optimize process, leverage technology).

## Context & Usage

The capstone domain, applied whenever learnings must become product changes. Its worked example: value-based metrics showed a mobile accessories app with a high Customer Effort Score; assessment revealed customers had to empty a full cart to buy a single item, and each fix option (direct-purchase button, empty-cart-in-one-go, cart limits) had to be weighed against the business objective of revenue through cart items. The Guide positions this domain as the converter between Learn Fast and Deliver Often: learnings are analyzed in context, turned into valuable options for the customer, and delivered through an optimized path — each release being an opportunity to realize value at the point of customer impact.
