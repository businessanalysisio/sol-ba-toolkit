# Real Options

## Overview

Where most prioritization techniques answer what to decide, real options exists to help determine when to make decisions. In the Guide's POA perspective, each product backlog item can be treated as an option carrying a decision — postpone, abandon, scope up, scope down, or switch — defined together with the business, the customer, and the product team, and easily adapted with a decision tree to evaluate the value of choosing one option over the others. Some options carry an expiry timeframe: certain PBIs become irrelevant if not included in the product by a specific time, and real options makes that decay explicit rather than letting value silently lapse.

## Context & Usage

The Guide positions real options across all seven POA domains: as a framework for reviewing the urgency of decisions from a business perspective (structuring work and teams, aligning the product to strategy, choosing the right business capabilities); as a way to assess options from different personas' perspectives, including the customer's, to find decisions with maximum customer value; and as a continuous process for sequencing features and PBIs, which iteration or release planning meetings can leverage directly. Two elements must both be met for impactful feature delivery — the value derived and the timing of the release — and real options is called a critical tool for analyzing exactly that pairing. Qualifying options as real and imminent is deliberately a team effort: a POA practitioner may outline the initial analysis, but the whole team, especially the extended product team, decides as a group which options are relevant. Historical evidence, product metrics, and customer feedback become learning mechanisms once mechanisms exist to observe both the options and their consequences.
