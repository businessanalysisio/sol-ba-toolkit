# Risk Analysis and Management

## Overview

Identifies areas of uncertainty that could negatively affect value, analyzes and evaluates those uncertainties, and develops and manages ways of dealing with them. In the POA context this becomes a continuous process running for a longer duration than a time-boxed project, because product risks fold in long-term market and business environment components. The Guide splits product risk into two categories: Delivery and Execution Risk (cost, schedule, resources, and the risk of building unintended features that create no value) and Market Risk (the product does not generate the right outcomes for the business or customers) — noting that while practitioners are familiar with the first, the second demands its own mitigation toolkit: additional customer research such as ethnography, right-sized launches like an MVP, phased alpha/beta rollouts, careful sequencing of feature chunks, A/B tests of customer acceptance, functional research via spikes and PoCs, and metrics/KPIs.

## Context & Usage

The product team conducts the risk exercise at least once per iteration so the risk register reflects current state — the Guide bluntly names unmitigated risks the primary cause of failed products. Identification starts with customer needs and an analysis of organizational capabilities (team competencies, delivery capabilities, support mechanisms); the POA practitioner brings in the customer perspective, but the entire team must highlight delivery- and execution-related risks. Product decisions with make-or-break, irreversible consequences must be weighed with detailed analysis and an appropriate mitigation plan, and even carefully managed delivery risk cannot rescue a product that misses on market risk — fast customer feedback and KPI analysis are what close that gap.
