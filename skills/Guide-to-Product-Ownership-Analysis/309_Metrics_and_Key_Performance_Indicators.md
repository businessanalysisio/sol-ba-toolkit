# Metrics and Key Performance Indicators

## Overview

Measures of product performance organized, in the POA view, by level of impact: strategic measures tied to business objectives (ROI, MRR/ARR per user, customer acquisition cost, NPV, total cost of ownership, IRR, time-to-market), product measures of customer acceptance (Net Promoter Score on a 10-point scale, Customer Effort Score usually on a 5-point scale, adoption rates, feature usage rate, retention/churn), and delivery measures of team performance (iteration goal success, escaped defects, defect density, scope change rate, burndown, team velocity). To align signals with product goals the Guide recommends the HEART framework — Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task Success — where each goal maps to a signal and a metric (e.g., Happiness → reduced effort to complete a user goal → CES).

## Context & Usage

Applied to resolve the competing tensions the Guide lists — business objectives vs. customer needs, short- vs. long-term benefits, strategic vs. tactical considerations, existing vs. new segments, experience vs. more features — each of which can decide product success or failure. Metrics are predetermined and agreed with the team, measured as often as needed, and re-prioritized by scenario (churn may be deprioritized when acquisition cost rises under a new strategy). Since individual feedback cannot cover a large customer pool, metrics like NPS, CES, and retention stand in for customer perception, but the practitioner must combine them with persona knowledge to extract insight — and avoid vanity metrics that merely embellish the ongoing work.
