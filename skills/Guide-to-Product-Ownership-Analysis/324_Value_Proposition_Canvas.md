# Value Proposition Canvas

## Overview

Originally developed by Dr. Alex Osterwalder, this canvas expresses the product's value from the customer's perspective across two halves: the Customer Profile (a circle on the right capturing customer jobs — functional, social, and emotional — plus the pains experienced while getting those jobs done and the gains the customer expects) and the Value Proposition Map (a square on the left describing the products and services, the pain relievers that address customer frustrations, and the gain creators that deliver desired outcomes and experiences). The Guide frames the resulting value proposition as the culmination of ideas, customer intimacy, market insights, evidence, and design articulated into a promise of value — stating how the product solves the pain, what benefits customers can expect, and why they should choose it over competitors — and notes that the canvas feeds the customer and value proposition segments of the Business Model Canvas (or vice versa).

## Context & Usage

The Guide's four-step process: (1) define the customer segments and agree as a team who the real customers are, creating one canvas per segment; (2) complete the Customer Profile by detailing customer jobs, discussing the pains of each activity, and uncovering gains, which are often hidden ambitions beyond mere pain relief; (3) brainstorm solution options and document the one that addresses the jobs, pains, and gains in the Value Proposition Map; (4) test canvas success — roughly 3-5 jobs and 3-5 prioritized pains and gains per canvas, with every pain and gain mappable to a pain reliever or gain creator. Sequence matters: fill in the customer side first, then design the product side around what the customer really needs. The chief limitation is segmentation discipline — merging multiple customer segments into a single canvas weakens the value proposition and diminishes the technique's benefits.
