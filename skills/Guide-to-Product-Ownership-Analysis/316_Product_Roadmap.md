# Product Roadmap

## Overview

A strategic, visual tool for communicating direction and progress toward the vision for a solution or initiative — a representation of how the team plans to implement its product strategy over progressively longer time horizons, describing how the product is likely to grow, align to stakeholders' needs, and acquire a budget for delivery. The Guide is emphatic about what it is not: a succession of milestones, tasks, and deliverables disconnected from product goals; the focus stays on goals, benefits, and demonstration of value, expressed iteratively in terms of now, next, and later. It names three commonly used forms: the goal-oriented roadmap (goal, features, and success metrics per release), the now-next-later roadmap (granular scope and user stories in "now", prototypes and mockups in "next", high-level big picture in "later"), and the story map.

## Context & Usage

The roadmap is the link between product vision and product delivery — it influences how the product will be built and aligns architectural and technical strategic decisions, making it the starting point for product planning. The Guide's five-step process runs: begin with the vision and its goals (including market opportunities and pressures), determine the capabilities or features needed to achieve those goals, prioritize (weighing budget, resources, dependencies, high-impact easy wins), develop the roadmap by placing features into time blocks, then share it with stakeholders to gain buy-in and build the product backlog. Tips for success warn that a 2+ year timeframe adds too much uncertainty, that the roadmap is a living document that must not devolve into the backlog, and that roadmaps should only be developed after the product strategy is defined and agreed upon; misused, it becomes a milestone-driven feature list that loses its purpose.
