# Product-Market Fit

## Overview

A concept popularized in the start-up world for identifying a strong market where a product can thrive — achieved, per the Guide, "when the company's value proposition, customers, and distribution channels co-exist in a balanced ecosystem," and visible when customers help sell the product or demand exceeds supply. Its model elements are problem statement, solution, value proposition, customers, assumptions and risks, and metrics. Measurement is unusually concrete: quantitatively, the 40% rule (at least 40% of surveyed customers consider the product a "must-have"), churn rate, NPS, growth rate, and market share; qualitatively, word of mouth and media buzz. Examples span both worlds: Snapchat as start-up fit that must keep being measured, and Facebook adding Marketplace — leveraging seller visibility and in-app messaging trust — as an established enterprise renewing desirability.

## Context & Usage

Created in initial product conceptualization and then re-verified at the end of each customer release, with feedback driving backlog decisions for future releases. The Guide's nine-step process runs: identify customer segments; know customers deeply (personas, journey maps, observation); evaluate top competitors; define a high-level solution; state the value proposition; plan the MVP feature set using the 80/20 rule; elicit feedback; measure fit; and avoid complacency — monitoring market dynamics, since stagnating sales may point to a missing feature, and emotions or egos blocking product evolution damage fit. For holistic strategy work it should be paired with other techniques such as the Business Model Canvas.
