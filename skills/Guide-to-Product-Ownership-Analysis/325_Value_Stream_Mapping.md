# Value Stream Mapping

## Overview

Provides a complete, fact-based, time-series representation of the activities required to deliver a product or service to the customer — a technique with its origins in manufacturing supply-chain analysis, adopted into product ownership with one crucial twist. Historically, "value" in supply-chain processes meant cost or cycle time; in the POA reading, the extended product team decides the definition of value, and that definition changes across the product lifecycle — the Guide's example being customer acquisition as the value measure at product launch, giving way to customer lifetime value in the maturity phase. The definition can even stay fluid and subjective rather than a hard metric, provided the team shares an understanding of the value delivered, and the value stream itself is defined at the appropriate agile horizon: features value stream at the delivery horizon, product value stream at the initiative horizon, enterprise value stream at the strategy horizon.

## Context & Usage

Because it enumerates the flow of activities along a time axis, the Guide credits value stream mapping with more detail than ordinary process flows, and running it demands the entire product team plus cross-functional representatives for a variety of perspectives. Practitioners apply it to analyze the flow of product features, workflows, or transactions — assessing which pieces earn a better product-market fit, crafting a sharper value proposition, and finding the right time to ship features for maximum impact. At the portfolio level it evaluates whether one product's output benefits another, aligning products to enterprise objectives, while at delivery level it feeds iteration and release planning by contextualizing KPIs across multiple value streams so every element demonstrably improves the metrics being tracked.
