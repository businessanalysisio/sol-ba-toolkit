# Collaborative Games

## Overview

Structured games that give participants a safe space to build shared understanding, trust, and team spirit. The Guide catalogues five POA-relevant families with named examples: team-building games (bonding, with or without product objectives); prioritization games such as the $100 test, where participants "spend" $100 across proposed features to reveal what matters most; stakeholder analysis games such as "friend or foe," assessing whether a stakeholder would support product activities; problem-solving games such as semantic environment mapping (analyzing rules, touchpoints, actors, environment, keywords); and product-perception games — 2 Brains "Tell It and Sell It," product box, elevator pitch, and sound bites — designed to elicit what excites customers and test emotional appeal.

## Context & Usage

Deployed for elicitation, prioritization, risk assessment, knowledge sharing, problem-solving, decision-making, and planning, typically with cross-functional members, the extended product team, and customers, depending on intent. Especially useful in early team setup and capability analysis, and as a learning tool when concrete data is not yet available for robust analysis. The Guide warns these games are often skipped under schedule pressure, yet a well-conducted game is memorable, motivates the team, and — when customers participate — lets them express themselves without reservation.
