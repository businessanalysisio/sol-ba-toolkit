# Visioning

## Overview

Concisely words the desired outcome for an initiative — in POA terms, it derives a cohesive, integrated, and aspirational view capturing the essence of why the product should exist, describing the value provided (the value proposition) to a specific group of stakeholders (the target market) whose needs are unmet. The Guide characterizes visioning exercises as open-ended discussions conducted as a team with relevant stakeholders, producing a guiding statement aligned with both the organization's vision and the product vision; that statement is then shared often to build shared understanding. Crucially, it cannot be performed in isolation: the vision represents the enterprise's and the whole product team's perspective on the problem being solved, and team members need their ideas heard for the vision to motivate them.

## Context & Usage

Re-run the exercise whenever the product pivots — products typically need re-evaluation after initial launch to confirm they are still solving the right problem — and update the vision as learning from key stakeholders and customers improves product-market fit, since most strategic, market, and delivery assessments are conducted with the product vision at the centre of focus. A well-crafted vision does double duty externally and internally: customers who relate to it form an emotional connection that helps build the right brand, while the team uses it to assess the value delivered through each product increment and to identify needed changes. One honest caveat from the Guide: visioning has no significant role in ensuring timely deliveries (the Deliver Often domain) — delivering frequently is instead how the product gets tested against the vision originally crafted.
