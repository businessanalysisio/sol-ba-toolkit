# Learn Fast

## Overview

The measurement-and-assessment domain of the POA Framework, built on two central themes: Measure What Matters and Assess What Is Delivered. Value must be identified and measured at three levels — strategic measures (effectiveness of product vision and strategy, tied to business objectives: product lifetime value, revenue share, average order value, conversion rates), product measures (customer acceptance and popularity), and delivery measures (the team's delivery performance) — then converted into assessments: strategic assessment feeding vision, strategy, and roadmap; product/market assessment shaping scope and features; and delivery assessment revealing gaps in team effectiveness. The Guide stresses timing: customer acquisition rate suits launch, while performance-against-specification and satisfaction-per-price-point better reflect post-launch stability and growth.

## Context & Usage

Used across the product lifecycle because speed and innovation are what differentiate a successful product — if a product cannot deliver value at the rate customer expectations change, it falls behind. The domain closes a common enterprise gap: teams collect feedback but consolidate and apply learnings ad hoc, without asking what customer and business value mean, which metrics best describe that value, and how the product should change based on learnings. Metric choice depends on product purpose, definition of value, business model, go-to-market strategy, lifecycle stage, timing, and monetization — and on role: financial and market metrics tend to sit with product management rather than the Product Owner.
