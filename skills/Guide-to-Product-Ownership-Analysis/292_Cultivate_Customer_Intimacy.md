# Cultivate Customer Intimacy

## Overview

The POA domain centred on knowing customers as real people rather than data points — the Guide frames it with Steve Jobs' "faster horse" remark: people don't know what they want until you show it to them, so the job is "to read things that are not yet on the page." Two sub-areas structure the domain: Know the Customer (get personal, build empathy, engage in observation, validate problems) and Apply Customer Learnings (integrate learnings from analytics, domain knowledge, and research; evaluate and evolve the product). The Guide names six values as the building blocks of customer relationships — trust, respect, empathy, appreciation, communication, and commitment — and stresses that intimacy is a deeper, personal knowledge of the customer "that is constantly refreshed," not a one-time research exercise.

## Context & Usage

Exercised continuously by the Product Owner or POA Practitioner in the customer-advocate role: creating a relationship with customers, maintaining a presence so they feel heard and valued, keeping feedback loops open, and inspiring customers to contribute to improvements. It matters most before problem definition and solution design — targeted activities often reveal customers have different needs than originally assumed — and it underpins the quality of every downstream artifact, from personas to value propositions. The Guide pairs the domain with concrete elicitation techniques: personas for motivations, interviews and surveys for opinions, journey maps for pain points and points of delight.
