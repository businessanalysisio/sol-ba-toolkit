# Job Stories

## Overview

A way to express a product backlog item or requirement as a "job to be done" by a stakeholder, using the format "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]" — putting the triggering context ahead of any persona. The Guide's POA perspective dismantles the notion that job stories and user stories are mutually exclusive: they are complementary, with job stories covering the common actions most users perform and user stories capturing the more nuanced user-to-user differences that need deeper analysis of customer pain points. Its worked example: "When searching for a specific insurance policy on the ABC Insurance application, a policy buyer must not be shown any ads. That way, the policy buyer can complete the transaction and not click away from the product."

## Context & Usage

Best used to streamline the common activities, transactions, and workflows in a product — including work that provides business value but is of little direct interest to the customer, which job stories capture better than user stories. Because job stories emphasize the expected outcome, they relate directly to value; filtering common activities into job stories frees the team to spend its story-writing effort on customer-specific delight. The Guide also notes they integrate easily with Kanban-style agile frameworks, where boards accelerate delivery of these flow-oriented items; they play little role in the foundational, whole-team, or learn-fast domains.
