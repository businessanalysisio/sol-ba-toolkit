# Spikes

## Overview

Time-boxed research, design, exploration, investigation, or prototyping activities used to understand the effort required to deliver a backlog item or initiative — a spike buys knowledge and an estimation baseline rather than a shippable feature. Per the Guide, spikes exist primarily to establish an effort estimate for PBIs that need elaboration, with the POA practitioner supplying the background knowledge or product vision that drives the need for the item; designated team members then investigate for clarity. Scaled up, the same idea becomes a Proof of Concept (PoC), where the goal shifts from estimating effort to demonstrating the value of a thought process, product idea, prototype, or a whole group of PBIs — most prevalent when new products are being incubated or investment decisions must be made.

## Context & Usage

An individual spike is typically owned by a single team member responsible for discovering the details, but the results are debated by the whole team to arrive at a shared baseline estimate. Expect heavier spike usage in the initial phases of the product lifecycle while PBIs are in flux; as the team's understanding matures, fewer spikes should need to be accommodated. Because spikes usually consume effort beyond the traditional iteration budget, the Guide stresses fast learning and prompt sharing of findings — and in the early lifecycle they can even be pointed inward, e.g. to verify whether the organizational context and team capabilities allow certain PBIs to be delivered at all. They play no significant role in the Cultivate Customer Intimacy domain.
