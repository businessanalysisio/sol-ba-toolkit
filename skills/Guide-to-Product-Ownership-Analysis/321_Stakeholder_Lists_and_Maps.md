# Stakeholder Lists and Maps

## Overview

Assists the practitioner in analyzing stakeholders and their characteristics to identify all possible sources of product requirements, methods of engagement, and the target market for the product. Where the traditional activity was directed internally at requirement sources, the Guide's POA perspective points it outward: stakeholder lists and maps aid in segmentation, targeting, and positioning the product. Beyond the typical stakeholder matrix, RACI, and onion diagrams, it introduces Customer Grouping — organizing customers by commonalities across four dimensions (geographic: countries, regions, language, climate; demographic: age, gender, family, income; psychographic: lifestyle, values, beliefs; behavioural: user status, use rate, loyalty, occasions) — as a first step toward persona archetypes that shape design, solution, marketing, and promotion.

## Context & Usage

Product value is realized only when correctly targeted customers use the product in ways that address their unique needs, so well-analyzed customer information translates directly into product scope aligned with business strategy. Customer grouping surfaces whether each market segment has distinct needs and delivery channels — the Guide's example: an insurance company engaging Baby Boomers and Generation Z through different channels because their expectations diverge along generational lines. Building the groups takes multiple iterations with input from the entire product team (a tech-savvy member can predict how a segment will perceive the product), the personas representing groups need frequent review as the evolving product shifts perceptions, and when groups hold competing priorities the team must sequence PBIs to deliver the best value across them.
