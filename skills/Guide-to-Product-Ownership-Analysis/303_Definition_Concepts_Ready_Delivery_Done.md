# Definition Concepts (Ready, Delivery, Done)

## Overview

Three team-agreed, prominently displayed checklists that govern a backlog item's lifecycle. Definition of Ready: criteria before development starts — sufficient detail and clarity (business rules, UI mock-ups, NFRs, data mapping), the PBI meets INVEST (Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable), relative estimation done, and acceptance criteria defined. Definition of Delivery: what must be done for items to be eligible for release — marketing materials, training (manuals, FAQs, videos), quality (open defects resolved), technical (security, deployment plan), and production support readiness. Definition of Done: what completeness means, with the Guide's examples including "100% of acceptance criteria have been developed and passed testing" and "80% of the code has been reviewed." Unlike acceptance criteria, which are tied to a specific PBI, definitions span many PBIs.

## Context & Usage

Created collaboratively in an iteration, release, or delivery planning session, then revisited as team understanding matures, members change, or the product changes direction — retrospectives are a natural review point. The Guide's tips: make criteria specific ("acceptance criteria must be met" vs. the measurable version), achievable (not "product shipped to the customer" for every increment), and whole-team agreed, since the definitions are a commitment to quality. Teams operating with a clear Definition of Ready followed by Definition of Done accelerate velocity, delivery, and PBI acceptance; the Definition of Delivery adds granularity to the roadmap and sets stakeholder expectations for complex releases.
