# Deliver Often

## Overview

The delivery domain of the POA Framework, summed up by the Guide as delivering "just enough of the right product to the right people, early and often." It splits into Plan Delivery (manage the product backlog, define the MVP, plan releases) and Build Product (plan iterations, build features, validate features), and distinguishes two planning modes drawn from the Agile Extension: iterative planning, which prioritizes and refines work in short cycles to increase feedback, and adaptive planning, which continuously changes long-term plans based on evaluation. Distinctive details include the pipeline from Vision (customer intimacy, roadmap, value proposition, business model canvas) through Product Backlog (components/features/PBIs, estimation, refinement) to Delivery (iteration planning, capacity plan, ready backlog, MVP/MMR/MMF/MMP), and the team discipline of answering "How will we know?" before starting work.

## Context & Usage

Applied throughout the delivery horizon whenever value is at risk of arriving late, big, or wrong. Deliver Often breaks deliverables into smaller, meaningful increments that surface early feedback, expose risk and cost for early adjustment, and keep the customer at the centre of everything. The Guide notes that the product backlog is owned by the Product Owner and "is not a dumping ground for every request"; increments are shared sooner for customer validation, and Definitions of Done and Delivery are used to align stakeholder expectations — for example when an experimental prototype delivers less than stakeholders assumed agile delivery promised.
