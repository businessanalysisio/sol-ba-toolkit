# Business Cases

## Overview

A justification for a course of action based on benefits versus cost and effort — but with a product-ownership twist the Guide makes explicit: in POA, value and benefits are realized continuously over a long period, unlike the one-time event of a project context. The business case here often takes the form of a comprehensive product plan containing product vision; product strategies (needs assessment, target market, competitive assessments, industry analysis); business value and value propositions; financial assessments such as ROI mapped to the product lifecycle; key strategy and product metrics; product roadmap; business model; go-to-market strategies; and risks, constraints, and assumptions. Value framing is no longer unidirectional revenue/cost/schedule — for example, a strategy may sacrifice funds for rapid user onboarding to win better customer lifetime value.

## Context & Usage

Prepared by the product management function, but the POA Practitioner contributes significant input on the roadmap, the sequence of features, and the customer perspective — and must synchronize across product management, product ownership, marketing, and technology functions to assemble it. Created upfront and iteratively developed; once product build begins, the plan that evolved from the business case is a live artifact, kept current to reflect prevailing product and market conditions, and doubles as a learning tool for securing investment and testing whether the value and timing of build activities are justified.
