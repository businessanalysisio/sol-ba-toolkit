# Decision Modelling and Analysis

## Overview

Decision modelling shows how repeatable business decisions are made; decision analysis formally assesses a problem and its alternatives under uncertainty. The Guide characterizes three types of critical product decisions, each with its own toolset: product strategy decisions (customer needs, target market, goals, KRAs/KPIs — addressed with strategy analysis tools), product feature decisions (synthesizing ideas, feature requests, and business considerations — addressed with solution assessment and validation), and feature priority and timing decisions (the release order that generates maximum value — addressed with prioritization techniques and a value matrix). For repeatable decisions, it recommends a decision framework built from process flows, checklists, a decision authority matrix, or QA stage gates.

## Context & Usage

Applied because product decisions are inherently made with uncertainty about how customers will receive the product. The Guide notes decisions have expiry windows — for example, forgoing revenue to attract users is only relevant until the product reaches critical user mass — so the team must evaluate both the impact and timing of each feature decision. Customer research supplies the data for inclusion and ordering choices; stakeholder engagement should be embedded in the decision process; and some decisions are best validated through product metrics, simulations, or A/B tests. Every decision must trace to a goal aligned with the product vision.
