# Fishbone diagrams / Cause-and-effect diagrams / Ishikawa diagrams

## Overview

A root-cause analysis diagram created by quality pioneer Kaoru Ishikawa in 1960s Japan, drawn as a fish skeleton: the problem (effect) sits at the head, and major cause categories form the main bones, with contributing causes branching off each bone. Classic manufacturing categories are the 6 Ms — manpower (people), machines, methods, materials, measurement and mother nature (environment) — while service settings often substitute categories such as people, processes, systems, suppliers and surroundings. Teams populate the bones by repeatedly asking "why does this happen?", pushing past symptoms toward causes that, if removed, would prevent recurrence.

## Context & Usage

The standard workshop tool for problem analysis when a performance issue has many suspected contributors and opinions differ about the real culprit. The category structure prevents the group anchoring on the first plausible cause, and the finished diagram shows at a glance where causes cluster. BAs pair it with evidence-gathering — sampling or special-purpose records to test which candidate causes actually occur at meaningful frequency — before proposing remedies, since a fishbone alone documents hypotheses, not proof.
