# Force-field analysis

## Overview

Kurt Lewin's diagramming technique for weighing the forces around a proposed change: the change is drawn as a central line, driving forces that push for it are arrayed as arrows on one side, and restraining forces that resist it push back from the other, with arrow length or a numeric weight indicating each force's strength. The picture makes an otherwise fuzzy judgement explicit — will this change stick? — and points to the levers: Lewin's key insight is that weakening restraining forces is usually more effective than simply adding more drivers, because piling on pressure tends to increase resistance in proportion.

## Context & Usage

Used when assessing whether a change option is achievable and when planning its implementation — typically in a workshop where stakeholders nominate and weight the forces themselves, which builds shared awareness of the resistance. It also serves as an evaluation aid in the business case (an option facing overwhelming restraining forces may be technically sound but organisationally doomed). For each significant restraining force, the output should be an action: who will address it, how, and by when.
