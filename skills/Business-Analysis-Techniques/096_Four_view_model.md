# Four-view model

## Overview

A compact holistic framework that partitions any business system into four interacting views — processes, people, organisation, and information & technology — and requires the analyst to examine all four together rather than in isolation. Like Leavitt's Diamond and POPIT, it embodies the socio-technical principle that business change succeeds only when every view moves in step: redesigned processes need re-skilled people, an enabling structure, and supporting systems. Its value lies less in novelty than in discipline: four named lenses that must each yield findings.

## Context & Usage

Typically used to structure situation investigation and option definition. During investigation, findings from interviews, observation and document analysis are sorted into the four views to reveal where problems really originate — a "system problem" often turns out to be a process or skills problem. During solution shaping, each candidate option is described across all four views so its full cost and impact are visible. Presenting the four views side by side also helps mixed business/IT audiences agree what the change actually comprises.
