# SARAH model (shock, anger, rejection, acceptance, hope)

## Overview

An emotional-response curve, adapted from Elisabeth Kübler-Ross's grief cycle, describing how people typically react to unwelcome organisational change: Shock — numbness and inability to take the news in, when detailed communication is wasted; Anger — the change and its messengers are attacked, questions turn hostile; Rejection — denial and bargaining, people carry on as before hoping "it won't really happen" or actively resist; Acceptance — the reality is acknowledged and attention turns, tentatively, to living with it; Hope — energy returns as people find their place in the new arrangement and performance climbs back, often above the old baseline. Morale traces a valley through the middle stages, and individuals move through the curve at different speeds — some get stuck, and some cycle backwards on fresh bad news.

## Context & Usage

Applied when planning the communication and support around any change that costs people something — restructures, new systems that deshape valued jobs, relocations. The model's practical payoff is timing: expect little comprehension during shock (so repeat key messages later), don't read anger as failure of the change (it is a stage, not a verdict), provide listening and involvement to move people through rejection, and supply training and early wins once acceptance opens the door to hope. For the analyst it also reframes "user resistance" during requirements work as a predictable emotional stage to be managed, not irrationality to be argued down.
