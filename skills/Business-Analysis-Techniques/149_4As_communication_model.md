# '4As' communication model

## Overview

A preparation checklist built on four A-headed prompts that a communicator works through before delivering any significant message, presentation or document. Aim: what outcome must this communication achieve — a decision, approval, awareness, a changed behaviour? Audience: who will receive it, what do they already know, what do they care about, and what objections or sensitivities will they bring? Approach: given aim and audience, which medium, tone and level of detail fit — a workshop, a one-page summary, a formal presentation, an informal conversation? Assembly: how should the material be structured and sequenced so the key message lands — headline first for executives, evidence trail for analysts. Working the prompts in order matters, because approach and assembly choices only make sense once aim and audience are pinned down.

## Context & Usage

Relevant at every point where analysis results must move someone: presenting a business case, walking stakeholders through findings, requesting sign-off, breaking unwelcome news about scope or cost. BAs commonly fail not on analysis but on transmission — a technically sound recommendation packaged for the wrong audience dies in the meeting — and the model exists to prevent exactly that. It pairs naturally with stakeholder analysis, which supplies the audience insight the second A depends on.
