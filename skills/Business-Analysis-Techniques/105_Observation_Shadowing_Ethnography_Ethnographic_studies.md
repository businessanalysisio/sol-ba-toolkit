# Observation / Shadowing / Ethnography / Ethnographic studies

## Overview

A family of investigation techniques based on watching work actually being done rather than asking about it. Intensity varies: formal observation of a task at a workstation; shadowing, where the analyst follows one person through a full shift or working day; and ethnographic study, a prolonged immersion in the work community to understand its culture, language and unwritten rules. Observation exposes the say-do gap — workarounds, undocumented steps, interruptions and exception handling that interviewees forget or choose not to mention. Its known weakness is the Hawthorne effect: people behave differently when watched, which extended presence partly mitigates.

## Context & Usage

Deployed when the analyst suspects the documented or described process diverges from reality, when tacit manual skill matters (e.g. front-line service work), or when quantifying interruptions and delays that never appear in procedure manuals. Shadowing is particularly effective early in an engagement for building empathy and credibility with operational staff. Always agree ground rules first — purpose, confidentiality, how findings will be used — since being observed is intrusive, and validate observed anomalies with the person before reporting them.
