# Interviewing

## Overview

One-to-one (or small-group) questioning of stakeholders, and the most widely used investigation technique in business analysis. Interviews run on a spectrum from structured (fixed question set, comparable answers) through semi-structured (themes with freedom to probe) to unstructured (exploratory conversation). A classic session follows a funnel: open questions to let the interviewee talk broadly, probing questions to dig into specifics, then closed questions to confirm facts. The technique's discipline lies in the three phases around the conversation — preparation (objectives, interviewee selection, agenda sent in advance), conduct (listening far more than talking, capturing verbatim points), and follow-up (a written summary sent back for confirmation).

## Context & Usage

The default opening move of situation investigation: interviews build rapport with key stakeholders while yielding rich qualitative material — opinions, motivations and organisational politics that no document will reveal. They are expensive per data point, so BAs typically interview a cross-section (sponsor, managers, front-line staff) and then corroborate claims with observation, sampling or document analysis, since interviewees describe work as they believe it happens, not always as it does.
