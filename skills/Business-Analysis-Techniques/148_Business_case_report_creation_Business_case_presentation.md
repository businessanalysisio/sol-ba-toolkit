# Business case report creation / Business case presentation

## Overview

Assembles the products of options evaluation into the document and presentation on which an investment decision is made. A conventional report structure runs: introduction and background; management summary (written last, readable alone); description of the current situation and the problem or opportunity; the options considered, including do-nothing and those rejected at feasibility with reasons; for each surviving option an analysis of costs, benefits, impacts and risks; the recommendation with its justification; and appendices holding the detailed models and financial workings. The accompanying presentation is not the report read aloud — it selects the decision-relevant headlines for the audience in the room and anticipates the questions the figures will provoke.

## Context & Usage

Produced at the end of the evaluate-options stage, then revisited at lifecycle gates: a business case is a living justification that should be re-confirmed when costs move, benefits shrink or context shifts, and a project whose case has evaporated should be stopped. Effective practice includes socialising the recommendation with key decision-makers before the formal presentation (no ambushes), quantifying wherever possible while never burying intangibles, and being transparent about assumptions and sensitivities rather than presenting single-point estimates as certainty.
