# Cultural analysis

## Overview

Examines "the way we do things around here" before attempting to change it, using established typologies. Charles Handy's four organisational cultures are the standard lens: power culture (a spider's web around a dominant centre, decisions by access to the boss — typical of founder-led firms), role culture (the classic bureaucracy where the job description matters more than the person, strong on procedure, slow to change), task culture (project- and expertise-oriented, resources flow to the problem — receptive to change but hard to control), and person culture (the organisation exists to serve individual professionals, as in partnerships and chambers). For work crossing national boundaries, Hofstede's dimensions — power distance, individualism, uncertainty avoidance, masculinity/femininity, long-term orientation — flag where practices that succeed in one country misfire in another.

## Context & Usage

Performed when planning change or requirements engagement strategy, because culture determines what will work: a sign-off cascade suits a role culture but insults a power culture, where the real decision needs the centre's ear; workshops thrive in task cultures and can fall flat where hierarchy silences juniors — a high power-distance trait. Evidence comes from observation, stories, symbols and what actually gets rewarded rather than from the values poster. The analyst's output is rarely a document titled "cultural analysis"; it is a change and communication approach visibly tuned to the culture found.
