# Context diagram

## Overview

The highest-level scoping model in structured analysis: a single circle or box representing the entire system (or business area) under study, surrounded by the external entities it interacts with — customers, suppliers, regulators, other systems — connected by labelled flows of data or goods. In data flow diagramming terms it is the level-0 DFD, the parent from which lower-level process decompositions descend. Everything inside the central shape is in scope; everything shown as an external entity is out of scope but relevant; anything absent from the page is declared irrelevant, which is exactly the conversation the diagram exists to provoke.

## Context & Usage

Produced early — often in the first scoping workshop — to force explicit agreement on system boundaries before requirements work begins. Disputes about whether a party or interface belongs on the diagram are cheap to resolve at this stage and expensive later. BAs also use context diagrams to enumerate interfaces (each arrow implies an interface requirement) and as the stable frontispiece of a requirements document that orients every reader. Keep it to one page and one level of detail; decomposition belongs in lower-level models.
