# Learning cycle / Kolb cycle / Learning styles

## Overview

David Kolb's experiential learning cycle holds that durable learning loops through four phases: concrete experience (do something), reflective observation (review what happened), abstract conceptualisation (form a theory or general rule from the reflection), and active experimentation (test the rule in a new situation, generating the next concrete experience) — learning that visits only one or two phases, such as theory without practice, does not stick. Honey and Mumford mapped preferred entry points onto four learning styles: activists learn by doing and dive into the experience; reflectors prefer to watch and mull it over; theorists want the model and the logic first; pragmatists engage when they can see the practical application. Most people favour one or two styles, and a complete pass round the cycle serves them all.

## Context & Usage

Shapes how the analyst designs training and go-live support for a business change: sessions that combine hands-on exercises (activists), demonstration and review time (reflectors), the underlying rationale (theorists) and job-relevant scenarios (pragmatists) land with a mixed audience where a lecture alone would not. The cycle also underpins the profession's own improvement loop — post-implementation reviews and retrospectives are institutionalised reflective observation and conceptualisation — and explains why pilots and prototypes teach stakeholders things a specification document never could: they supply the concrete experience the cycle starts from.
