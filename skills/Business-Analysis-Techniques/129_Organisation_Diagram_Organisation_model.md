# Organisation Diagram / Organisation model

## Overview

The familiar hierarchy chart — boxes for organisational units, roles or post-holders, lines for reporting relationships — read by an analyst as an analytical instrument rather than wall decoration. It reveals the structural design principle in force (functional, divisional/market-based, geographic, or matrix, where staff answer to both a functional and a project/product line), spans of control, layers between the front line and decision-makers, and, crucially, which unit boundaries the process under study must cross — every boundary crossing being a likely site of handoff delay, translation error and divided accountability. Modelling both the current and a proposed future structure makes the organisational impact of a change explicit and negotiable.

## Context & Usage

Consulted early to locate stakeholders, plan interviews and understand reporting politics, then again during solution design when a to-be process implies new roles, merged units or moved responsibilities. Two cautions recur: the published chart is frequently out of date, and it shows formal authority only — the informal networks through which work actually flows need social network analysis or observation to uncover. Pairs naturally with a RACI chart, which allocates specific responsibilities within the structure the diagram depicts.
