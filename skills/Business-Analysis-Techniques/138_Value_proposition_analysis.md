# Value proposition analysis

## Overview

Examines what an organisation actually offers its customers and why those customers choose it over alternatives. The analysis unpacks the proposition into components — the target customer segment, the product or service attributes offered, the benefits the customer receives (functional, financial, emotional), the price and effort demanded in return, and the points of differentiation from competitors. Attributes such as quality, availability, speed of service and brand image are rated for how much each segment values them versus how well the organisation delivers them, revealing where the proposition is strong, weak or over-engineered. Osterwalder's Value Proposition Canvas (customer jobs, pains and gains matched to products, pain relievers and gain creators) is a widely used elaboration of the same idea.

## Context & Usage

Applied during strategic analysis and when evaluating change options: any proposed redesign of processes, channels or pricing should be tested against the question "does this strengthen or erode what customers value about us?". BAs also use it to challenge inward-looking projects — a change that cuts internal cost but lengthens delivery times may damage the proposition of a segment that buys on speed. Evidence should come from customers (surveys, complaints, win/loss data), not from managers' assumptions about what customers want.
