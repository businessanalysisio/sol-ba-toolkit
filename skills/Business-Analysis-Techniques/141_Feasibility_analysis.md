# Feasibility analysis

## Overview

Screens each shortlisted option through three lenses before detailed investment analysis is spent on it. Business feasibility asks whether the option fits the organisation's strategy, culture, structure and appetite for change, and whether it can be absorbed alongside other initiatives. Technical feasibility asks whether the technology is proven and available, whether it can meet demands for performance, volume and security, and whether it is compatible with the existing architecture. Financial feasibility asks whether the organisation can fund the option — cash flow, borrowing capacity, payback horizon — irrespective of how attractive the eventual return looks. An option must clear all three hurdles; a variant framing widely used elsewhere is the TELOS checklist (Technical, Economic, Legal, Operational, Schedule).

## Context & Usage

Applied early in options evaluation as a coarse filter, saving full cost-benefit treatment for options that could actually be delivered. Findings feed directly into the business case, where infeasible options are recorded with the reason for rejection — evidence to decision-makers that alternatives were genuinely considered. Revisit feasibility whenever assumptions shift (a funding cut, a technology deprecation), since an option feasible at initiation can quietly become undeliverable.
