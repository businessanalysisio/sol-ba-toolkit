# Protocol analysis

## Overview

A think-aloud technique with roots in cognitive psychology (notably Ericsson and Simon's work on verbal reports): a subject performs a task while narrating every step and the reasoning behind it, and the recorded narration — the protocol — is then analysed to reconstruct the decision rules actually in use. Unlike an interview, which asks people to recall and generalise, protocol analysis captures reasoning at the moment it happens, making it one of the few ways to extract expertise that has become automatic and therefore invisible to its owner.

## Context & Usage

Applied when documenting complex judgement-based tasks — credit approvals, claims assessment, diagnostic triage, pricing exceptions — where the official procedure explains perhaps half of what experts really do. The BA turns the analysed protocol into explicit business rules, decision tables or training material. Two cautions: verbalising slows and can slightly alter the task, and experts sometimes rationalise rather than report; running several subjects on the same cases and comparing protocols controls for both.
