# Document analysis

## Overview

Systematic examination of the organisation's existing written artefacts — forms, reports, procedure manuals, system screens, correspondence, contracts — to extract data items, business rules, volumes and process evidence. A distinctive sub-practice is form analysis: studying completed forms rather than blank templates, because filled-in examples reveal which fields are actually used, which are habitually left empty or overwritten, and what marginal annotations compensate for the form's shortcomings. Each document examined is catalogued with its purpose, source, frequency and the data it carries, often feeding a data dictionary.

## Context & Usage

A low-cost, non-intrusive starting point for investigation: documents can be studied before stakeholder time is spent, and they yield precise detail (field names, codes, validation rules) that interviews rarely produce. It is essential groundwork for data modelling and for specifying migrations from paper or legacy systems. Its principal hazard is currency — manuals and procedures often describe a process several revisions out of date — so treat documents as evidence of intent and verify actual practice through observation or interview.
