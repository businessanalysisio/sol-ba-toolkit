# Value chain analysis / Porter's value chain

## Overview

Michael Porter's model (Competitive Advantage, 1985) of the firm as a chain of value-creating activities. Five primary activities move the offering towards the customer — inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, and service — while four support activities underpin them all: firm infrastructure, human resource management, technology development, and procurement. The margin the firm earns is the difference between the total value customers pay for and the collective cost of performing these activities, so analysis proceeds link by link: where is cost incurred, where is value added, and where could an activity be performed differently to gain cost or differentiation advantage?

## Context & Usage

Used in strategic context analysis to locate where a proposed change actually matters — an initiative that optimises an activity contributing little value may be effort misplaced. It also frames make-versus-buy and outsourcing questions (candidates are activities that are neither a source of differentiation nor cost advantage) and extends beyond the firm into supplier and channel value systems. BAs pair it with value stream mapping when they need to drop from the strategic view of activities to the operational flow of a specific product or service.
