# Critical success factors

## Overview

The small set of areas — usually no more than five to eight — in which an organisation must perform well for its mission and objectives to be achieved. The concept originates with D. Ronald Daniel (1961) and was developed into a management method by John Rockart at MIT Sloan in the late 1970s, who used executive interviews to distil what genuinely mattered from the noise of everything measured. CSFs may be industry-based (what every player must do), strategy-based (specific to the chosen competitive position), environmental or temporal, and each CSF is subsequently operationalised through one or more KPIs.

## Context & Usage

Derived during strategy analysis, typically by working down from mission and objectives: for each objective, ask "what must go right for this to be achieved?". The resulting CSF list gives the BA a prioritisation lens — requirements, options and process changes that support a CSF carry demonstrable strategic weight, while work that supports none deserves scrutiny. CSFs also anchor performance discussions in workshops because executives can rarely disagree with their own stated success conditions.
