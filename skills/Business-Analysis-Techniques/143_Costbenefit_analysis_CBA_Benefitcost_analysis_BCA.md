# Cost–benefit analysis (CBA) / Benefit–cost analysis (BCA)

## Overview

Sets out, for each option, everything it will cost against everything it will deliver, classified on a four-quadrant grid: costs and benefits are each split into tangible (expressible in money with confidence) and intangible (real but hard to monetise), and again into one-off/initial versus ongoing/recurring. Typical entries include hardware, licences and development effort (tangible one-off), support and subscription fees (tangible ongoing), staff savings and error reduction (tangible benefits), and improved morale, brand reputation or customer satisfaction (intangible benefits). The discipline lies in quantifying every tangible item, evidencing rather than inflating intangibles, and never double-counting a benefit that is really the same saving seen twice.

## Context & Usage

Core content of the business case, prepared per option so decision-makers compare like with like. The BA works with finance colleagues to agree costing conventions and with business managers to get benefit estimates owned by someone accountable for delivering them — an unowned benefit is a warning sign. Where money flows extend over years, the raw cost-benefit figures are handed to investment appraisal techniques (payback, NPV, IRR) to account for the time value of money.
