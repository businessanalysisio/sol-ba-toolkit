# Benefits categorisation / Benefits classification

## Overview

Sorts the claimed benefits of a change by how firmly each can be evidenced, forcing realism into the investment case. The widely used ladder (associated with Ward and Daniel's benefits-management work) runs: financial — a monetary value can be calculated with confidence; quantifiable — the size of improvement can be forecast but not yet reliably priced; measurable — a measure exists and the current baseline is known, but the scale of improvement cannot be predicted; and observable — improvement will be recognisable by agreed criteria or expert judgement only. Classification is deliberately conservative: a benefit sits at the lowest rung its evidence supports, and only the upper rungs can carry the financial justification.

## Context & Usage

Applied while assembling the business case, after cost-benefit analysis has listed candidate benefits and before investment appraisal consumes the financial ones. The categories drive behaviour: for each benefit the BA records its owner, its measure, its baseline, and the change activity that will cause it — and a benefit nobody will own or measure gets challenged out. The classification also structures the later benefits plan, since financial and quantifiable benefits get targets while measurable and observable ones get review criteria.
