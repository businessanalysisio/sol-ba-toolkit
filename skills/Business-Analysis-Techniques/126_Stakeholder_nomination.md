# Stakeholder nomination

## Overview

A snowballing identification method: each confirmed stakeholder is asked, usually at the end of an interview or workshop, "who else has an interest in this, and who else should we be talking to?" — and every nominee is asked the same question in turn, until the names being suggested are all already on the list. Its distinctive strength over checklist approaches is that it surfaces informal players an organisation chart never shows: the long-serving administrator who actually understands the process, the manager in another division who consumes the output, the external body whose approval is quietly assumed. Its distinctive weakness is inherited bias — people tend to nominate allies and colleagues who share their view, and rarely volunteer their opponents.

## Context & Usage

Used early in a project alongside — never instead of — systematic prompts such as the stakeholder wheel, which supplies the categories nominators forget (regulators, suppliers, competitors). Record for each nominee who nominated them and why, since that reveals the informal network itself; then assess the consolidated list on a power/interest grid. Deliberately probe for missing voices: "who will be worst affected?", "who might oppose this?" — questions that counteract the technique's tendency to assemble a friendly audience.
