# Facilitated workshops / Joint requirements planning workshops / Joint Application Development Workshops

## Overview

Structured group sessions in which selected stakeholders work together, under a neutral facilitator, to reach agreed outputs — requirements, scope decisions, process designs — in hours rather than the weeks that serial interviews take. The JAD (Joint Application Development) form was pioneered at IBM in the late 1970s by Chuck Morris and Tony Crawford. Defined roles are part of the technique: facilitator (guides process, stays neutral on content), scribe (records decisions and actions), participants (empowered decision-makers), and sponsor. Within the session the facilitator deploys sub-techniques — brainstorming, round-robin, sticky-note affinity grouping, breakout tasks — against a pre-published agenda and ground rules.

## Context & Usage

The workhorse of collaborative elicitation and decision-making: use one when several stakeholder groups must converge (conflicting views get resolved in the room rather than relayed by the BA), when speed matters, or when buy-in to the output is as important as the output itself. Success is mostly determined before the day: clear objective, right attendees with authority to decide, pre-circulated materials. Follow up promptly with the documented outputs and actions, because unconfirmed workshop decisions decay quickly.
