# Gap analysis

## Overview

Systematically compares a desired future state against the existing situation and itemises the differences as gaps to be closed. The comparison is anchored by two artifacts: a model of the target — often a Business Activity Model or to-be process design — set against evidence of the current state, examined dimension by dimension. A common frame is POPIT: Processes, Organisation, People (skills, staffing, motivation) and Information/Technology. Each gap is recorded with its size, its business consequence, and candidate actions to close it, turning a vague "we need to improve" into a concrete change agenda.

## Context & Usage

Performed after the current state has been investigated and a future-state view agreed, and before options are formulated — the catalogued gaps are the raw material from which options and requirements are built. In package selection the same mechanics run in reverse: candidate software is scored against the requirements catalogue, and the shortfall list drives configure/customise/change-the-process decisions. Two disciplines matter most: describe the future state in measurable terms (otherwise a gap cannot be sized), and resist proposing solutions while gaps are still being listed.
