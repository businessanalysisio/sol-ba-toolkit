# McKinsey 7-S

## Overview

An organisational alignment framework developed at McKinsey by consultants including Tom Peters and Robert Waterman around 1980. It examines seven interdependent elements: three "hard" S's (Strategy, Structure, Systems) and four "soft" S's (Shared values, Skills, Style, Staff), conventionally drawn as a web with shared values at the centre. The core claim is that the elements must reinforce one another — a strategy change that leaves skills, style and systems untouched will stall, because the soft elements pull the organisation back to its old equilibrium.

## Context & Usage

BAs reach for 7-S when diagnosing why performance lags or when assessing the ripple effects of a proposed change: walk each S, describe its current state, then test whether the seven descriptions are mutually consistent and consistent with the intended future state. Misalignments become candidate problem areas or change requirements. It works well as a workshop checklist during situation investigation and as a completeness check on a change programme's scope — especially for exposing neglected soft elements.
