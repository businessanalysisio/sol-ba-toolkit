# Planning Poker

## Overview

A consensus-based estimation game devised by James Grenning (2002) and popularised by Mike Cohn, used by agile teams to size user stories in relative units (story points). Each estimator holds a deck of cards carrying a modified Fibonacci sequence — typically 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 20, 40, 100 — whose widening gaps acknowledge that big items cannot be sized precisely. A story is read out and discussed briefly, then everyone reveals a card simultaneously; the simultaneous reveal is the point of the ritual, preventing anchoring on the first or loudest opinion. When estimates diverge, the highest and lowest estimators explain their reasoning — often exposing a hidden assumption or forgotten complexity — and the team re-votes until it converges.

## Context & Usage

Run during backlog refinement or sprint planning with the whole delivery team, since the people doing the work give the estimates. The BA or product owner attends to answer scope questions, not to vote. Beyond the numbers, the technique's real yield is the conversation: a 3-versus-20 split on the same story is a requirements defect detector. Items that stay large after discussion are split before they enter a sprint; teams needing faster coverage of a big backlog switch to derivatives such as affinity estimating and return to poker for contentious items.
