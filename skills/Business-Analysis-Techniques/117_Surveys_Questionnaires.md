# Surveys / Questionnaires

## Overview

A standardised set of written questions issued to a large or geographically dispersed population, trading the depth of an interview for breadth and comparability. Question design carries the technique: closed questions (multiple choice, ranking, Likert-type rating scales) yield quantifiable results, while a small number of open questions capture issues the designer did not anticipate. Good practice includes piloting the instrument with a handful of respondents before full release, keeping completion time short, deciding sampling and anonymity up front, and planning the analysis before writing the questions — a question that cannot be analysed should not be asked.

## Context & Usage

Chosen when the analyst needs input from more people than can feasibly be interviewed — branch networks, whole user populations, customer bases — or wants statistically presentable evidence of opinion or practice. Response rates are the perennial weakness (often well under half without active chasing), and self-selection can skew results toward the disgruntled or the enthusiastic. Surveys therefore work best in combination: interviews and workshops to discover the issues, a survey to measure how widely each issue is felt.
