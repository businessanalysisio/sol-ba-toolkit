# Kotter's approach to change

## Overview

John Kotter's eight-step sequence for leading major change, set out in *Leading Change* (1996) from his study of why transformation efforts fail: (1) establish a sense of urgency — without it nothing starts; (2) form a powerful guiding coalition with enough position, expertise and credibility to drive the effort; (3) create a vision and strategy that can be grasped quickly; (4) communicate the vision relentlessly, through word and deed; (5) empower broad-based action by removing structural obstacles and disempowering blockers; (6) generate short-term wins — visible, unambiguous improvements within months, deliberately planned rather than hoped for; (7) consolidate gains and produce more change, resisting the premature declaration of victory; (8) anchor the new approaches in the culture so they survive leadership turnover. Kotter's core warning is that skipping steps creates only the illusion of speed.

## Context & Usage

Used as a programme-level roadmap and, for the analyst, as a diagnostic checklist when a change effort stalls — the symptom usually traces to a skipped step (no real urgency, a coalition without clout, wins never made visible). Steps map naturally onto BA contributions: the case for urgency draws on gap and root-cause analysis, the vision on the business case, short-term wins on incremental delivery and benefits tracking. Compared with Lewin's three stages, Kotter elaborates the same arc into actionable leadership behaviours, and the two are frequently used together.
