# Capability modelling

## Overview

A business-architecture technique that maps what an organisation is able to do — its capabilities — independently of who performs them, how they are executed, or which systems support them. Capabilities (e.g. "customer onboarding", "demand forecasting") are decomposed hierarchically, commonly to two or three levels, into a capability map. Because capabilities are far more stable than processes, org structures or technologies, the map provides a durable anchor: analysts overlay heat-mapping (colour-coding each capability by performance, strategic importance or investment level) to show where change money should go.

## Context & Usage

Used in strategy analysis and portfolio planning to connect strategic objectives to concrete investment decisions: which capabilities must be new, uplifted or retired to deliver the strategy. BAs also use capability maps to scope programmes ("this initiative uplifts these three level-2 capabilities"), to spot duplication where several divisions build the same capability separately, and to define a target operating model. Keep capability names noun-based and implementation-free — the moment a capability is named after a system or department, the map loses its stability.
