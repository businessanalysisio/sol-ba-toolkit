# Risk analysis / Risk identification / Risk management

## Overview

A three-stage cycle applied to the uncertain events that could derail an option or project. Identification captures each risk as a cause-and-effect statement ("because supplier X is new to us, delivery may slip, delaying go-live"). Assessment rates every risk for probability and impact — commonly on a 3x3 or 5x5 grid whose product gives a severity score used to rank attention. Response planning then assigns each significant risk an owner and a strategy from the standard set: avoid (remove the cause), reduce (lower probability or impact), transfer (insurance, contract terms), or accept — with contingency plans prepared for accepted risks that would hurt badly if they materialised. The register is a living document, re-reviewed as circumstances change, not a one-off appendix.

## Context & Usage

In business analysis work, risk analysis is applied per option during evaluation — decision-makers weigh each option's risk profile against its returns — and again at project level once an option is chosen. BAs contribute the business risks that technical registers miss: stakeholder resistance, benefit erosion, regulatory exposure, dependency on other change initiatives. A practical test of register quality: every high-severity risk has a named owner and a dated action, and no entry is written so vaguely ("project may fail") that no countermeasure could address it.
