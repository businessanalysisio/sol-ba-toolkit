# Timesheets

## Overview

A self-recording investigation method in which staff themselves log how their working time is spent, against activity categories defined by the analyst, over an agreed period (commonly one or two representative weeks). The design of the recording form is the technique's craft: categories must be few, mutually exclusive and meaningful to the people filling them in, with a granularity (e.g. 15- or 30-minute slots) coarse enough not to burden the work being measured. Compared with activity sampling, timesheets need no observer and cover every hour, but they trade observer bias for self-report bias — people under-record unofficial or embarrassing activities.

## Context & Usage

Chosen when quantitative effort data is needed across many people or locations where stationing observers is impractical, or when work is intermittent and would be missed by spot observations. The resulting effort profile feeds staffing analyses, activity-based costing and the "cost of the current process" line in a business case. Brief participants on purpose and confidentiality first — timesheets introduced without explanation are read as surveillance and the data quality collapses — and cross-check totals against known workload volumes.
