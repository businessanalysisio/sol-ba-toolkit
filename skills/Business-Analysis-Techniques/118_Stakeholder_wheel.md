# Stakeholder wheel

## Overview

A stakeholder-identification aid, associated with the BCS business analysis school of Paul and Cadle, that arranges generic stakeholder categories as segments of a wheel around the project or organisation: customers, partners, suppliers, competitors, regulators, owners, employees and managers. The wheel functions as a completeness prompt — the analyst walks each segment in turn asking "who, specifically, fills this category for this change?" — so that whole classes of stakeholder (regulators and competitors are the habitual omissions) are not forgotten simply because nobody in the room represents them.

## Context & Usage

Used at the start of stakeholder analysis, typically in a short workshop, to generate the raw stakeholder list before any assessment of power, interest or attitude. Each generic segment usually expands into several concrete stakeholders (e.g. "regulators" becomes the data-protection authority, the industry regulator and internal audit), and some individuals appear in more than one segment. The populated list then feeds the power/interest grid and the stakeholder management plan. Revisit the wheel at each project stage — stakeholder populations change as a project moves from design to implementation.
