# Prototyping

## Overview

Puts a partial working model of the solution in front of users so requirements emerge from reaction rather than abstraction — the antidote to "I'll know it when I see it". Prototypes span a fidelity spectrum, from paper mock-ups and clickable wireframes to functioning software slices, and divide on a crucial design decision: throwaway prototypes exist purely to learn and are discarded once they have answered their question, while evolutionary prototypes are grown, iteration by iteration, into the delivered system. Each cycle follows the same loop — build the next version, let users exercise it against realistic scenarios, harvest the corrections and omissions, refine.

## Context & Usage

Most valuable where requirements are uncertain, novel or contested, and for eliciting the tacit knowledge users cannot articulate in interviews — people who cannot describe their needs will readily criticise a screen that gets them wrong. The BA facilitates sessions with prepared scenarios and records what the reactions reveal about requirements, not just about cosmetics. Two managed risks come with the technique: stakeholders mistaking a slick prototype for a nearly finished system (set expectations explicitly), and endless polishing cycles (timebox the iterations and agree exit criteria up front).
