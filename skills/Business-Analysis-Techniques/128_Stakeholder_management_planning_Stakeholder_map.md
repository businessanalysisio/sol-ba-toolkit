# Stakeholder management planning / Stakeholder map

## Overview

Turns a stakeholder assessment into a deliberate engagement strategy. From each stakeholder's position on the power/interest map follows a baseline stance — high power/high interest: constant, active management as key players, ideally partners in the work; high power/low interest: keep satisfied and watch for their interest awakening; low power/high interest: keep informed and use as a source of detail and goodwill; low power/low interest: minimal effort beyond monitoring. The stakeholder management plan then records, per stakeholder or segment: current versus desired attitude (e.g. opponent → neutral → advocate), the issues that matter to them, planned actions and messages, communication channel and frequency, and a named relationship owner — often the sponsor or SRO for the most senior figures rather than the analyst.

## Context & Usage

Produced once the stakeholder population has been identified and assessed, and treated as a living, politically sensitive document — stakeholder positions shift as the project starts to bite, so the map is re-scored at each phase boundary or major announcement. The plan is deliberately confidential in its franker parts ("CFO is a blocker; route through the audit committee"), which is why many teams keep a publishable communication plan separate from the private management strategy. Its test of success is movement: stakeholders migrating toward the attitude the plan targeted.
