# User stories

## Overview

The agile unit of requirement: a short statement in the canonical form "As a <role>, I want <capability>, so that <benefit>", small enough to fit on a card and deliberately incomplete — Ron Jeffries' "three Cs" capture its lifecycle as Card (the written token), Conversation (the discussion that fills in the detail just in time) and Confirmation (the acceptance criteria that prove it done). Story quality is tested against Bill Wake's INVEST checklist: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable. The "so that" clause is not decoration; a story whose benefit cannot be articulated is a solution looking for a justification, and stories too big to build in one iteration are split (by workflow step, business rule, data variation) rather than fattened into specifications.

## Context & Usage

Used as backlog currency in agile deliveries: the product owner prioritises them, the team estimates them (often via Planning Poker), and each is elaborated through conversation shortly before build. BAs add value by keeping stories vertical slices of user value rather than technical tasks, attaching Given-When-Then acceptance criteria, and grouping related stories under epics and themes so the big picture survives the decomposition. Stories complement rather than replace other models — data models, business rules and NFRs still need homes of their own.
