# Business process modelling / Process maps / Swimlane diagrams

## Overview

Maps an end-to-end business process as a swimlane diagram: one horizontal lane per actor or organisational unit, the triggering business event at the left, tasks placed in the lane of whoever performs them, flow lines showing sequence and decisions, and the business outcome at the right. The layout makes handoffs — every point where the flow crosses a lane boundary — visually unmissable, and handoffs are where processes go wrong: queues form, context is lost, work bounces back, and no single actor owns the delay. Modelled at more than one level (an organisation-level value-chain view decomposing into individual process maps, whose tasks may decompose further into task models), with 'as-is' models capturing today's reality and 'to-be' models expressing the redesign.

## Context & Usage

The workhorse of process improvement work: build the as-is model from interviews and, critically, observation (people describe the official process, then do something else), annotate it with timings, volumes and problem hotspots, then apply redesign thinking to draft the to-be. Analysis questions follow directly from the notation — can handoffs be eliminated by widening a role? are checks worth their delay? which tasks add no value the customer would pay for? Keep each map to one triggering event and one page; sprawl signals the scoping step (business event analysis) was skipped.
