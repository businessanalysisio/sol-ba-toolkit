# Impact analysis

## Overview

Traces the knock-on consequences a proposed option or change would have across the organisation before anyone commits to it. The examination sweeps a checklist of impact areas: organisation structure and reporting lines, cross-functional working and hand-offs, business processes and procedures, people (roles, skills, headcount, working patterns), customers and suppliers, premises and infrastructure, and other in-flight projects competing for the same resources. Each identified impact is recorded with who is affected, how severely, and what mitigating or preparatory action is needed — distinguishing it from risk analysis, which deals with uncertain events, whereas impacts are consequences that will happen if the option proceeds.

## Context & Usage

Performed for every shortlisted option during business case preparation, where the impacts section sits alongside costs, benefits and risks; an option with attractive economics can still fail on unacceptable organisational disruption. The technique reappears later in the lifecycle whenever a requirements change is requested: the BA assesses what the modification would do to related requirements, designs, builds, tests and the schedule before the change authority decides. Interviewing the managers of affected areas beats desk speculation — they see consequences the project team cannot.
