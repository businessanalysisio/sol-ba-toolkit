# Business Activity Model (BAM)

## Overview

A conceptual model, drawn from Checkland's Soft Systems Methodology, of the activities a business system must logically carry out from the standpoint of one stakeholder's world-view — what the business *should do*, not how it currently does it. A BAM contains five kinds of activity linked by dependency arrows: doing activities (the core transformation that delivers value), enabling activities (obtain and prepare resources), planning activities (decide what to do and how), monitoring activities (gather performance data against the plans), and controlling activities (take corrective action when monitoring reveals a shortfall). Because each model is built from a single perspective (often articulated via CATWOE), several BAMs are usually produced and then negotiated into a single consensus model the stakeholders can all accept.

## Context & Usage

The consensus BAM earns its keep when the real organisation is compared against it: activities that exist in the model but are absent, weak or unsupported in practice become business issues and candidate requirements, making the BAM a bridge from soft, perspective-laden investigation to hard gap analysis. It is deliberately abstract — no departments, job titles or IT systems appear — which is what lets stakeholders debate purpose without defending territory. Typical placement is after rich-picture investigation and perspective analysis, before process modelling and requirements definition.
