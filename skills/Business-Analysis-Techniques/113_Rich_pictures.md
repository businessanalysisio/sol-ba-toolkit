# Rich pictures

## Overview

A free-format, cartoon-style drawing of a problem situation, originating in Peter Checkland's Soft Systems Methodology at Lancaster University. There is deliberately no fixed notation: the analyst sketches actors, relationships, processes, external pressures and — crucially — the soft elements formal models exclude, using conventions such as crossed swords for conflict and think bubbles for private concerns and worldviews. The point is holism: one page that captures the mess as it is experienced, including politics and emotion, before any attempt to impose structure on it.

## Context & Usage

Drawn early in situation investigation, ideally with stakeholders contributing at a whiteboard, when the problem is tangled and different parties describe it differently. The act of drawing forces surfacing of relationships and tensions that would stay unsaid in a requirements list, and the finished picture becomes a shared reference for agreeing which parts of the mess the project will address. In SSM it precedes the definition of relevant systems and Business Activity Models. Resist tidying it up — a rich picture that looks like a process diagram has lost its purpose.
