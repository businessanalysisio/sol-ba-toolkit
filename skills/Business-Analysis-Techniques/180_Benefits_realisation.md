# Benefits realisation

## Overview

The back end of the benefits lifecycle: the work done after a solution goes live to convert its potential into measured, banked improvement. Centrepiece is the benefits review (or post-implementation review), scheduled once the change has bedded in — long enough after go-live for the learning-curve dip to pass, commonly three to six months — at which actual results are compared against the baseline and the targets in the business case, benefit by benefit. Three verdicts follow per benefit: realised (bank it and report it), shortfall (diagnose — the usual cause is that the enabling business change never fully happened — and commission remedial action, since benefits can often still be recovered), or emergent (unplanned benefits discovered in use, which are legitimate to claim once evidenced). Findings feed lessons learned and recalibrate the organisation's habitual optimism in future business cases.

## Context & Usage

Owned by the business, with benefit owners reporting against their own targets, but frequently facilitated by a business analyst — one of the few roles present from original case to final review and therefore able to keep the measures honest and comparable. Two disciplines decide whether the review is real or theatre: baselines captured before the change (without a "before", no "after" means anything), and separation from the project post-mortem — reviewing whether delivery ran well is a different question from whether the investment paid. In portfolio-managed organisations, realisation results gate future funding for the same sponsors.
