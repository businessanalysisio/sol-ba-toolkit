# Social network analysis / Sociometry / Network analysis

## Overview

Mapping the informal organisation: people (and sometimes teams or systems) are drawn as nodes, and their actual communication, advice-seeking or influence relationships as connecting edges, producing a sociogram that can be analysed qualitatively or with graph metrics. Sociometry — Jacob Moreno's technique from the 1930s — is the ancestor; modern network analysis adds measures such as degree centrality (how connected a person is) and betweenness centrality (how often a person sits on the path between others), which identify hubs, brokers, gatekeepers and isolated clusters. The map almost never matches the organisation chart, and the differences are the findings.

## Context & Usage

Applied when a change depends on how information and influence really travel: choosing champions for adoption (high-centrality individuals move opinion faster than formal managers), spotting single points of failure where one broker connects two departments, or diagnosing why decisions stall between silos. Data comes from short surveys ("who do you go to for advice on X?"), meeting and email metadata, or workshop elicitation. Handle results with care — a sociogram names people and exposes their informal standing, which raises confidentiality and ethics obligations beyond most BA artefacts.
