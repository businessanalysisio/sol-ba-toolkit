# Requirements validation

## Overview

The formal quality gate at which stakeholders examine the documented requirements and agree they are fit to build from. Reviewers — business owners, users, developers, testers, and where relevant architects and compliance — each read from their own angle, applying the classic defect checklist: is each requirement unambiguous, atomic, testable, feasible, correctly prioritised, free of embedded design, consistent with the others, and is anything missing? Review formats range in rigour from informal peer read-through, to structured walkthrough (author presents, reviewers challenge), to formal inspection with defined roles, defect logging and re-review; the outcome is an agreed action list and, once actions are cleared, sign-off that baselines the requirement set.

## Context & Usage

Scheduled before the requirements are committed to — before contract, before development of an increment — because a defect corrected at review costs a fraction of the same defect discovered in test or production. Prototypes, scenarios walked end-to-end, and drafting acceptance criteria all strengthen validation by forcing concreteness that reading alone never achieves. The known pathologies are sign-off theatre (approvals from people who never read the document) and review fatigue on large sets — countered by validating in slices, giving reviewers specific perspectives to check, and making the sign-off's meaning explicit.
