# Outcome Frame / Outcome orientation / Outcome thinking

## Overview

A questioning discipline, borrowed from neuro-linguistic programming's "well-formed outcome", that swaps the problem frame ("what's wrong, whose fault is it, why does it keep happening?") for an outcome frame built from a specific question set: What do you want? — stated positively, since "less rework" names a problem while "right first time by March" names a destination; How will you know when you have it? — sensory, measurable evidence; Where, when and with whom do you want it (and not want it)? — the context that bounds scope; What resources do you have and need?; Is achieving it within your control or influence?; and the ecology check — what else would getting this outcome affect, and is every consequence acceptable? An outcome that survives all the questions is well-formed; most first statements do not.

## Context & Usage

Deployed whenever a discussion is circling a problem instead of moving toward a result — a workshop stuck in blame, a sponsor whose objective is "sort out the order process", a stakeholder demanding a solution ("we need a new CRM") whose desired outcome nobody has stated. For the analyst it doubles as an objective-quality tool: running a proposed project objective through the questions exposes vagueness, missing measures and unowned dependencies before they are baked into a business case, complementing SMART-style objective definition with the evidence and ecology angles SMART lacks.
