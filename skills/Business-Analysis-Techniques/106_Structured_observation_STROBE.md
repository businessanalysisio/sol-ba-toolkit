# Structured observation / STROBE

## Overview

Observation conducted against a predefined checklist of things to look for, so that findings from different sites or people are comparable. STROBE — STRuctured OBservation of the Environment, devised by Kendall and Kendall in the systems-analysis field — directs the analyst's attention to concrete physical cues in a decision-maker's workplace: office location and accessibility, desk placement, fixed and portable equipment, props such as manuals and trade journals, external information sources, and even lighting and dress. Each cue is read as evidence about how the person gathers information and makes decisions, which the analyst then compares against what interviews claimed.

## Context & Usage

Used to corroborate or challenge interview findings cheaply: if a manager claims to rely on the corporate dashboard but the desk shows annotated paper printouts and a wall of sticky notes, the real information flow differs from the stated one. The structured checklist makes short visits productive and lets several analysts gather consistent evidence across departments. Treat individual cues as hypotheses, not verdicts — physical evidence suggests behaviour, and follow-up questions confirm it.
