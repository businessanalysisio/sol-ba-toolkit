# Balanced Business Scorecard

## Overview

Kaplan and Norton's performance-measurement framework (introduced in a 1992 Harvard Business Review article) that counterbalances purely financial reporting with three further perspectives: customer, internal business process, and learning & growth. Under each perspective the organisation defines objectives, measures, targets and initiatives, and the perspectives are causally linked — investment in learning improves processes, better processes improve customer outcomes, and satisfied customers drive financial results. The "balance" is between lagging financial results and the leading indicators that predict them.

## Context & Usage

For a BA the scorecard serves two purposes. In strategy analysis it translates objectives (from MOST or similar) into a measurable framework, showing whether a proposed change actually moves a metric the organisation cares about. In benefits work it supplies the measurement scaffolding for a business case: each claimed benefit should map to a scorecard measure with a baseline and target. It is also a diagnostic — an organisation whose scorecard is all financial measures is likely under-managing capability and customer health.
