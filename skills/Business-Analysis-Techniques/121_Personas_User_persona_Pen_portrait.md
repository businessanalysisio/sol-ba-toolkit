# Personas / User persona / Pen portrait

## Overview

A fictional but research-grounded archetype standing in for a class of users or customers, popularised by Alan Cooper in interaction design in the late 1990s. A persona is given a name, a face, and a one-page profile covering goals, behaviours, skills, frustrations, context of use and representative quotes — all synthesised from real interview, observation or survey data about the segment it represents. The pen portrait is the prose variant: a short narrative description of a typical member of a stakeholder group. The concrete, humanised format is the mechanism: teams design and prioritise better for "Marta, a night-shift dispatcher juggling three screens" than for "user type B".

## Context & Usage

Built after user analysis has segmented the population, and then used throughout: requirements are checked against each persona's goals, scenarios and user stories are written from a persona's viewpoint, prototypes are evaluated by walking a persona through them, and prioritisation debates get resolved by asking which persona a feature serves. Two or three primary personas usually suffice; a wall of ten dilutes focus. The cardinal sin is invention — a persona assembled from team assumptions rather than field data simply launders prejudice into design authority.
