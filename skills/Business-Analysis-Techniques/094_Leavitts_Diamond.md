# Leavitt's Diamond

## Overview

Harold Leavitt's 1965 socio-technical model of organisational change, drawn as a diamond whose four corners — task, people, structure and technology — are all connected to each other. Its central insight: the four variables are mutually dependent, so altering one corner (say, introducing new technology) forces compensating adjustments in the other three (the tasks people perform, the skills they need, and the structure they work within). It is an ancestor of later holistic models such as POPIT and McKinsey 7-S.

## Context & Usage

Useful early in impact assessment, when a change is being framed narrowly as "an IT project" or "a restructure". The BA traces each proposed intervention around the diamond and asks what each other corner must look like for the change to hold — surfacing training needs, role redesign and process changes that would otherwise appear late as surprises. Its simplicity (only four elements) makes it a quick whiteboard tool with senior stakeholders before applying more detailed frameworks.
