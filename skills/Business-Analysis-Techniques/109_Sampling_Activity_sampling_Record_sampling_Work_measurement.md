# Sampling / Activity sampling / Record sampling / Work measurement

## Overview

Quantitative investigation techniques that estimate whole-population facts from a statistically chosen subset. In activity sampling, an observer records what each member of a work group is doing at random or fixed instants over a period; the proportion of observations in which an activity appears estimates the proportion of total time it consumes, with sample size chosen for the desired confidence level and error margin. Record sampling applies the same logic to documents and transactions — inspecting a sample of orders, claims or invoices to estimate volumes, values and error rates. Work measurement times defined tasks to establish standard durations for costing and capacity planning.

## Context & Usage

Used when a business case or process redesign needs numbers rather than impressions: how much effort the as-is process really consumes, how often exceptions occur, what an error rate costs. These figures baseline the current state so that projected savings can be defended in investment appraisal. Randomise observation instants (staff quickly learn a predictable schedule), and treat results as estimates with stated confidence, not exact measurements.
