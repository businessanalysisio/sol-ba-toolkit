# Contextual inquiry

## Overview

A hybrid of interview and observation from Beyer and Holtzblatt's Contextual Design method: the analyst sits with users at their own workplace while they perform real tasks, questioning as the work unfolds. Four principles define it. Context — study work where and while it happens, not in a meeting room. Partnership — adopt a master–apprentice relationship in which the user is the expert teaching the analyst. Interpretation — share your interpretations in the moment so the user can correct them. Focus — steer the conversation with a defined project focus so sessions stay relevant. Sessions are typically 1.5–2 hours with a handful of representative users.

## Context & Usage

Best suited to understanding detailed work practice ahead of designing systems or redesigning processes — it captures the sequence, artefacts, intents and breakdowns of real tasks with far more fidelity than retrospective interviews. BAs commonly consolidate findings into task models, scenarios or personas. Because interpretation happens live and is validated by the user, contextual inquiry produces fewer analyst misreadings than pure observation, at the cost of some intrusiveness into normal work.
