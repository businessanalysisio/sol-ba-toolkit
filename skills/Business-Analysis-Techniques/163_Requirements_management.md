# Requirements management

## Overview

The discipline that keeps a requirement set trustworthy from elicitation through to delivery and beyond. Its standard elements: unique identification (every requirement referenceable forever, identifiers never reused); traceability (each requirement linked backward to its source and business objective and forward to design, build and test artefacts); recorded origin and ownership (who raised it, who may change it); change control — a proposed change is documented, its impact assessed via the traceability links, and it is accepted or rejected by an authority, never edited in quietly; configuration management and baselining, so everyone knows which version of the set a given release was built against; and software support, since maintaining cross-references by hand collapses beyond a few dozen requirements.

## Context & Usage

Proportionate to risk: a contractual, safety-related or multi-supplier programme needs the full apparatus, while an in-house agile team may realise the same principles through a tool-managed backlog with story history. The recurring failure mode is not the absence of process but its silent bypass — undocumented "small changes" agreed in corridors — so the analyst's job is partly cultural: making the change-control route faster and more predictable than the workaround. Requirements management is also what makes scope disputes resolvable, since the baseline records what was actually agreed.
