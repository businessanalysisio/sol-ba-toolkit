# Timeboxing

## Overview

Inverts the traditional project trade-off: the deadline and resources are fixed, and scope is the variable that flexes. Work is organised into timeboxes of set duration — Scrum sprints of one to four weeks are the best-known instance; DSDM structures each timebox with a kick-off, then investigation, refinement and consolidation phases, and a close-out where the outcome is formally accepted. Because the end date cannot move, every timebox needs prioritised content (MoSCoW is the usual companion): when time runs short, Could-haves drop first, Should-haves next, and the Must-haves define the guaranteed minimum the timebox will still deliver.

## Context & Usage

Used wherever momentum and predictability beat exhaustive completeness — iterative delivery, requirements elicitation phases, spikes and investigations, even individual meetings. The discipline counters two chronic failure modes: analysis paralysis (the investigation that never ends) and deadline slippage justified by "just one more feature". It works only with genuinely prioritised scope, empowered decision-making inside the box, and the nerve not to extend; a timebox that gets extended whenever content is unfinished is simply a deadline wearing a fashionable name.
