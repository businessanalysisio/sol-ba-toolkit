# Key performance indicators

## Overview

Quantified measures that track performance against critical success factors — the measurable evidence that a CSF is being met or missed. A well-formed KPI has a precise definition (formula and data source), a baseline, a target, an owner, and a measurement frequency; without these it is a slogan, not an indicator. KPIs divide into lagging indicators (results already achieved, such as quarterly revenue) and leading indicators (predictors of future results, such as pipeline conversion rate), and a healthy measurement set mixes both. Each CSF normally spawns a small number of KPIs; dozens of KPIs per factor signals measurement theatre.

## Context & Usage

BAs define or refine KPIs whenever a change must prove its worth: baseline the KPI in the current state, set the target in the business case, and re-measure after implementation to confirm benefits. KPIs also drive requirements — reporting, data capture and data-quality requirements frequently exist only so a KPI can be computed. Watch for perverse incentives: a call-centre KPI of "average handle time" alone will shorten calls at the expense of resolution, so pair efficiency measures with quality counterweights.
