# Background reading / Background research / Report analysis

## Overview

Desk research carried out before engaging stakeholders directly: studying the organisation's annual report and accounts, strategy and mission statements, organisation charts, board and steering-group minutes, procedure manuals, marketing material, the company website and intranet, prior project documentation, and external commentary such as analyst or press coverage. From these the analyst extracts three kinds of raw material — candidate stakeholders (names attached to functions, sign-offs and committees), the organisation's own vocabulary, and the officially stated objectives, structures and processes against which observed reality can later be compared. The annual report alone typically yields the senior stakeholder cast, the financial pressures of the moment and the strategic language executives will expect the project to echo.

## Context & Usage

The cheapest investigation technique and the polite prerequisite for the others: arriving at a first interview already fluent in the terminology and structure signals respect and buys credibility, and questions can target what documents cannot say instead of what they already do. Treat everything read as a hypothesis, not a fact — documents describe the official position at the date of writing, and procedure manuals in particular are notorious for describing a process nobody has followed for years. Findings feed the stakeholder wheel, nomination interviews and the investigation plan.
