# Mind maps / Concept maps / Semantic networks / Webs

## Overview

Radial and network diagrams for capturing and organising ideas. A mind map — the form popularised by Tony Buzan — places a central topic in the middle and grows a hierarchy of branches outward, each branch carrying a single keyword or short phrase, with colour and imagery used to aid recall. Concept maps (developed by Joseph Novak) generalise this into a network: nodes hold concepts and the connecting lines are labelled with the relationship ("causes", "requires", "is part of"), forming propositions that can be read as sentences. Semantic networks and webs are the same idea taken further, allowing many-to-many linked structures rather than a single hierarchy.

## Context & Usage

BAs use mind maps to structure interview and workshop notes in real time, to decompose a scope or problem area, and to brainstorm without losing the thread — the hierarchy imposes just enough order while staying fast to draw. Concept maps suit knowledge capture where relationships matter as much as topics, such as untangling business terminology before glossary or data modelling work. Both formats double as communication artefacts: a one-page map of an investigation is frequently more digestible for stakeholders than the report behind it.
