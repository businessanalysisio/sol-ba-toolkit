# Wireframes

## Overview

A skeletal, deliberately unstyled layout of a single screen or page: plain boxes and lines marking where the navigation, headings, content areas, data fields, buttons and messages will sit, usually in greyscale with placeholder text. What a wireframe specifies is structure and content — what appears on the screen, its grouping and relative prominence, and the actions available — while what it withholds is visual design, precisely so review conversations stay on "is the right information here?" instead of "I don't like that blue". Annotations along the margin carry the behaviour: validation rules, field sources, what each button does, conditional display logic.

## Context & Usage

Produced while detailing functional requirements for anything with a user interface, typically after storyboards have settled the journey and before higher-fidelity prototypes or visual design begin. Reviewing wireframes with users catches missing data items, wrong groupings and unusable workflows at the cheapest possible moment, and the annotated versions hand developers an unambiguous brief. Keep them honest about their fidelity: a wireframe polished to look finished invites stakeholders to believe the system is nearly built.
