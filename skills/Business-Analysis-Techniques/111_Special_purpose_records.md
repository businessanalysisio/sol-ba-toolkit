# Special-purpose records

## Overview

Bespoke recording instruments — tally sheets, occurrence logs, simple count cards — designed by the analyst for one specific investigation question and kept by the staff doing the work. A typical example: a five-bar-gate tally sheet on which counter staff mark every telephone interruption, or a log capturing each occasion a particular form is rejected and why. Unlike timesheets, which profile all working time, a special-purpose record counts one phenomenon precisely, over a defined window, at almost zero cost. The instrument's simplicity is deliberate: anything that takes more than a few seconds to complete will stop being filled in.

## Context & Usage

Ideal when an investigation hinges on the frequency of something no existing system records — interruptions, workarounds, informal queries, categories of error. The analyst designs the sheet, agrees the recording period, briefs the recorders on definitions (what counts as one occurrence), and collects and collates the results into evidence for problem analysis or a business case. Keep the window short and representative, avoid weeks with unusual events, and pilot the sheet with one or two people first to flush out ambiguous categories.
