# Deciding Whether You Need a Manager

## Overview

The usual trigger is span of control: once a founder has more than roughly five to eight direct reports, or spends most of the week supervising rather than selling and building, coordination starts eating the hours that should go to strategy. The decision then has three parts. First, diagnose whether the problem is genuinely managerial — clearer role definitions, better processes, or simple delegation sometimes fix it without adding a salary. Second, choose between promoting from within (cheaper, culture-preserving, but the best operator is frequently not the best manager) and hiring externally (brings experience the team lacks, at a salary typically well above the people being managed and with real onboarding risk). Third, be explicit about what authority actually transfers: a manager without decision rights is an expensive messenger, and a founder who keeps overriding them will drive them out within a year.

## Context & Usage

Revisit the question at each headcount step-change — around five, ten, and twenty staff — or when the symptoms appear: decisions queuing on the founder's desk, staff unsure whom to ask, quality drifting because nobody owns supervision. It also arises when the founder wants to step back from operations; in that case, write the manager's remit down before recruiting, not after.
