# Coming Up with Your Idea

## Overview

The disciplined generation and capture of business ideas from repeatable sources rather than waiting for inspiration: personal frustrations ("why is there no decent X?"), professional expertise you could sell independently, hobbies with paying audiences, emerging trends and technologies, and existing products done better, cheaper, or for a niche the incumbents ignore. A strong idea sits at the overlap of three circles — something you are good at, something you enjoy enough to sustain for years, and something people will pay for. Keeping an idea journal and scoring candidates against those three tests turns ideation into a filtering process instead of a bet on the first idea.

## Context & Usage

The natural second step after deciding you want to start a business but before committing capital. Also useful for existing owners hunting for a second product line. The key discipline is generating many candidates and killing most of them cheaply — an idea that survives a weekend of desk research on competitors, search demand, and rough unit economics earns the right to real validation.
