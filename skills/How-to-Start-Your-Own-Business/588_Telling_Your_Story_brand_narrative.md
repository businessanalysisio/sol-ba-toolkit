# Telling Your Story (brand narrative)

## Overview

A brand narrative converts facts about the business into a story people retell: the origin (the moment the founder hit the problem personally), the villain (the frustration, injustice, or bad status quo the business exists to fix), the fight (what you do differently and what it costs you to do it), and the customer's transformation — with the customer, not the founder, as the ultimate hero. Small businesses hold a structural advantage here: a real person with a real reason beats a corporation's mission statement, which is why "the accountant who watched small firms get ignored" outsells "we provide accounting solutions." The narrative must be true and specific — verifiable details (dates, places, first failures) create the authenticity that generic claims cannot, and an invented backstory is a time bomb. Once written, the story compresses into layers: a one-liner, a 100-word "About" paragraph, and the full telling for talks and press.

## Context & Usage

Craft the narrative alongside brand development and reuse it everywhere origin questions arise — the website About page, pitch decks, packaging inserts, PR outreach, and the founder's own networking introductions. Journalists and podcast hosts buy stories, not product specs, making the narrative the small firm's cheapest PR asset. Refresh it as the business grows: the scrappy-start story eventually needs a second chapter about what you've become.
