# Starting a Family Business

## Overview

Family firms combine unusual strengths — deep trust, shared long-term horizons, willingness to sacrifice short-term pay — with failure modes no ordinary company has: roles assigned by birth order rather than competence, business disagreements bleeding into holiday dinners, and succession treated as inheritance instead of a hiring decision. The protective mechanisms are boring on purpose: written job descriptions and market-rate pay for every family member, a shareholders' or partnership agreement covering exit and valuation before anyone needs it, clear separation of family money from company money, and a stated policy on whether in-laws and next-generation members must work elsewhere first. On succession, "fair" and "equal" are different things — dividing ownership equally among children while only one runs the business is a classic source of later conflict.

## Context & Usage

Apply these structures at formation, when the relationships are warm and agreement is easy — not at the first crisis. Institute a regular family council or board meeting that is separate from operational catch-ups, and bring in at least one non-family adviser or non-executive voice as the business grows to keep decisions commercial. Revisit governance at every generational transition.
