# Identifying Initial Costs

## Overview

Startup costing separates three buckets that behave differently. One-off setup costs: company registration, equipment and fit-out, initial stock, licenses and permits, branding and website build, deposits on premises. Ongoing running costs from day one: rent, utilities, insurance, software subscriptions, loan repayments, and the founder's own living draw — the item most often left out and the one that sinks households. Working capital: the cash that sits inside the operating cycle, funding stock and unpaid invoices before customers pay. The total funding requirement is setup costs plus enough months of running costs to reach break-even — commonly six months or more — plus a contingency of 10–20%, because first budgets reliably discover forgotten items (professional fees, delivery vehicles, card-reader fees, waste disposal). Distinguish essential from nice-to-have ruthlessly: leased or second-hand equipment, and fixed costs converted to variable ones, all shrink the amount you must raise.

## Context & Usage

Build this list before seeking funding — it is the evidential core of the business plan's financial section, and lenders probe it line by line. Get real quotes rather than guesses for the big items, and date each cost so the list becomes a spending timeline, not just a total. Revisit after the first three months of trading: actuals versus estimates is the fastest lesson in your own forecasting bias.
