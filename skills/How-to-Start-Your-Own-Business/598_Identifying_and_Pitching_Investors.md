# Identifying and Pitching Investors

## Overview

Investor targeting comes before pitch polish: angels and funds specialize by sector, stage, and cheque size, and a beautifully delivered pitch to the wrong investor is a wasted meeting. Research who has backed comparable companies, seek warm introductions (cold decks convert poorly), and treat the process as a pipeline of many parallel conversations. The pitch deck itself follows a near-universal skeleton of roughly ten slides: problem, solution, market size (bottom-up, defensible), business model, traction to date (the slide investors weight most — paying customers beat projections), competition and differentiation, team and why you specifically, financial projections, and the ask — how much, at what valuation, funding which milestones. Expect the real scrutiny in questions and due diligence: unit economics, customer references, cap table, contracts. Valuation is a negotiation anchored by comparable deals and traction, not a number you deserve; and remember you are choosing a long-term partner — reference-check the investor with founders they have backed, including ones whose ventures failed.

## Context & Usage

Raise when evidence is strongest (growth curve visible, milestone just hit) and before cash pressure forces bad terms — fundraising typically consumes three to six months of founder attention. Rehearse the ten-minute version and the two-minute version; capture every objection heard and patch the deck between meetings. After a yes, negotiate the term sheet with a lawyer: liquidation preferences, board seats, and anti-dilution clauses matter more than headline valuation.
