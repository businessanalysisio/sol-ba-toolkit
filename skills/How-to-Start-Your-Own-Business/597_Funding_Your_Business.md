# Funding Your Business

## Overview

Every funding source sits somewhere on the debt–equity spectrum, and the fundamental trade is repayment obligation versus ownership dilution. Bootstrapping (savings plus reinvested revenue) keeps 100% ownership and forces cost discipline but caps speed. Debt — bank term loans, government-backed startup loans, overdrafts, asset finance — must be serviced from month one regardless of trading, and small-business lending routinely demands personal guarantees, which quietly pierce limited-liability protection. Grants are free money with strings: competitive applications, restricted use, reporting duties. Equity — friends and family, angel investors, venture capital — brings cash and often expertise with no repayment, in exchange for a permanent share of the business and a voice in decisions; VC additionally presumes aggressive growth toward an exit, a fit for very few small businesses. Crowdfunding splits into reward-based (pre-selling product, doubling as demand proof) and equity crowdfunding (many small shareholders). Match the source to the need: short-term working-capital gaps suit overdrafts or invoice finance, long-lived assets suit term loans or leasing, and pre-revenue product development is where equity or grants belong.

## Context & Usage

Quantify the requirement first (see Identifying Initial Costs, 595) — how much, for what, repaid or returned how — because "as much as possible" signals unreadiness to every funder. Layer sources over time: bootstrap the proof, borrow against traction, and sell equity only for growth that debt cannot responsibly fund. Read the total cost of each offer — interest, fees, guarantees, covenants, or percentage of the company at exit — before comparing headlines.
