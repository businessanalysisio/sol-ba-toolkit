# Finding and Recruiting Talent

## Overview

Recruiting starts before the advert: write a job description that separates must-have skills from trainable ones, decide the employment form (permanent, part-time, fixed-term, freelance — each with different cost and legal profiles), and price the role against market benchmarks, remembering that the true cost of an employee runs roughly 1.25–1.4x salary once employer taxes, benefits, and equipment are added. Sourcing channels differ by role: job boards and LinkedIn for volume, personal networks and referrals for early hires (typically the highest-quality source for startups), agencies for hard-to-fill specialisms at 15–25% of first-year salary. Selection should combine a structured interview — same questions to every candidate, scored against defined criteria — with a work-sample task, which predicts performance far better than unstructured conversation; always check the right to work and references before making a written offer.

## Context & Usage

Use this when workload consistently exceeds founder capacity or a skill gap blocks growth — and note that a bad early hire is disproportionately damaging in a five-person company, so slow the process down rather than filling a seat fast. Each hire should trace back to the action plan: what revenue or capacity does this role unlock, and when does it pay for itself?
