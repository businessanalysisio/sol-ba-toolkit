# Choosing a Business Name

## Overview

A workable name passes two independent filters. The marketing filter: short, easy to say and spell after hearing it once (the "radio test"), suggestive of what you do or at least not contradicting it, and roomy enough to survive growth — "Sarah's Wedding Cakes" pinches if the future is corporate catering. The legal-and-availability filter, checked before falling in love: company-register search (the name may already be taken or too similar to an existing registration), trademark database search in your class of goods/services, domain availability (the .com or local TLD, ideally exact-match), and social handle availability — plus a sanity check on meanings and pronunciation in any market you might export to. Name types trade off differently: descriptive names explain themselves but are hard to trademark; invented names (coined words) are distinctive and protectable but need marketing spend to acquire meaning; founder names personalize but complicate a future sale.

## Context & Usage

Settle the name before printing, registering, or building the website, since it propagates into everything and renaming later means re-doing brand assets, signage, and SEO. Shortlist three to five candidates, run all the availability checks on each, and test them on strangers, not friends. Once chosen, secure the domain and key social handles immediately and consider trademark registration early — it is far cheaper than a dispute.
