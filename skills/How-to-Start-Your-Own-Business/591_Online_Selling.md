# Online Selling

## Overview

The structural choice is marketplace versus own store, and most sellers end up blending both. Marketplaces (Amazon, Etsy, eBay) bring ready-made traffic and trust in exchange for commission — typically 6–15% of each sale, plus listing or fulfilment fees — intense price comparison, and no ownership of the customer relationship; the platform can change rules or suspend accounts unilaterally. An own store (Shopify and similar platforms run from a monthly fee plus ~1.5–3% payment processing) keeps margin, brand, and customer data, but every visitor must be earned through marketing. Whichever route, conversion lives in the details: product photography from multiple angles, benefit-led descriptions, visible reviews, transparent shipping costs (surprise fees at checkout are the leading cause of cart abandonment), guest checkout, and mobile-first design. Returns policy and delivery promise are part of the offer — online buyers price the risk of not being able to touch the product.

## Context & Usage

Marketplaces suit launch — demand is proven with minimal setup — while an own store becomes worthwhile once repeat purchases and brand recognition justify acquiring traffic directly. Track the numbers that matter weekly: traffic, conversion rate (around 2–3% is typical for e-commerce), average order value, and fully loaded margin per channel after fees, shipping, and returns; a channel can be busy and unprofitable. Keep listings synchronized across channels to avoid overselling stock.
