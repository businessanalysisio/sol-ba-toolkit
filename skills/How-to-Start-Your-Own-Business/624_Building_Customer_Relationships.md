# Building Customer Relationships

## Overview

A relationship is a sequence of kept promises across the customer lifecycle: first contact, purchase, delivery, aftercare, repeat. Managing it means designing touchpoints at each stage rather than hoping goodwill accumulates — an order confirmation that sets delivery expectations, a check-in after delivery, help content that pre-empts the common problems, and periodic contact that offers value (tips, relevant news) instead of only offers. Two mechanisms carry most of the weight. Expectation management: satisfaction is the gap between what was promised and what was experienced, so under-promising slightly and delivering fully beats bold claims followed by excuses. And service recovery: a complaint handled fast, personally, and generously often leaves the customer more loyal than if nothing had gone wrong — the "recovery paradox" — whereas a fumbled one is retold to friends and review sites. Feed everything back into the customer record: preferences, past issues, and conversation history are what let a growing business keep the personal feel of a small one.

## Context & Usage

This is daily work from the first sale onward, but it deserves deliberate design at two moments: when defining the post-purchase journey before launch, and when the founder stops handling every customer personally — that handover is where relationships quietly die unless the touchpoints and tone are written down as standards. Ask for feedback routinely and act on it visibly; customers forgive flaws far more readily when they can see their input changing things.
