# Sourcing Products and Supplies

## Overview

Four sourcing routes, each shifting the balance of margin, control, and cash: make it yourself (highest control and margin, lowest volume), buy from domestic wholesalers (fast delivery, easy returns, thinner margin), import direct from manufacturers (best unit price, but MOQs — minimum order quantities — that can run to thousands of units, weeks of lead time, freight, duties, and quality risk you carry), and dropshipping (no inventory at all; the supplier ships to your customer, in exchange for slim margins and zero control over stock and delivery experience). Supplier evaluation goes beyond unit price: sample quality, lead-time reliability, payment terms (30–60 days' credit is working capital you don't have to fund), certifications your market requires, and communication responsiveness. The standing rule is redundancy — a qualified backup supplier for every critical input, because a single-source failure stops the whole business.

## Context & Usage

Begin sourcing once demand signals justify stock, ordering conservatively — excess inventory is cash in a form customers may never want. Always order samples, then a small trial batch, before committing to MOQs; inspect imports before final payment (third-party inspection services exist for exactly this). Renegotiate terms annually as volumes grow, and track landed cost per unit — price plus freight, duty, and defects — not the invoice price alone.
