# Setting Up a Workspace

## Overview

The workspace decision runs along a cost-and-commitment spectrum: home office (near-zero cost, possible tax deduction for the business-use portion, but planning/lease restrictions and isolation), coworking space (flexible monthly membership, networking built in), serviced office (furnished, short notice periods, premium per-desk price), and a conventional lease (lowest cost per square foot but multi-year commitment, fit-out costs, and repair liabilities). Weigh headcount growth over the lease term, client-facing needs, storage and equipment requirements, and regulatory constraints — food production, for example, usually cannot run from a domestic kitchen. A practical rule: commit to the shortest term that covers the next 12–18 months of realistic growth, because an oversized lease is one of the most common fixed-cost traps for young businesses.

## Context & Usage

Tackle this after the business model and initial costs are drafted but before hiring, since workspace drives both fixed overhead and the employer obligations that come with it (insurance, ergonomics, safety). Revisit the decision at each growth inflection — first employee, first client visits, first inventory holding — rather than defaulting to lease renewal.
