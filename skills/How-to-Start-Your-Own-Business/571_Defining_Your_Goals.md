# Defining Your Goals

## Overview

Goal-setting for a new venture works on two levels that must agree with each other: personal goals (income needed, hours you will work, whether you want a lifestyle business or a company you eventually sell) and business goals (revenue, customer numbers, market position by a given date). The standard discipline is SMART — specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound — so "grow the shop" becomes "reach 40 repeat wholesale accounts and £8k monthly revenue by month 12." A useful hierarchy runs mission → 3–5 year objectives → annual goals → quarterly milestones, each layer testable against the one above. The lifestyle-versus-growth fork matters most: it determines whether you take investment, hire early, and accept years of reinvested profit, or optimize for owner income and autonomy.

## Context & Usage

Set goals before writing the business plan — the plan is the route; goals are the destination. Review quarterly against actuals and rewrite rather than silently abandon targets that events have invalidated. Share headline goals with co-founders and early hires: misaligned ambitions (one partner wants an exit, the other a steady salary) are a leading cause of founder splits.
