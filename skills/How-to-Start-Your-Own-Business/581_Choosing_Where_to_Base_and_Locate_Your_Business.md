# Choosing Where to Base and Locate Your Business

## Overview

Premises decisions run a spectrum of cost and commitment: home-based (near-zero rent, but check mortgage/lease terms, insurance, and local rules on business use), co-working or serviced offices (monthly flexibility, professional address, meeting rooms by the hour), light-industrial or warehouse units for making and storing, and retail frontage — the only option where the location itself does the marketing. For customer-facing sites the calculus is footfall versus rent: count passing traffic at different times before signing, and study neighbouring tenants (complementary draws help; a dying parade hurts). Lease terms deserve as much scrutiny as rent — length, break clauses, repair obligations, rent-review mechanisms, and permitted use — because a small business locked into five years of unaffordable rent rarely survives it. Factor in staff commutes, parking, deliveries, and planning/zoning permissions.

## Context & Usage

Decide after the business model is clear, since the model dictates the need: an online seller needs storage and courier access, not passing trade. Default to the cheapest arrangement that works and upgrade on evidence — many businesses start at home, move to co-working when clients visit, and lease premises only when stock or staff force it. Model each option's full monthly cost (rent, rates, utilities, insurance, fit-out amortized) into the break-even calculation before committing.
