# Spreading the Word / Creating a Buzz

## Overview

Buzz is earned attention: coverage and word-of-mouth the business does not pay for directly. It runs on a story angle, not a product description — journalists and sharers need a hook such as a founder's unusual journey, a local-first, a contrarian claim, or a genuinely novel product twist. The working toolkit is small. A press release with the news in the first sentence, a quotable line, and a photo, sent to a hand-built media list of named journalists who cover the beat (local press and trade titles are far more reachable for a startup than nationals). Product seeding: putting the product in the hands of reviewers, micro-influencers, and well-connected early customers before launch so opinions exist when publicity starts. And launch events or stunts that give people a reason to talk. Word-of-mouth itself follows a rule: people share what makes them look informed or generous, so give customers something remarkable to relay — a striking fact, a giveaway, an origin story — rather than asking them to repeat an advert.

## Context & Usage

Concentrate buzz-building in the weeks around launch, a new product, an award, or a milestone — earned media needs a news peg, and "we exist" is not one. Time seeding two to four weeks ahead of the date so coverage and posts land together, and capture the attention while it is hot: every mention should point somewhere that collects an email address or a sale, because buzz decays in days.
