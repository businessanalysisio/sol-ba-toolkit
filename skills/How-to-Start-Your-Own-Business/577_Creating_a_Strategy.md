# Creating a Strategy

## Overview

Strategy is the connected chain from purpose to daily action: a mission (why the business exists), a vision (what it looks like in 3–5 years), a handful of strategic objectives, and — crucially — explicit choices about where to play and how to win: which customers, which offerings, which geography, and on what basis you beat alternatives (cost, differentiation, or niche focus). Standard inputs are a SWOT analysis (internal strengths/weaknesses against external opportunities/threats) and an environmental scan (PESTLE: political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental forces). A real strategy is as much about what you will not do; a list of aspirations with no trade-offs is a wish, not a strategy. Each objective should cascade into measurable annual targets so progress is checkable.

## Context & Usage

Draft strategy after market research and goal-setting, as the backbone of the business plan — then treat it as a living document reviewed at least annually or when a major assumption breaks (new competitor, regulation, supply shock). In a small firm the useful format is one page the whole team can recite, not a binder. Guard against strategy drift: every large spending decision should be traceable to a stated strategic choice.
