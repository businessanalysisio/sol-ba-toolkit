# Diversity and Inclusion

## Overview

Diversity is the mix of people — across gender, ethnicity, age, disability, background, and thinking style — while inclusion is whether that mix can actually contribute: a team can be diverse on paper and still exclusionary in practice. For a small business the case is both legal and commercial. Legally, discrimination law protects defined characteristics through hiring, pay, promotion, and dismissal, and claims can arise from a company's first employee onward. Commercially, homogeneous founding teams tend to hire in their own image, narrowing the ideas and customer perspectives available just when a startup needs range. Practical levers are concrete: write job adverts in neutral language and post them beyond the founders' own networks, use structured interviews with identical scored questions for every candidate, consider name-blind CV screening, and set inclusive defaults early — meeting times, accessibility, flexible working — because norms harden fast in a five-person company.

## Context & Usage

Address this at first hire, not at fifty staff: the earliest recruits set the demographic and behavioral template everyone afterward is matched against. Revisit whenever hiring channels, promotion decisions, or pay reviews recur, and audit periodically — who applies, who gets offers, who leaves — since exit patterns reveal inclusion failures that surveys miss.
