# Assessing Whether Marketing Is Driving Sales

## Overview

The question is attribution: which marketing activity caused which sale, as opposed to merely happening before it. The measurement chain runs funnel-shaped — impressions or reach, then clicks or enquiries, then conversions, then revenue — and each stage yields a rate whose movement localizes problems: strong traffic with weak conversion points at the offer or the landing experience, not the advert. The decision metrics sit at the end of the chain: cost per acquisition (total channel spend divided by customers won through it), return on ad spend or marketing ROI, and, for the true test, acquisition cost compared against customer lifetime value rather than first-order value. Tracking mechanics make this possible — tagged URLs, per-channel discount codes, distinct phone numbers, and the humble "how did you hear about us?" at checkout — while the honest caveats are correlation (sales that would have happened anyway get claimed by whatever ad ran last) and lag, since brand-building spend pays back over months, not the week it runs.

## Context & Usage

Review channel performance monthly once real spend begins, and always before renewing or scaling any campaign — the discipline is to cut or fix the channels below the CPA threshold and reallocate to those above it, rather than averaging everything into a single "marketing works" verdict. Where attribution is genuinely murky, run simple experiments: pause a channel for a period, or run it in one region only, and watch what actually changes.
