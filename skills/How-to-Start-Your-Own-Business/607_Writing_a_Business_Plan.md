# Writing a Business Plan

## Overview

A business plan is a structured argument for why the venture will work, built from a standard set of sections: an executive summary (written last, read first — many lenders decide within its one to two pages), a description of the product and the problem it solves, market and competitor analysis, the marketing and sales approach, the operations model, the management team's credentials, and financial projections. The financials are the part most scrutinized: a three-year profit-and-loss forecast, a monthly cash-flow projection for at least year one, and a break-even calculation showing the sales volume at which revenue covers total costs. Every headline claim should trace to evidence — market-size figures to named sources, revenue assumptions to pricing and demand data — because unsupported optimism is the fastest way to lose a reader's confidence.

## Context & Usage

Write the plan when seeking a bank loan, grant, or investment, but also when no outsider will ever read it: the discipline of committing assumptions to paper exposes gaps a founder can otherwise talk around. Treat it as a living document — revisit the forecasts quarterly and rewrite sections when real trading data contradicts them, rather than filing it away after launch.
