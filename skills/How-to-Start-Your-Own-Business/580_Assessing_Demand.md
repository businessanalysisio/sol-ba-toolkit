# Assessing Demand

## Overview

Demand assessment asks not "do people like this?" but "how many will pay, how much, how often?" — and the reliable evidence is behavioural, because stated intent inflates: surveys and polite interviews systematically overpredict purchases. Cheap behavioural probes, in rising order of strength: search-volume and marketplace data (are people already looking?), a landing page with a sign-up or "notify me" button and a small ad budget (measures click-to-interest conversion), a waiting list with a deposit, pre-orders or crowdfunding pledges (money committed before the product exists), and a limited pilot batch or pop-up (real sales, real repeat rate). Alongside volume, probe price elasticity — the same offer at two price points tells you more than any questionnaire — and seasonality, since annualizing a December test overstates the year.

## Context & Usage

Run demand tests after identifying the target customer but before ordering stock, signing a lease, or building the full product; each test should be sized to answer one question for the least money. Set pass/fail thresholds in advance (e.g. "3% of visitors pre-order or we rethink") to keep enthusiasm from grading its own homework. Repeat when entering new segments or launching new lines — demand proven for one offer does not transfer automatically.
