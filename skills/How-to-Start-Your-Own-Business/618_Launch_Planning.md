# Launch Planning

## Overview

A launch is a scheduling problem worked backward: fix the go-live date, then plot every prerequisite — product ready and tested, payment and fulfilment working end-to-end, website live, staff trained, stock in place, legal and insurance in force, launch marketing booked — on a timeline with owners and buffer. Two structural choices shape the plan. Soft versus hard launch: a soft launch opens quietly to a limited audience (friends, one locality, a beta list) to shake out operational faults before publicity multiplies them; a hard launch concentrates spend and PR on a single high-visibility date for maximum initial attention. And the launch-day runbook: who does what hour by hour, what gets monitored (orders, site uptime, social mentions), and pre-agreed fallbacks for the likely failures — site crash, stock shortfall, supplier no-show. The most common error is letting the product's readiness slip while the marketing date stays fixed, so the buzz arrives before the business can serve it.

## Context & Usage

Start launch planning six to twelve weeks out for a typical small business, once the offer, pricing, and channels are settled. Run a full dress rehearsal — a real order placed, paid, fulfilled, and refunded — at least a week before go-live, and hold back part of the marketing budget for the post-launch weeks, when early customer feedback shows which messages actually convert.
