# Selling the Business

## Overview

A business sells for what a buyer can defend paying, and three valuation lenses anchor the negotiation: a multiple of sustainable earnings (small private firms commonly trade at low single-digit multiples of adjusted annual profit/EBITDA, with the multiple rising for recurring revenue, growth, and low owner-dependence), asset value (the floor, relevant for asset-heavy or underperforming businesses), and discounted future cash flows (more common in larger or high-growth deals). Preparation moves the multiple more than negotiation does, and it starts two to three years out: clean, accountant-verified books (599), profits normalized of founder perks, customer concentration reduced (one customer at 40% of revenue is a discount), contracts and IP formalized (602, 603), and — decisively — the business made to run without the seller, because a company that is really the founder's personal practice has little to sell. Buyers come in flavors with different logic: trade buyers (competitors, suppliers) pay for synergies, financial buyers pay for cash flow, and a management buyout pays for continuity, often on deferred terms. Expect due diligence to re-examine every claim, and expect deal structure to matter as much as headline price — cash at completion versus earn-outs contingent on future performance (which keep the seller working and at risk), plus warranties that claw back value if problems surface later. Confidentiality runs through the whole process: staff, customers, and suppliers learning of a sale prematurely can damage the very value being sold.

## Context & Usage

Start acting sale-ready long before wanting to sell — every value driver above is also just good management, and buyers appear unannounced. When the process is real, appoint advisors (broker/corporate finance, lawyer, tax accountant) before the first conversation, because the seller's first offer and first disclosure both set anchors that are hard to move. Time the market as well as the business: sell on a rising trend, since buyers extrapolate the last twelve months. This is one branch of the wider exit decision in 651.
