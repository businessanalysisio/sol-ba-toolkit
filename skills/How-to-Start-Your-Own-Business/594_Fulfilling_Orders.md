# Fulfilling Orders

## Overview

Fulfilment is the pick-pack-ship pipeline between "order received" and "customer satisfied," and its design choices compound with volume. In-house fulfilment (your garage, then a unit) keeps control and per-order cost low at small scale but eats founder hours; a 3PL (third-party logistics provider) stores stock and ships against a per-order and per-pallet fee, buying back time and better courier rates at the cost of margin and hands-on quality control; marketplace programs like Fulfilment by Amazon bundle storage, shipping, and customer service for their channel. Key operating parameters: the dispatch promise (same-day cut-off times set customer expectation), courier selection per weight band and destination, packaging that survives transit without inflating dimensional-weight charges, and tracking numbers sent proactively — "where is my order?" messages are the top support driver. Returns are part of fulfilment, not an exception: a stated window, a simple label process, and rapid refunds convert a failed sale into retained trust.

## Context & Usage

Design the process before launch at whatever scale is coming, and write it down as a checklist so anyone can pack correctly on day one — errors (wrong item, missing item) cost double shipping plus goodwill. The usual migration is founder-packed, then part-time help, then a 3PL when orders outgrow the spare room or the founder's evenings; run the cost comparison per order, including your own time. Monitor dispatch time, delivery time, damage rate, and return rate monthly.
