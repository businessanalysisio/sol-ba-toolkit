# Encouraging Customer Loyalty

## Overview

The economics come first: winning a new customer typically costs around five times more than keeping an existing one, and small improvements in retention compound into outsized profit gains because repeat customers buy more, cost less to serve, and refer others. Loyalty mechanics fall into two families. Transactional schemes — points per purchase, stamp cards, tiered membership with escalating perks, paid subscriptions that convert loyalty into a contractual habit — reward the behavior directly; they work best where purchases are frequent and margins can absorb the reward. Emotional loyalty — built through consistent quality, recognition ("welcome back"), surprise extras, and service recovery when things go wrong — is slower to build but far harder for a competitor to poach with a discount. Track it with a handful of numbers: repeat purchase rate, time between purchases, customer lifetime value, and a satisfaction or Net Promoter–style score, watching for the early-warning signal of lengthening gaps between orders.

## Context & Usage

Start loyalty work as soon as there are customers to keep — the second purchase is the pivotal one, so many businesses aim a specific incentive at first-time buyers within weeks of their initial order. Design any scheme against margin reality (a 10% reward on a 20% margin halves the profit on rewarded sales), and revisit it whenever churn rises or a competitor starts converting your regulars.
