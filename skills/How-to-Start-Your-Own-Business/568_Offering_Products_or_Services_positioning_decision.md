# Offering Products or Services (positioning decision)

## Overview

The foundational choice between selling things and selling time-and-expertise, each with distinct economics. Products carry inventory, manufacturing or sourcing costs, and fulfilment logistics, but scale without adding headcount and can sell while you sleep; services need almost no upfront capital and reach revenue fast, but income is capped by billable hours and quality depends on whoever delivers. The decision hinges on four factors: available startup capital, whether your value lies in a thing or a skill, how the target customer prefers to buy, and how you intend to scale. Hybrids blur the line — productized services (fixed-scope, fixed-price packages), products with service wrappers (installation, training, support contracts), and service firms that later package their method into a sellable product.

## Context & Usage

Make this call early, because it drives nearly every downstream decision: pricing model, premises, staffing, marketing channels, and cash-flow shape (products tie up cash in stock; services often wait on invoices). Revisit it when growth stalls — many service founders escape the hours ceiling by productizing, and product businesses add recurring service revenue to smooth seasonal sales.
