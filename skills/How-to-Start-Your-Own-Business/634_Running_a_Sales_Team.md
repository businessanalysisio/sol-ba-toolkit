# Running a Sales Team

## Overview

A sales team runs on a managed pipeline: defined stages from lead to close (for example enquiry, qualified, proposal, negotiation, won/lost), each with entry criteria and a known conversion rate, so revenue becomes forecastable — if 1 in 4 proposals closes and the average deal is £2,000, hitting a £20,000 month means keeping 40 live proposals moving. Management splits into two metric families: outcome measures (revenue, deals won, average deal size) and the activity measures that lead them (calls made, meetings booked, proposals sent), and correcting activity is how a manager influences next quarter's outcomes rather than lamenting last quarter's. Compensation design does heavy lifting: base-plus-commission balances security against drive, but pay plans are obeyed literally — commission on revenue alone invites discounting, so tie incentives to margin or to the behavior actually wanted. The rest is coaching cadence (pipeline reviews that ask "what's the next step and when," joint calls, win/loss debriefs) and CRM discipline, because a pipeline that lives in salespeople's heads leaves with them.

## Context & Usage

This applies from the moment selling stops being solely the founder's job — even one dedicated salesperson needs defined stages, a target, and a pay plan, and the founder's own informal method must be written down to be teachable. Watch for the classic small-team pathologies: a single rainmaker holding all key accounts (concentration risk), stale deals padding the pipeline, and quotas set from hope rather than historical conversion rates.
