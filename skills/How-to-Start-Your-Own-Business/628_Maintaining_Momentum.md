# Maintaining Momentum

## Overview

Most new businesses hit a post-launch dip: the opening burst of curiosity buyers, friends, and press fades within weeks, and the numbers sag just as the founder's adrenaline does. Sustaining momentum is about building engines that do not depend on launch energy — a pipeline of planned follow-ups (new products or variants, seasonal campaigns, partnerships) scheduled before launch so there is always a "next thing"; a reason for customers to return, since repeat purchase is what replaces novelty as the revenue base; and a steady marketing cadence funded by holding back part of the launch budget for months two through six. Internally, momentum is a morale problem as much as a market one: set near-term milestones that can actually be hit, celebrate them, and keep contact with customers frequent — their feedback both supplies the improvement list and reminds a tired team why the work matters. The trap to avoid is complacency's mirror image, thrash: changing everything at once when growth slows, which destroys the ability to learn what actually works.

## Context & Usage

Plan for the dip before launch — write the 90-day post-launch calendar at the same time as the launch plan — and pay closest attention in months two to six, when early businesses most often stall. If momentum does flatten, diagnose before acting: distinguish a marketing problem (no one new is hearing about you) from a product problem (they hear, try once, and do not return), because the remedies are opposite.
