# Outsourcing Tasks

## Overview

The core sorting rule: keep in-house what differentiates the business (the "core"); consider outsourcing everything that merely keeps it running — bookkeeping and payroll, legal, IT support, design, logistics, customer service overflow. The decision arithmetic compares a specialist's fee against the founder's opportunity cost: an hour spent wrestling payroll is an hour not selling, and a specialist is usually both faster and less error-prone. Options range from freelancers (flexible, per-task) through agencies (capacity and continuity at a markup) to offshore providers (lowest rates, higher coordination overhead and time-zone friction). What makes outsourcing work is the handover apparatus: a written brief or SOP defining the task, deadlines and quality standards (in ongoing arrangements, an SLA), agreed communication cadence, and confidentiality/data-processing terms where customer information is involved.

## Context & Usage

Outsource early for genuinely specialist work (accounting from day one is cheaper than fixing a year of bad books), and progressively as founder hours become the constraint — a recurring task you've done manually more than a few times and documented is a candidate. Keep customer-critical judgment inside until a provider has proven reliability on low-stakes work. Review each arrangement yearly: as volume grows, tasks that made sense to outsource sometimes justify a hire, and vice versa.
