# Analyzing Business Performance

## Overview

Performance analysis means watching a small set of indicators across the whole business, not just the bank balance: financial (revenue, gross and net margin, cash position, debtor days), customer (acquisition, retention, satisfaction, average order value), and operational (delivery times, defect or return rates, capacity utilization, stock turn). Three habits turn the numbers into insight. Compare against something — budget, the same month last year, or industry benchmarks — because a figure without a reference point is just trivia; the variance between plan and actual is where the questions live. Watch trends rather than snapshots: three months of slowly rising returns is a signal, one bad week may be noise. And investigate before reacting — a revenue dip decomposes into fewer customers, smaller baskets, or lower prices, and each has a different fix. A one-page dashboard, updated on a fixed rhythm, beats an occasional heroic deep-dive: the value is in the repetition, which is what makes anomalies visible.

## Context & Usage

Institute a monthly review from the first month of trading, with a deeper quarterly look that questions the plan itself, not just progress against it. Performance analysis is also the prerequisite for other decisions in this library — pricing changes, hiring, dropping a product line, seeking finance — since each needs a trusted baseline to argue from.
