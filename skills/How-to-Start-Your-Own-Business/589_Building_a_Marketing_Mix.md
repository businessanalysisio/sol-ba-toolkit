# Building a Marketing Mix

## Overview

The marketing mix is the checklist ensuring your go-to-market decisions reinforce rather than contradict each other. The classic 4Ps: Product (features, quality, packaging, range — what the customer actually gets), Price (level, discounts, payment terms — signalling as much as revenue: a premium product at a bargain price confuses rather than delights), Place (the channels through which customers buy: own shop, website, marketplaces, wholesale, distributors), and Promotion (advertising, PR, social, email — how the offer becomes known). Service businesses extend to 7Ps by adding People (who delivers and how they're trained), Process (the experience of buying and being served), and Physical evidence (premises, uniforms, documentation — the tangible cues customers use to judge intangible services). The test of a good mix is coherence: each P should tell the same story to the same target customer.

## Context & Usage

Assemble the mix once positioning, personas, and brand are set — it is where strategy becomes concrete spending decisions. Use it as a diagnostic when sales disappoint: mismatches (right product, wrong channel; right price, invisible promotion) surface quickly when each P is examined against the target segment. Revisit annually and whenever a P changes materially, since altering one (a price rise, a new channel) shifts the balance of the others.
