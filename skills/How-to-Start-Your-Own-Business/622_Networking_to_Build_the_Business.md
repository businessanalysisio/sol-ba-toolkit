# Networking to Build the Business

## Overview

Networking converts strangers into a referral system, and it works on a counterintuitive principle: weak ties — acquaintances outside your immediate circle — supply more new opportunities than close contacts, because they move in different information pools. The practical kit is simple. A short, benefit-led introduction (what problem you solve and for whom, in under thirty seconds) that invites a question rather than closing the conversation. A deliberate choice of venues: trade associations and industry events for suppliers and peers, chambers of commerce and local business groups for referrals, online communities for reach beyond geography. And the give-first rule — make introductions, share leads, answer questions before asking for anything, because reciprocity is what turns a contact into an advocate. The step most people skip is follow-up: a personalized message within 48 hours, a note on where you met and what was discussed, and a reason to talk again. A contact without follow-up is a business card in a drawer.

## Context & Usage

Network continuously but with intent: before launch to find suppliers, mentors, and first customers; during trading to keep referrals flowing; ahead of fundraising to warm up introductions to investors, since a warm referral outperforms any cold approach. Set a small recurring quota — one event or three meaningful new conversations a month — and track which relationships actually produce work, weighting future time accordingly.
