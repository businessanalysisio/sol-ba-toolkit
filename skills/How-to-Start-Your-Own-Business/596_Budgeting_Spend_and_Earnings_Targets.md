# Budgeting Spend and Earnings Targets

## Overview

A budget joins two forecasts and one anchor number. The sales forecast builds bottom-up — units × price per month, seasonality included — rather than top-down ("1% of a huge market"); for a new business, ground it in test results and comparable firms, then prepare pessimistic, expected, and optimistic cases. The expense budget lists fixed costs (rent, salaries, insurance — payable regardless of sales) and variable costs (materials, transaction fees, shipping — scaling with each unit). The anchor is break-even: fixed costs ÷ contribution margin per unit (price minus variable cost per unit) gives the sales volume at which the business stops losing money — e.g. £3,000 monthly fixed costs and a £15 contribution per unit means 200 units a month before any profit exists. Earnings targets then sit deliberately above break-even, giving each month a number that funds both the business and the owner.

## Context & Usage

Draft the first budget with the business plan and convert it into a rolling monthly discipline: compare actuals to budget, investigate variances beyond a set threshold (say 10%), and re-forecast the remaining year rather than defending stale numbers. The break-even calculation doubles as a pricing sanity check — if reaching break-even requires implausible volume, the price, the cost base, or the idea needs to change before launch, not after.
