# Managing a Team

## Overview

Team management rests on a simple contract: every member knows what outcome they own, how it will be judged, and where to get help — ambiguity on any of the three produces either duplication or dropped balls. The working tools are unglamorous. Clear goals cascaded from the business plan, so individual targets visibly connect to company ones. Regular one-to-ones — short, scheduled, and owned by the employee's agenda — which surface problems weeks before they become resignations or crises. Feedback given close to the event, specific and behavioral in both directions (praise is a performance tool, not a nicety). And calibrated delegation: hand over outcomes rather than task lists, matching the level of autonomy to the person's competence on that particular job — micromanaging an expert and abandoning a novice are the two symmetrical failures. Style should flex the same way: new or struggling team members need direction, experienced ones need context and room, and applying one style to everyone guarantees it is wrong for most.

## Context & Usage

These practices start with the first employee, not the tenth — a team of two already needs goals, one-to-ones, and honest feedback. Reinforce them at pressure points: onboarding (the first month sets the working relationship), delegation of anything formerly done by the founder, and whenever output or morale dips, where the cause is more often unclear expectations than lack of effort.
