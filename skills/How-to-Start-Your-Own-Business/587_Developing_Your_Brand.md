# Developing Your Brand

## Overview

A brand is the sum of what people expect from you — the logo is just its signature. Building one deliberately means stacking four layers: positioning (who it's for and what single idea you want to own in their mind), personality and values (the three or four adjectives that govern every choice — playful or authoritative, premium or thrifty), verbal identity (name, strapline, tone of voice in everything from ads to error messages), and visual identity (logo, a disciplined colour palette, typography, photography style). The working deliverable is a brand guidelines document — even two pages — specifying logo usage, colours by hex code, fonts, and voice do's/don'ts, so that every future flyer, package, and web page looks and sounds like the same company. Consistency is the mechanism by which small businesses appear bigger and become memorable; a brand applied erratically is money spent teaching customers nothing.

## Context & Usage

Develop the brand after positioning and naming but before spending on marketing, packaging, or the website, since all of them consume it. Founders can bootstrap a credible identity with templates and a freelance designer for the logo; the discipline matters more than the budget. Audit touchpoints twice a year — signage, invoices, voicemail, delivery experience — for drift, and evolve the brand consciously (a refresh) rather than by accretion.
