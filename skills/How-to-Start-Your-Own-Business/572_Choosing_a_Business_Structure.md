# Choosing a Business Structure

## Overview

The legal form of the business determines who is liable for its debts, how profits are taxed, and how much administration is required. The main options: sole trader / sole proprietorship (simplest and cheapest to run, but the owner is personally liable for all business debts — home and savings are exposed); partnership (two or more owners sharing profits and, in a general partnership, unlimited joint liability, which is why a written partnership agreement is essential); limited company / LLC (a separate legal person, so liability is capped at what you invest, in exchange for public filings, statutory accounts, and director duties); and corporation with shareholders for ventures that will raise equity. Tax treatment differs materially — sole traders pay personal income tax on all profit, while companies pay corporation tax and owners choose a salary/dividend mix.

## Context & Usage

Decide before trading begins in earnest, since the structure affects contracts, banking, insurance, and tax registration — but the choice is not permanent: many businesses start as sole traders and incorporate once revenue, risk, or investor interest grows. Take paid advice from an accountant when profits become significant or when taking on partners or investors; the wrong structure can cost real money in tax and expose personal assets.
