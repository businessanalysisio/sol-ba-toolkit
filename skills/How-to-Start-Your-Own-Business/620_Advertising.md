# Advertising

## Overview

Advertising buys attention that earned channels cannot guarantee, and every campaign is defined by four choices: audience (who exactly, defined tightly enough to exclude most people), channel (search ads capture existing demand; social and display create demand; print, radio, and outdoor buy local reach), message (one benefit, one call to action — the AIDA sequence of attention, interest, desire, action is still the standard test of whether an ad does its whole job), and budget with a measurement plan attached. Digital channels price by auction — cost per click (CPC) or per thousand impressions (CPM) — and reward relevance, so a small budget targeted narrowly routinely beats a large one sprayed wide. Whatever the channel, make response traceable: unique URLs, discount codes, or "how did you hear about us?" at checkout, because an ad whose effect cannot be observed cannot be improved. Ads must also comply with advertising standards — claims substantiated, paid promotion identified as such.

## Context & Usage

Advertise when there is a working conversion path to send traffic to and a number the ad must beat: if a customer's contribution is £40, a channel costing £60 per acquired customer fails no matter how good the creative looks. Start with small parallel tests across two or three channels, kill the losers, and scale the winner — and refresh creative regularly, since response decays as the same audience sees the same ad repeatedly.
