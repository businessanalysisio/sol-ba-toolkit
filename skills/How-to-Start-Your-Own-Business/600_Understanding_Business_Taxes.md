# Understanding Business Taxes

## Overview

A small business typically faces four distinct tax families, each with its own base, rate, and deadline. Tax on profits depends on structure: sole traders and partnerships pay personal income tax plus self-employment/social contributions on business profit, while incorporated companies pay corporation tax and owners are then taxed again on salary or dividends drawn out. Sales taxes (VAT/GST) are collected on behalf of the government once turnover crosses a registration threshold — a hard line to watch, because crossing it unregistered creates back-liability; once registered, the business charges output tax, reclaims input tax on purchases, and remits the difference. Payroll taxes arrive with the first employee: withholding income tax and employer social contributions on every pay run. Finally, deductible expenses reduce taxable profit — costs incurred "wholly and exclusively" for the business, plus capital allowances on equipment — which is why disciplined receipts and books (599) directly lower the tax bill. Most regimes demand payments in installments or on account, so tax owed on this year's profit falls due before the year feels finished; the classic founder failure is spending the tax money because it sat in the same account as trading cash.

## Context & Usage

Map the tax calendar in the first month of trading: which taxes apply, at what thresholds, filed when. Sweep a fixed percentage of every sale into a separate tax reserve so deadlines are events, not emergencies. Revisit the structure question (572) once profits grow — the sole-trader/company tax trade-off shifts with income level — and buy an accountant's advice before decisions with tax consequences (hiring, buying assets, taking money out), not after.
