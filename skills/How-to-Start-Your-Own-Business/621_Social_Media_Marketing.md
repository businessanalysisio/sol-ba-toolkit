# Social Media Marketing

## Overview

The first decision is platform selection by audience, not fashion: a B2B consultancy lives on LinkedIn, a visual product on Instagram or TikTok, a local trade on Facebook and community groups — and a small business should master one or two platforms rather than post thinly across five. Content follows a ratio discipline: the bulk should inform, entertain, or show the work behind the product, with direct selling kept to a minority of posts, because feeds punish accounts that only broadcast offers. A content calendar — themes mapped to weeks, posts batched in advance — is what separates sustained presence from the typical burst-then-silence pattern. Measure engagement that predicts revenue (link clicks, saves, DMs, mentions leading to enquiries) over vanity counts like follower totals. Organic reach for business pages is structurally throttled on most platforms, so treat organic as trust-building and paid boosts as the distribution mechanism, and remember that social is two-way: replying to comments and complaints publicly is part of the channel, not an interruption to it.

## Context & Usage

Build the social presence before launch so the audience exists when there is something to sell, then run it on a steady cadence the business can actually sustain — three good posts a week beat a daily grind that collapses in month two. Review platform analytics monthly against enquiries and sales, and reallocate effort toward whichever format demonstrably drives them.
