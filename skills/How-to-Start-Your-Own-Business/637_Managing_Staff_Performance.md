# Managing Staff Performance

## Overview

Performance management is a loop, not an annual event: set expectations, observe, feed back, support, and adjust — continuously. It starts with objectives each person has agreed to, measurable where possible and connected to business goals, because "underperformance" is undiagnosable against expectations that were never explicit. Ongoing feedback does the real work: frequent, specific, and prompt ("the quote went out two days late and the customer chased us" rather than "you need to be more organized"), with an annual or half-yearly review serving only to summarize what regular conversations have already covered — a review containing surprises is evidence the loop is broken. When performance genuinely falls short, follow a fair escalation: name the gap privately and check for causes (unclear brief, missing training, workload, personal circumstances — most shortfalls are situational, not motivational), agree an improvement plan with support, timescale, and review dates, and document each step; documentation protects the employee from arbitrariness and the employer legally if dismissal eventually follows. And manage the top as deliberately as the bottom — strong performers who are ignored because they cause no problems are the ones competitors hire away.

## Context & Usage

Begin with each hire's probation period, which is the lowest-stakes moment to address problems and the point where expectations get calibrated for good. Invoke the formal process whenever informal feedback has not moved the needle after a reasonable interval — and check the system before blaming the person, since a pattern of underperformance across several people points at unclear roles, bad tools, or overload rather than coincidence.
