# The Selling Process

## Overview

Selling is a staged process, not a single event: prospecting (building a list of potential buyers), qualifying (filtering for need, budget, and authority to buy — time spent on unqualified prospects is the biggest hidden sales cost), approaching and presenting (framed around the customer's problem, not your product's features), handling objections (price, timing, trust — each objection is information, and the answer to "too expensive" is usually value, not discount), closing (actually asking for the order, the step novices skip), and follow-up (delivery confirmation, satisfaction check, and the ask for referrals or repeat business). Two disciplines keep the process honest: listening more than talking during discovery, and tracking prospects through a pipeline so you know conversion rates at each stage — if 20 conversations yield 10 proposals and 3 sales, you know exactly which stage to improve and how many conversations next month's target requires.

## Context & Usage

Founders are the first salespeople whether they like it or not; learn the process on early customers, script the recurring parts (qualification questions, common objections and answers), and record every interaction so the pipeline survives beyond memory. The documented process later becomes the training manual for the first sales hire. Review stage-conversion rates monthly — a leak at one stage (many proposals, few closes) points to a specific fixable skill or offer problem.
