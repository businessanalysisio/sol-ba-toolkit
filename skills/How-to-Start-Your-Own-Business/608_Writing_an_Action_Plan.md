# Writing an Action Plan

## Overview

Where the business plan says what and why, the action plan says who does what by when. It decomposes each strategic objective into concrete tasks, each carrying four attributes: a named owner, a deadline, the resources required, and a measurable completion criterion (the SMART test — specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound — is the usual yardstick). Sequencing matters as much as listing: map dependencies so that, say, the website build starts only after branding is fixed, and flag the critical path — the chain of tasks whose slippage delays everything downstream. A 90-day horizon keeps the plan honest; anything further out belongs as a milestone rather than a task. Build in review checkpoints — weekly during launch, monthly thereafter — where each item is marked done, slipped, or blocked and the plan is re-cut accordingly.

## Context & Usage

Draft the action plan immediately after the business plan is agreed, and again at the start of any major initiative — a launch, a new product line, a premises move. It is the founder's main defense against the planning fallacy: written deadlines and owners convert vague intent into checkable commitments, and the standing review surfaces slippage while there is still time to react.
