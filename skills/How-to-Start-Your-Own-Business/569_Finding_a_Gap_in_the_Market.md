# Finding a Gap in the Market

## Overview

A gap is a customer need that existing suppliers leave unmet or badly met — and gaps come in recognizable types: a price gap (the good option is unaffordable), a quality gap (the affordable option disappoints), a convenience gap (buying is slow, distant, or complicated), a segment gap (a group nobody designs for, such as left-handed tools or plus-size sportswear), and a geography gap (a model proven elsewhere but absent locally). Hunting techniques include mining complaint threads and one-star reviews of incumbents, watching what customers bodge together themselves, tracking regulatory or technology shifts that create new needs, and importing ideas across borders. The critical test: a gap only matters if enough people will pay to fill it — some gaps exist precisely because there is no viable market behind them.

## Context & Usage

Use during ideation, before committing to a concept, and again whenever entering a new segment or region. Pair every candidate gap with a sizing exercise and a cheap validation probe (pre-orders, a waiting list, a pilot batch) before spending on inventory or development. Beware the "empty because it's barren" trap — check whether previous entrants tried and failed, and why.
