# Knowing Your Market

## Overview

Market research splits into secondary research (existing sources: industry reports, census and trade-body statistics, competitors' published prices and reviews — cheap, do it first) and primary research (data you gather yourself: interviews, surveys, focus groups, test stalls — slower but specific to your offering). The sizing discipline is TAM/SAM/SOM: total addressable market, the serviceable slice you could realistically reach with your channels, and the obtainable share you can win in the near term — investors and lenders care about the last number, founders too often quote the first. Competitor analysis belongs here as well: map direct rivals, indirect substitutes, and the "do nothing" option, noting their pricing, positioning, and visible weaknesses. Watch trend direction — a small growing market usually beats a large shrinking one.

## Context & Usage

Do the desk research before quitting, spending, or pitching; refresh primary research before launch, before major product changes, and annually thereafter, since markets move. Beware research theatre: friends telling you the idea is great is not data. Structure questions around past behavior ("when did you last pay to solve this?") rather than hypothetical intent ("would you buy this?"), which reliably overstates demand.
