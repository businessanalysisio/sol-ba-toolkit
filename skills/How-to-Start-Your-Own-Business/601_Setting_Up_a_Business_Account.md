# Setting Up a Business Account

## Overview

A dedicated business bank account draws the legal and practical line between the founder's money and the company's. For a limited company it is effectively mandatory — the company is a separate legal person and mixing its funds with personal accounts undermines the liability shield — while for sole traders it is optional but transforms bookkeeping, since every statement line is a business transaction by definition. Opening one requires proof of identity, proof of the business (registration/incorporation documents, tax number), and often a description of expected activity for anti-money-laundering checks; allow days to weeks, so open the account before the first invoice needs paying. Compare offers on the fee structure that matches your transaction pattern: monthly account fees, per-transaction charges, cash-deposit fees (heavy for retail), international payment costs, and any free introductory period. The decision also sets up future capability — merchant services for taking card payments, an overdraft line, and a lending relationship all hang off the account — and challenger/fintech banks trade branch access and cash handling for lower fees and better software integration with accounting tools.

## Context & Usage

Do this as part of company setup, immediately after registering the business and before money moves. Route absolutely everything through it — even small founder-paid expenses should be reimbursed formally — so the account becomes the single source of truth that monthly reconciliation (599) checks against. Revisit the banking choice when the business starts needing what the cheap account lacks: cash deposits, multi-currency, or credit.
