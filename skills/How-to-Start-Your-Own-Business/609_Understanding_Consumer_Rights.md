# Understanding Consumer Rights

## Overview

Consumer protection law gives buyers enforceable claims against a business regardless of what its own terms and conditions say: goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, and as described, while services must be performed with reasonable care and skill. Distance selling (online, phone, mail order) adds a statutory cooling-off period — in the UK and EU, 14 days to cancel for any reason with a full refund — plus mandatory pre-sale disclosure of price, delivery cost, and the seller's identity. Remedies typically escalate in sequence: repair or replacement first, then price reduction or refund, and a business cannot contract out of these rights with "no refunds" signage. Getting this wrong exposes a startup to enforcement action, chargebacks, and reputational damage that outweighs the cost of a generous returns policy.

## Context & Usage

Review consumer rights before writing terms of sale, a returns policy, product descriptions, or advertising copy — and again whenever the business starts selling into a new country, since rules differ by jurisdiction (e.g., US rules are largely state-level and FTC-driven, while the EU applies a harmonized directive). Day to day, this knowledge shapes how customer-service staff handle complaints: a founder who knows the legal floor can resolve disputes quickly and turn a statutory obligation into a loyalty-building moment.
