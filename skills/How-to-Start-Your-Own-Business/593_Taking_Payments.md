# Taking Payments

## Overview

Every payment method is a trade between cost, speed, and customer convenience. Card payments — in person via a terminal or app-based reader, online via a gateway (Stripe, PayPal, Square and peers) — cost roughly 1.5–3% per transaction plus sometimes a fixed fee, and settle to your account in one to several days; that settlement lag belongs in the cash-flow forecast. Bank transfers and direct debits carry low fees and suit invoiced B2B work and subscriptions; digital wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay) lift checkout conversion by removing card-entry friction; cash costs nothing per transaction but adds counting, banking, and theft risk. Two obligations arrive with card acceptance: PCI-DSS handling rules (largely absorbed by using a compliant provider — never store raw card numbers yourself) and chargebacks, where a disputed transaction is clawed back — mitigated by clear descriptors on statements, delivery tracking, and honest product descriptions. For invoiced work, payment terms are part of pricing: deposits up front, due dates stated, and late-payment interest reserved.

## Context & Usage

Choose providers before launch — online sellers need the gateway wired into checkout, and face-to-face traders increasingly find card acceptance mandatory as cash declines. Compare providers on total cost at your expected volume and ticket size (fixed fees hurt small transactions; percentage fees hurt large ones), payout speed, and contract lock-in. Reconcile processor payouts against sales records weekly, and review fees annually — rates are negotiable once volume grows.
