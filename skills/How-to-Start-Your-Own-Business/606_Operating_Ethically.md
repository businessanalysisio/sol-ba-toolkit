# Operating Ethically

## Overview

Ethics in business is the standard applied where the law is silent — compliance is the floor, ethics is how the business behaves when nobody could sue. In practice it decomposes by relationship: with customers, honest marketing, transparent pricing, and no exploitation of small print; with staff, fair pay, safe conditions, and consistent treatment regardless of who is watching; with suppliers, paying on the agreed terms (late payment by bigger customers is the classic small-business ethics failure — and small firms suffer it most); with competitors, winning on value rather than disparagement or stolen information; and with the state and community, paying taxes owed and refusing bribery in any market, including where "facilitation payments" are customary and anti-bribery laws still reach you at home. A short written code of conduct converts the founder's instincts into something a team can apply without asking, and the useful decision test is publicity: would this choice survive being described accurately to your customers or on the front page? Ethical operation compounds commercially through trust — reputation is built in years of consistent behavior and spent in one exposed shortcut — and increasingly it is screened for by corporate customers, lenders, and talent choosing where to work.

## Context & Usage

Define the code early, because the first real test arrives with the first cash-strapped month, and decisions made under pressure become precedent. Apply it hardest in the invisible transactions — supplier payment runs, refund decisions, data handling — and empower staff to flag concerns without penalty. Ethics, sustainability (605), and brand story (588) reinforce each other only when behavior precedes the claim.
