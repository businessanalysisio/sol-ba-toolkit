# Establishing a Company Culture

## Overview

Culture is not the values poster; it is what behavior gets rewarded, tolerated, and punished when nobody is quoting the poster. In a young company it forms by imitation of the founder — how they treat customers when a deal goes wrong, whether they answer email at midnight, what they let slide — so the first lever is deliberate example. The second is codification: write down three to five values as observable behaviors ("we call the customer before they call us"), not abstractions ("excellence"), and use them in real decisions — hiring, promotion, and especially the hard cases, because keeping a high performer who violates the values teaches everyone the values are decorative. The third is ritual: how wins are celebrated, how mistakes are discussed, how meetings run — small repeated practices carry culture better than any handbook. Culture also compounds like debt: with five people, a bad norm can be corrected in a conversation; at twenty-five it is "how things are done here" and requires an intervention.

## Context & Usage

The founding team sets culture whether or not anyone is deliberate about it, so make it explicit before roughly hire five — that is when the founder stops being present in every interaction and the norms must survive on their own. Revisit culture consciously at moments of strain: rapid hiring, a first manager layer, remote working, or after any incident that revealed a gap between the stated values and what actually happened.
