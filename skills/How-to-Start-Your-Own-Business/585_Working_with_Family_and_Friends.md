# Working with Family and Friends

## Overview

Hiring or partnering with people you love runs on a paradox: the trust that makes it attractive is exactly what tempts you to skip the paperwork that would protect it. The rule is to formalize as if you were strangers — written employment contracts or partnership/shareholder agreements, explicit roles and reporting lines, market-rate pay (favours breed resentment on one side or the other), and pre-agreed mechanisms for underperformance and exit, including how shares are valued if someone leaves. Equity deserves particular care: a 50/50 split feels fair and deadlocks every dispute; agree a tie-breaker or unequal split up front. Money borrowed from family belongs in a signed loan agreement with terms, because "pay me back when you can" corrodes relationships precisely when the business struggles. Separate the relationship channels: business disagreements stay in work meetings, not Sunday lunch.

## Context & Usage

Have the awkward conversations before starting, when goodwill is highest — covering commitment levels, what happens if one person wants out, and who decides what. Test the working relationship on a small project before binding it in equity. If employing friends later, be explicit that the manager-report relationship at work differs from the friendship outside it, and apply the same performance standards as for any hire.
