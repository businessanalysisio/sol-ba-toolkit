# Identifying Your Customers

## Overview

"Everyone" is not a customer. Segmentation slices the market along four classic dimensions — demographic (age, income, occupation, life stage), geographic (where they live, work, or shop), psychographic (values, lifestyle, attitudes), and behavioural (usage frequency, price sensitivity, channel preference) — until you can describe a specific buyer who needs what you sell. The working artefact is a persona: a named, one-page profile of a typical customer covering their problem, buying triggers, objections, and where they get information. B2B adds a layer: the company is the account, but decisions involve multiple people — a user, an economic buyer who signs, and often a gatekeeper — each needing different persuasion. Note also that the payer and the user can differ (parents buy, children use), which changes who marketing must convince.

## Context & Usage

Build personas from real research — interviews and observed purchases, not armchair invention — right after market sizing and before choosing channels, setting prices, or writing copy, since every one of those decisions references "who exactly are we serving?" Start with one or two primary segments; serving too many at launch dilutes both product and message. Revisit personas yearly and whenever actual buyer data (from sales records or the customer data system) contradicts the assumed profile.
