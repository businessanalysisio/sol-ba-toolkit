# Streamlining Business Processes

## Overview

Streamlining starts by making the process visible: map the as-is flow step by step — who does what, in what order, with what handoffs and waits — as it actually happens, not as everyone assumes it does; the gap between the two is usually the first discovery. Then hunt the classic wastes: duplicate data entry, approval steps that never change the outcome, batching that makes work wait, rework caused by unclear inputs, and hunting for information that should be at hand. Fix in a deliberate order — eliminate steps that add no value the customer would pay for, simplify what remains, standardize it (one written best way, so quality stops varying by person), and only then automate, because automating a bad process just produces mistakes faster. Focus effort on the bottleneck, the single slowest step that sets the pace of the whole flow; an hour saved anywhere else is largely an illusion. Measure before and after — cycle time, error rate, cost per transaction — so improvement is a fact rather than a feeling.

## Context & Usage

Streamline when symptoms cluster: orders taking longer as volume grows, the same mistakes recurring, staff overtime rising faster than sales, or a key process that only works when one specific person runs it. It is also the essential pre-step before buying software or hiring "because we're swamped" — many capacity problems dissolve when the process stops fighting itself, and both automation (642) and delegation depend on a standardized process to hand over.
