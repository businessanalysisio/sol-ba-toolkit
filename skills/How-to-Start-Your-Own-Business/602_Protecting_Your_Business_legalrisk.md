# Protecting Your Business (legal/risk)

## Overview

Protection is built from four layers, cheapest first. Structure: trading as a limited company caps most claims at the company's assets rather than the founder's house — though personal guarantees on loans and negligent conduct pierce that cap. Contracts: written terms and conditions with customers (payment terms, liability limits, delivery obligations) and suppliers convert handshake ambiguity into enforceable positions; the moment to fix a contract is before signing, not in dispute. Insurance: some covers are legally required — employer's liability the moment you hire in many jurisdictions, motor cover for business vehicles — while public liability (injury/damage to third parties), professional indemnity (bad advice or errors in service work), product liability, business interruption, and key-person cover are bought against your specific exposure map. Compliance: licenses, permits, sector regulation, and data-protection duties (613), where the cost of ignorance arrives as fines and forced closure. Underneath all four sits a simple risk register: list what could hurt the business, score each threat by likelihood and impact, and choose per line whether to avoid, reduce, transfer (insure), or accept it.

## Context & Usage

Run the exposure review at setup, then again whenever the risk profile jumps: first hire, first premises, first product shipped, first big contract. Insurance follows the register, not the other way round — buy covers that match the top-scored risks and skip those that don't apply. Keep a renewals-and-obligations calendar so licenses, policies, and contract expiry dates never lapse silently; a lapsed employer's liability policy is an uninsured payroll.
