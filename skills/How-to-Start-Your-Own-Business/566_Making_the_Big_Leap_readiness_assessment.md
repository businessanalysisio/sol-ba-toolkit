# Making the Big Leap (readiness assessment)

## Overview

A structured self-audit performed before leaving salaried employment to start a business. It examines four dimensions: personal financial runway (most advisers suggest 6–12 months of living expenses saved before going full-time), risk tolerance (how you and your dependents cope with irregular income), a skills-gap inventory (what you can do yourself versus what you must hire or learn — sales, bookkeeping, product), and early market validation (evidence, not enthusiasm, that someone will pay). Many founders de-risk the leap by running the venture as a side business first and only quitting once it replaces a defined share of their salary.

## Context & Usage

Used at the very beginning of the founder journey, before incorporation, spending, or resignation letters. Revisit it whenever circumstances change — a new mortgage, a co-founder joining, or a pilot that outperforms expectations. The honest output is sometimes "not yet": a plan to close the biggest gap (savings, a first paying customer, a missing skill) within a set number of months rather than an immediate launch.
