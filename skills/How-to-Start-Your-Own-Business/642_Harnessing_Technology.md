# Harnessing Technology

## Overview

Technology choices should start from a process pain and work backward to a tool, never the reverse — the diagnostic question is "which repetitive, rule-based task eats hours or produces errors?", because software excels exactly there and disappoints everywhere judgment is required. A small business's core stack usually settles into five slots: accounting software (feeding 599 automatically from bank data), a customer database/CRM (617), the selling channel (website or e-commerce platform, 591/611), communication and document collaboration, and a payments provider (593) — with automation glue between them so data entered once flows everywhere, since re-keying between disconnected systems is the hidden tax of a badly chosen stack. Cloud/SaaS subscription pricing suits startups by converting capital cost into a monthly fee that scales with use, but evaluate total cost of ownership, not the headline tier: per-user fees at future headcount, setup and migration effort, training time, and the exit cost of getting your data back out. Two discipline rules keep the stack healthy: integrate before you add (a tool that talks to nothing creates work), and automate only processes that are already standardized (638) — automating chaos produces faster chaos. Every added system also widens the security surface, so passwords, access control, updates, and backups (613) travel with each adoption.

## Context & Usage

Audit the stack at natural strain points: when admin hours grow faster than sales, when the team starts keeping shadow spreadsheets beside the official system, or before hiring for a role that is mostly copy-paste. Pilot one tool at a time with a real workflow and a decision date. Resist both failure modes — the laggard running a growing business from one inbox, and the magpie paying for fifteen barely-used subscriptions.
