# Providing a Service

## Overview

Service businesses sell time, skill, and outcomes — which makes pricing structure the central design decision. Hourly billing is simple but punishes your own efficiency and caps income at available hours; fixed-price quotes transfer estimation risk to you (underestimate and you work free) but reward speed; value-based pricing charges against the outcome's worth to the client (a fee for a tax saving, not the hours finding it) and yields the best margins where results are measurable; retainers trade a discount for predictable recurring revenue. Whatever the model, the scope must be written: deliverables, exclusions, number of revisions, and a change-order mechanism — scope creep, the gradual unpaid expansion of "just one more thing," is the classic service-business margin killer. Capacity is the other constraint: utilization (billable hours ÷ available hours) rarely exceeds 60–70% once admin, sales, and travel are counted, and quotes must be built on that reality.

## Context & Usage

Formalize terms from the first paying client — engagement letter or contract, deposit or staged payments for longer projects, and payment terms with late-payment consequences. Track actual hours against quotes even when billing fixed-price, because the variance is your estimating education. When demand exceeds capacity, raise prices before hiring; when repeat requests converge on a pattern, productize it into a fixed package (see Offering Products or Services, 568).
