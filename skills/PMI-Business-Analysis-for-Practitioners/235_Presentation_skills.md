# Presentation skills

## Overview

Delivering analysis to an audience so it lands, survives questioning, and produces a decision. The same findings demand entirely different presentations: an executive steering group gets the answer first — recommendation, cost, benefit, risk on one slide — with detail held in reserve for questions; a development team gets the process model walked through step by step with edge cases; a user group gets scenarios in their own vocabulary, not requirement IDs. Craft elements include the pyramid structure (conclusion, then supporting arguments, then evidence), rehearsing the hostile question you are dreading, cutting slides until each earns its minute, and reading the room well enough to abandon the deck when the conversation has moved past it.

## Context & Usage

Needs Assessment culminates in the highest-stakes presentation of the BA life cycle: pitching the business case to decision makers, where PMI expects the recommendation to be championed, not just emailed. Requirements Elicitation & Analysis relies on presentation skill in every model walkthrough and playback session — validation only works if stakeholders genuinely understood what was presented. Solution Evaluation results, especially disappointing ones, need careful staging: how variance from the business case is presented determines whether the response is learning or blame.
