# Requirements / software requirements / systems / value engineer

## Overview

Engineering-culture titles for business analysis practiced at specification-grade rigor, common in aerospace, defense, automotive, medical devices, and other environments where a missed requirement is a recall or a safety finding. BA work here manifests as formal "shall" statements, each atomic, unambiguous, and carrying a declared verification method — inspection, analysis, demonstration, or test — decided when the requirement is written, not when QA asks. The systems engineer flavor adds hierarchical decomposition: stakeholder needs flow down to system requirements, then to subsystem and component specs, with bidirectional traceability maintained at every level because certification audits will walk the chain. The value engineer variant attacks the cost side, analyzing each required function against what it costs to provide and hunting specifications that gold-plate beyond the need. Across all variants, requirement quality attributes (testable, singular, feasible, consistent) are enforced standards, not aspirations.

## Context & Usage

PMI names these engineering roles among those performing business analysis. Their workload concentrates in Requirements Elicitation & Analysis — executed with heavier tooling and stricter quality gates than typical business projects — and in Traceability & Monitoring, where the RTM is a contractual artifact rather than a courtesy. Solution Evaluation appears as formal verification and validation: proving the built system satisfies each specification, then confirming the specified system satisfies the mission. The transferable lesson for any BA is the discipline of deciding how a requirement will be verified at the moment of writing it.
