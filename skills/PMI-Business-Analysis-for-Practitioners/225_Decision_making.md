# Decision making

## Overview

Knowing which decisions the BA owns, which the BA facilitates for others, and how to move either kind to closure. The practical toolkit: agree the decision criteria before evaluating options (so the loudest stakeholder cannot retrofit criteria to their preference), timebox analysis to avoid paralysis on decisions that are cheap to reverse, and record every significant decision with its rationale and alternatives-considered in a decision log — six months later, "why did we exclude the EU warehouse?" must have a findable answer. Equally important is escalation judgment: a BA who decides scope questions that belong to the sponsor creates rework; one who escalates everything creates noise.

## Context & Usage

Decision making anchors Needs Assessment, where the whole domain culminates in a recommendation built from weighted criteria and valued options. In Requirements Elicitation & Analysis it appears as prioritization calls and as facilitating stakeholders to resolve requirement conflicts against agreed criteria. In Traceability & Monitoring, change control is decision making under governance — who may approve which class of change is defined in BA Planning and exercised here. Poorly made or unrecorded decisions are the root of most requirements churn.
