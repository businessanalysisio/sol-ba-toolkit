# Business and industry knowledge

## Overview

Command of the sector the BA works in — its value chain, regulatory landscape, competitors, margins, and vocabulary — plus knowledge of how the specific organization makes money and where it sits in that landscape. A BA in payments who understands PCI-DSS scope and interchange economics asks fundamentally different (and better) elicitation questions than one who does not; a BA in pharma who knows GxP validation requirements will not propose a delivery approach that regulators would reject. This knowledge is what lets a BA challenge stakeholder assertions credibly instead of transcribing them.

## Context & Usage

Most decisive during Needs Assessment, where situation statements, current-state assessments, and business case options all depend on understanding the industry context in which the problem lives. It also shapes Business Analysis Planning (which stakeholders and regulations must be covered) and Solution Evaluation (which industry benchmarks make a result "good"). BAs moving into a new domain deliberately build this knowledge through document analysis, background research, and shadowing before their first elicitation sessions — arriving without it costs credibility that is hard to recover.
