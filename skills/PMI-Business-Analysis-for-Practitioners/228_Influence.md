# Influence

## Overview

Moving people to act when you have no authority to compel them — the BA's permanent operating condition, since stakeholders, developers, and sponsors all outrank or sit outside the BA's reporting line. Effective influence is mostly preparation: framing the same recommendation in each audience's currency (for the CFO, cost avoidance and payback period; for the operations manager, fewer escalations per shift; for engineering, less rework), bringing evidence rather than opinion, and enlisting a respected ally to raise the point when your own voice will not carry. Credibility is the underlying asset — built through accurate work and kept promises long before it is spent asking someone to change their position.

## Context & Usage

Needs Assessment demands influence at its highest stakes: the recommendation only matters if decision makers act on it, which is why PMI ties it to a valued business case rather than analyst opinion. In Requirements Elicitation & Analysis, influence gets reluctant stakeholders to attend sessions, gets prioritization taken seriously, and gets "we've always done it this way" reexamined. In Traceability & Monitoring, it keeps change discipline alive when powerful stakeholders want to bypass the process. Influence exercised without political awareness (234) routinely backfires.
