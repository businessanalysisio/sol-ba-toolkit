# Creative and critical thinking

## Overview

Two complementary modes the BA must switch between deliberately: divergent thinking to generate genuinely different solution options, and convergent thinking to stress-test them against evidence and constraints. The creative half means refusing to anchor on the first workable idea — when asked to "add an approval step," a creative BA also tables removing the need for approval entirely (auto-validation rules, tolerance thresholds). The critical half means interrogating every option and every stakeholder assertion: What assumption does this rest on? What would falsify it? Who benefits if it is true? Common failure modes are brainstorming that never gets pruned, and skepticism applied so early that no option survives long enough to be examined.

## Context & Usage

In Needs Assessment this pairing drives option identification — PMI expects multiple viable alternatives (including "do nothing") before a recommendation, which requires generating options creatively and then eliminating them critically against feasibility and value criteria. In Requirements Elicitation & Analysis, critical thinking catches requirements that restate the current system, while creative thinking finds simpler ways to satisfy the underlying need. In Solution Evaluation, it guards against confirmation bias when interpreting whether the solution actually delivered.
