# Communication skills (business writing + verbal)

## Overview

The paired ability to write requirements that cannot be misread and to speak at the right altitude for each audience. On the writing side this means testable, unambiguous statements — active voice, one requirement per statement, no "etc.", "user-friendly", or "as appropriate" — plus knowing when a diagram communicates better than prose. On the verbal side it means running an interview without leading the witness, summarizing back what was heard, and translating between business language and technical language in both directions. The same finding gets a one-paragraph executive framing for the sponsor and a detailed specification for the delivery team.

## Context & Usage

Communication is the delivery mechanism for every PMI domain: elicitation questions and playback in Requirements Elicitation & Analysis, the communication approach defined during Business Analysis Planning, business case narratives in Needs Assessment, change-impact notices in Traceability & Monitoring, and evaluation readouts in Solution Evaluation. PMI's practice guide treats requirements documentation quality as a direct function of writing skill — most requirement defects trace back to ambiguity introduced at the point of writing, not to missing information.
