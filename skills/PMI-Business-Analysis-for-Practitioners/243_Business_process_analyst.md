# Business process analyst

## Overview

A specialization of the BA role where the process itself — not a software system — is the object of analysis. Day-to-day, this means mapping as-is workflows in swimlane detail down to the handoffs where work actually queues, measuring what the map cannot show (cycle time versus touch time, first-pass yield, rework loops), and locating the waste: the approval step that adds two days and catches nothing, the same customer data rekeyed by three teams, the exception path that has quietly become the main path. BA work then manifests as the to-be design and its justification — process requirements, redesigned flows, and the case for which steps to eliminate, automate, or leave alone, since automating a broken process just produces faster wrong answers.

## Context & Usage

Among PMI's roles that perform business analysis, this one straddles Needs Assessment and Requirements Elicitation & Analysis: process measurement quantifies the situation statement's impact, and the as-is/to-be pair is the guide's own recommended framing for scoping change. Elicitation leans on observation and contextual inquiry, because the documented process and the performed process diverge everywhere. Solution Evaluation is unusually concrete here — the same cycle-time and error-rate baselines captured during analysis become the yardstick for whether the redesign paid off.
