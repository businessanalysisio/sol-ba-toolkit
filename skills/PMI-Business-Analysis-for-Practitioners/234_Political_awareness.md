# Political awareness

## Overview

Reading the informal power structure and working with it rather than being ambushed by it: spotting who really owns a decision versus who holds the title on the org chart, whose quiet opposition can stall an initiative without a single objection on record, and which department heads have history that will resurface in your prioritization workshop. Politically aware BAs notice signals — who gets copied on escalations, whose comments the sponsor waits for, which "technical concerns" are actually turf defense — and use them constructively: pre-briefing a stakeholder so your findings never blindside them in front of their peers, and sequencing one-on-ones so allies are aligned before the contentious meeting. This is not manipulation; it is refusing to let good analysis die of avoidable ambush.

## Context & Usage

Stakeholder identification and analysis in Business Analysis Planning is the formal home — PMI's stakeholder characteristics explicitly include attitude and influence, which are political facts. Needs Assessment is politically charged by nature: naming a problem implies someone let it happen, so the situation statement must be framed to be accepted, not just accurate. In Traceability & Monitoring, political awareness predicts which change requests are really relitigated turf battles. Analysis that ignores politics is analysis that gets shelved.
