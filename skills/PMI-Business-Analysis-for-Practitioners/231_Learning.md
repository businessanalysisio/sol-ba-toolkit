# Learning

## Overview

The capacity to become functionally conversant in an unfamiliar domain fast — because BAs are routinely dropped into insurance claims, freight forwarding, or clinical trials with weeks, not years, to get useful. A deliberate ramp looks like: read the existing process documentation and last year's incident reports before the first interview, build a personal glossary from day one (domain vocabulary is the entry ticket to stakeholder credibility), shadow the actual work rather than relying on described work, and test understanding by explaining the domain back to an SME until they stop correcting you. Learning also runs project-to-project: harvesting lessons learned and updating your own technique repertoire instead of repeating last project's estimation misses.

## Context & Usage

Domain ramp-up front-loads Needs Assessment — you cannot assess a problem in a business you do not understand — and continues through Requirements Elicitation & Analysis, where every elicitation session doubles as a learning session and the BA's questions get sharper as the mental model fills in. PMI's Solution Evaluation domain institutionalizes the other direction of learning: comparing predicted value to measured results so the organization, and the BA personally, calibrate better next time. BA Planning should honestly budget ramp-up time for unfamiliar domains rather than pretending it is zero.
