# Conflict management

## Overview

Recognizing, surfacing, and resolving disagreement productively rather than letting it fester into stalled requirements or passive resistance. Central skills: distinguishing positions ("we must keep the old approval screen") from underlying interests ("my team cannot absorb retraining during peak season"), choosing deliberately among the Thomas-Kilmann modes — collaborating, compromising, accommodating, competing, avoiding — instead of defaulting to one, and de-personalizing disputes by anchoring them to shared criteria such as the business objective or agreed prioritization rules. A BA who avoids conflict ships requirements that one faction quietly plans to ignore.

## Context & Usage

Conflict concentrates in Requirements Elicitation & Analysis, where two departments routinely want incompatible behaviour from the same feature, and in Traceability & Monitoring, where change requests reopen settled decisions. It also appears in Needs Assessment when stakeholders disagree about what the real problem is. The PMI guide positions the BA as an honest broker: the goal is not to win but to get the conflict visible, structured against objective criteria, and decided by the right authority — then documented so it stays decided.
