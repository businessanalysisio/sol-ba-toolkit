# Analytical skills

## Overview

The core cognitive toolkit of business analysis: decomposing vague stakeholder statements into discrete, testable requirements, separating symptoms from causes, and spotting gaps, overlaps, and contradictions across a requirements set. When a stakeholder says "order processing is too slow," an analytically skilled BA breaks that into measurable parts — which order types, at which process steps, against what cycle-time baseline — before proposing anything. It also covers pattern recognition across data and models, structured comparison of options, and the discipline of asking "what evidence supports that?" rather than accepting assertions.

## Context & Usage

Analytical skills run through all five PMI domains but do the heaviest lifting in three. In Needs Assessment they drive root cause work and the framing of the situation statement (problem, effect, impact). In Requirements Elicitation & Analysis they turn raw elicitation notes into models — decompositions, data structures, rules — and expose the inconsistencies those models reveal. In Solution Evaluation they compare measured results against the predicted benefits and diagnose variances. A BA weak in analysis produces requirements that read well but cannot be built or tested.
