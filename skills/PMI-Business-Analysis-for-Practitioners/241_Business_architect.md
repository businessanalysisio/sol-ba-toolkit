# Business architect

## Overview

A role positioned above individual projects: the business architect maintains the enterprise's structural blueprints — capability maps, value streams, business-unit-to-process-to-system mappings, and the target operating model — and uses them to judge where change investment belongs. BA work in this role manifests at portfolio altitude: instead of eliciting requirements for one system, the architect assesses which capabilities (say, "customer onboarding" or "returns handling") are weakest relative to strategy, spots that three departments are independently funding overlapping fixes to the same broken capability, and hands project teams a scope anchored in the enterprise picture rather than a department's wish. The deliverables are reference models others build against, so their currency and internal consistency are the job.

## Context & Usage

PMI lists the business architect among the roles performing business analysis, concentrated in Needs Assessment executed at enterprise scale — capability gap analysis is essentially needs assessment with the whole organization as the unit of analysis. The role also feeds Business Analysis Planning on individual initiatives (project scope must trace to architectural direction) and supplies Solution Evaluation with the capability baseline that "did this investment improve anything?" gets measured against. Project BAs consume the architect's models; the architect consumes the project BAs' ground truth about how work really flows.
