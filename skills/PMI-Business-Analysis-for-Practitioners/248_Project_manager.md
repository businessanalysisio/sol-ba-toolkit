# Project manager

## Overview

The owner of schedule, budget, resources, and overall delivery risk — and, on smaller projects, frequently the person doing the business analysis too, which is exactly why PMI wrote a BA practice guide for a PM audience. When a PM wears the BA hat, BA work manifests as eliciting and documenting requirements between status meetings, and the danger is predictable: schedule pressure rewards declaring requirements "done" early, and the PM is negotiating scope with stakeholders while also being the person who defined it. When PM and BA are separate people, the guide describes a deliberate partnership with a fault line to manage — the PM controls the project scope baseline while the BA develops the product requirements inside it, so a requirements change is simultaneously the BA's impact analysis and the PM's schedule and cost problem. High-functioning pairs plan the BA effort together and jointly run change control; dysfunctional ones discover in testing that "in scope" meant different things to each.

## Context & Usage

Business Analysis Planning is the collaboration's center of gravity: PMI is explicit that the BA plan must nest inside the project management plan, with elicitation windows, review cycles, and approval gates on the shared schedule. In Traceability & Monitoring the PM consumes the RTM as a scope-control instrument and co-adjudicates change requests. During Needs Assessment the PM is often not yet assigned — which is why project charters inherit business cases they had no hand in, and why the PM must reconnect with the original need rather than just the milestone list.
