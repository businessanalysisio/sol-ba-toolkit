# Leadership

## Overview

Taking ownership of the requirements effort as a whole rather than executing tasks handed down. In practice: setting and defending a direction for the analysis work ("we model the claims process end-to-end before we touch screen designs, and here is why"), protecting elicitation time on stakeholder calendars against schedule raids, absorbing ambiguity instead of passing it downstream to developers, and saying clearly when the requirements are not ready even though the plan says they should be. Leadership for a BA also means developing others — reviewing a junior analyst's models, sharing techniques, and modeling intellectual honesty by publicly correcting your own earlier analysis when new evidence lands.

## Context & Usage

PMI frames the BA as the person accountable for requirement quality across all five domains, which is a leadership mandate regardless of title. It is most visible in Business Analysis Planning, where the BA must convince the project manager and sponsor that the planned BA effort is right-sized, and in Requirements Elicitation & Analysis, where sessions need someone willing to own outcomes. During Solution Evaluation, leadership shows as the courage to report that measured benefits fall short of the business case — the finding nobody upstairs wants.
