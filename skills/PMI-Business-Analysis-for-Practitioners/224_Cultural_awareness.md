# Cultural awareness

## Overview

Sensitivity to how national, regional, and organizational cultures change what "good elicitation" looks like. Concretely: in a strongly hierarchical culture, junior staff will not contradict their manager in an open workshop, so the BA collects input anonymously or in separate sessions; in a consensus culture, a decision "agreed" in the meeting may only be real after offline alignment; in some cultures "yes" signals "I heard you," not "I agree." Organizational culture matters just as much — a risk-averse bank and a growth-stage startup need different levels of documentation formality, and mistaking one for the other erodes credibility fast. The skill includes auditing your own defaults rather than treating them as neutral.

## Context & Usage

Cultural awareness shapes Business Analysis Planning most directly: the choice of elicitation techniques, meeting formats, review cadence, and sign-off protocol should be tailored to the culture of the stakeholder groups, and PMI explicitly calls for adapting the BA approach to organizational context. During Requirements Elicitation & Analysis it determines whether people actually tell you the truth about broken processes — admitting a workaround can feel unsafe in blame-oriented cultures. It also matters in Solution Evaluation, where willingness to report that a solution underperforms varies enormously by culture.
