# Negotiation

## Overview

Reaching explicit, durable agreements when stakeholders want more than the project can deliver — which is every project. The BA's staple negotiation is the scope trade: giving up a nice-to-have custom report to keep the data-validation rules that actually protect the business objective, and making that exchange visible so both sides see what was bought and what was paid. Core craft: probe for interests beneath stated positions (a demand for "real-time sync" often turns out to be an interest in "no stale prices at checkout," satisfiable far more cheaply), know your walk-away alternative before entering the room, and trade on differences in what parties value rather than splitting everything down the middle. An agreement nobody wrote down is not an agreement.

## Context & Usage

Negotiation saturates Requirements Elicitation & Analysis — prioritization is multi-party negotiation with the business objective as the referee, and PMI's guidance on resolving requirements conflicts assumes a BA who can broker rather than merely record. In Traceability & Monitoring, every change request is a negotiation over what leaves scope to make room. In Business Analysis Planning, the BA negotiates elicitation access and review commitments with a project manager guarding the schedule. Needs Assessment closes with negotiating whose success measures make it into the business case.
