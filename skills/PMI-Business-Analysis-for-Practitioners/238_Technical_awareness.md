# Technical awareness

## Overview

Enough architecture, data, and integration literacy to be a competent counterpart to engineers — without crossing into designing the solution. The test is the quality of your questions: when a stakeholder says "real-time," a technically aware BA pins down whether that means sub-second push or an hourly batch nobody would notice; when a requirement casually implies joining customer records across two systems, they flag the identity-matching and data-ownership problem hiding inside one sentence; when developers say an approach is "expensive," they can probe whether the cost is in the integration, the data migration, or the nonfunctional targets. This literacy also protects the business side — it lets the BA challenge technical pushback that is really preference, and translate constraint explanations into terms stakeholders can trade against.

## Context & Usage

Technical awareness sharpens Requirements Elicitation & Analysis most: interface analysis, data definitions, and nonfunctional requirements (performance, availability, security) are unwritable without it, and PMI's guide leans heavily on models — ERDs, state tables, system context — that presume it. In Needs Assessment it keeps option feasibility honest before the business case hardens around something unbuildable. In Solution Evaluation it helps distinguish "the solution failed" from "the solution was configured or adopted badly."
