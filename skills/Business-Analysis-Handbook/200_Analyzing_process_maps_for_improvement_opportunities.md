# Analyzing process maps for improvement opportunities

## Overview

Once the as-is map is validated, interrogate it with a known checklist of waste signatures: excessive handoffs (every lane crossing is a delay and an error opportunity); rework loops (arrows flowing backwards usually mean quality failures upstream); approval steps that never reject anything (pure delay, no control value); duplicated data entry into multiple systems; batching and waiting steps where work queues; exception paths so numerous they dwarf the happy path (a sign the process no longer fits the business); and steps whose output nobody downstream uses. Annotate the map with volumes, timings, error rates, and cost per step so opportunities can be ranked by impact rather than by loudest complaint. Standard redesign moves then apply: eliminate, combine, resequence, reassign to a more appropriate role, automate, or run steps in parallel — and the to-be map plus the gap between the two becomes the requirements source.

## Context & Usage

The payoff step of process work, used in improvement initiatives, system replacements (fix the process before automating it, or you automate the waste), and cost-reduction reviews. Involve the people who run the process when analysing it — they know which steps are theatre — but bring the data, because folklore about where time goes is often wrong. Quantified findings ("approval step 4 adds three days and rejects 0.2% of cases") convert directly into business-case benefits.
