# Agile-specific requirements approaches

## Overview

Agile does not abolish requirements work; it re-times it. Scope lives in a prioritised backlog of epics decomposed into features and user stories, and detail is added just in time — stories near the top of the backlog are refined to sprint-ready precision while distant ones stay deliberately coarse, avoiding the waste of specifying things that will change or be cut. Story quality is policed by INVEST (independent, negotiable, valuable, estimable, small, testable) and by acceptance criteria, often in Given/When/Then form, agreed in "three amigos" conversations between BA, developer, and tester before a story enters a sprint. A definition of ready gates what refinement must produce; a definition of done carries the global NFRs. The BA's craft shifts from authoring a complete document to continuous elicitation, backlog refinement, slicing stories thin enough to flow, and answering questions in-sprint — the conversation, not the card, carries the requirement.

## Context & Usage

Used wherever delivery is iterative — Scrum, Kanban, and the hybrid reality of most organisations, where an up-front scoping phase feeds an agile build. Hybrids need explicit translation: a signed-off BRD can seed the initial backlog, but someone (usually the BA) must own converting sections into stories and keeping the two views honest. The classic anti-patterns to guard against: stories written as chapter headings ("Reporting"), backlogs treated as append-only wish-lists that no one prunes, and acceptance criteria written after development as documentation of whatever was built.
