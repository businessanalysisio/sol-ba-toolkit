# Identifying needs that influence a solution

## Overview

Behind every requirement sits a need — a driver rooted in strategy, pain, or obligation — and solutions should be shaped by needs, not by accumulated wish-lists. Elicit them by asking sponsors what success looks like in twelve months, mining strategic objectives and KPIs for what the initiative must move, and cataloguing current pain points with their business cost. Separate needs (the customer-onboarding cycle must shrink from ten days to two) from wants (a dashboard would be nice), because needs justify scope and wants inflate it. Capture the shaping facts that quietly dictate solution architecture: transaction volumes, operating hours, locations and languages, user counts and skill levels, growth projections — these needs rarely appear in anyone's feature list but determine whether a solution fits.

## Context & Usage

Performed during scoping, hand in hand with constraint identification: needs define what the solution must achieve, constraints define what it must live within, and options are generated inside that frame. Trace every later requirement back to a need — a requirement that serves no identified need is a scope-creep candidate, and a need with no requirements against it is a delivery gap. Revisit needs when the business context shifts; a solution shaped by last year's needs can be obsolete at go-live.
