# Adapting communication style to different stakeholder types

## Overview

The same content must be packaged differently for different consumers, and misjudging the packaging loses the audience regardless of the quality of the analysis. Executives want the headline, the options, and the decision ask on one page — lead with the conclusion, keep the workings in an appendix. Subject-matter experts want to be walked through the detail and to see their corrections reflected. Technical teams want precision — models, data definitions, explicit boundaries — and distrust marketing language. Beyond role, read individual preferences: detail-hungry vs detail-averse, visual (show a diagram or mock-up) vs textual, formal vs conversational, and whether the person processes in the meeting or needs material in advance. Match frequency too: some stakeholders need weekly contact to stay confident; others resent anything beyond milestone updates.

## Context & Usage

In play every time the BA writes an email, books a meeting, or chooses between a slide, a document, and a diagram. It matters most at high-stakes moments — steering committee presentations, sign-off requests, delivering bad news — where a style mismatch reads as incompetence or disrespect. Diagnose style early (ask directly: "how do you prefer to receive updates?") and note it in the stakeholder plan rather than rediscovering it by failure.
