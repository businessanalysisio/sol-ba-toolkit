# Vendor procurement and response evaluation

## Overview

The procurement funnel runs RFI (a broad market scan to learn what exists and shortlist) then RFP/ITT (detailed requirements to shortlisted vendors, answered against your catalogue). The single most important discipline: agree the weighted scoring matrix — criteria, weights, and scoring scale — *before* any responses arrive, so evaluation cannot be quietly bent toward a favourite. Treat vendor self-assessments ("fully compliant") with suspicion and verify the claims that matter: script product demonstrations around your own scenarios and data rather than letting vendors run their polished demo path; take references from customers of similar size and sector, asking specifically about implementation overruns and support quality; and distinguish what the salesperson said from what the contract commits to — anything load-bearing goes in the contract or it does not exist. Involve procurement and legal early; commercial terms, exit clauses, and data ownership are requirements too.

## Context & Usage

Follows requirements definition in any buy-not-build initiative (see 208 for how the requirements themselves adapt). The BA's role is evaluation integrity: keeping scoring evidence-based, documenting the rationale for the recommendation so it survives challenge, and flagging when the best-scoring product carries the worst commercial or viability risk — vendor financial health and product roadmap belong in the matrix alongside features. A transparent paper trail matters doubly in public-sector and regulated procurement, where losing bidders may formally challenge the award.
