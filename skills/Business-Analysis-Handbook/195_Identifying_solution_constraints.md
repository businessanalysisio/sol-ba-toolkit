# Identifying solution constraints

## Overview

Constraints are the fixed walls the solution must fit inside, and finding them early keeps analysis from designing options that can never be built. Hunt them by category: budget ceilings and funding cycles; hard deadlines (regulatory dates, contract expiries, seasonal windows); mandated technology and architecture standards ("must run on our existing cloud platform"); legal and compliance obligations; existing supplier contracts and licence terms; available skills and resources; and organisational policies (data residency, procurement rules). Record each constraint with its source, owner, and rationale — separately from requirements, because constraints are imposed, not negotiated in the same way. Crucially, challenge suspected constraints: many are actually assumptions or preferences wearing a constraint's clothing ("we've always used vendor X"), and a false constraint eliminates good options for no reason.

## Context & Usage

Belongs in scoping, before options are generated, and feeds directly into feasibility assessment and the business case — every genuine constraint prunes the option tree. Revisit the constraint list when circumstances shift (new budget round, changed regulation, expired contract): a constraint that has lapsed can reopen a previously rejected and better option. A late-discovered constraint, by contrast, typically invalidates completed design work.
