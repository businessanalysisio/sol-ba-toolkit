# Structuring a business requirements document

## Overview

A BRD earns its length by being navigable: every reader should find their section without reading the whole thing. The proven skeleton — document control (version history, distribution list, approvals page with named sign-off roles); purpose and background; business objectives and success criteria; scope, with an explicit out-of-scope list that prevents more arguments than the in-scope list; stakeholder summary; current-state and future-state process overviews (maps, not prose); functional requirements organised by the agreed hierarchy; non-functional requirements; assumptions, dependencies, constraints, and risks each in their own labelled subsection; glossary; and appendices for supporting detail. Front-load a one-to-two-page executive summary for approvers who will read nothing else. Consistent section and requirement numbering makes review comments and traceability references precise ("comment on FR-4.2", not "the bit about invoices").

## Context & Usage

Assembled once elicitation stabilises and maintained as the baseline artifact through sign-off and change control. Templates should be tailored, not worshipped: cut sections that would be empty and say why, rather than padding them — reviewers punish padding with disengagement. The out-of-scope section and the approvals page are the two parts that settle disputes months later; treat them with corresponding care.
