# Building effective stakeholder working relationships

## Overview

Before formal elicitation begins, invest in short one-to-one introductions with each key stakeholder: learn their goals, pressures, history with previous projects, and preferred way of working (email vs call, detail vs summary, mornings vs afternoons). Frame the project in their terms — what's in it for them, what it threatens — and record what you learn in the stakeholder management plan so engagement is deliberate, not accidental. Thereafter the relationship is maintained through regular informal check-ins between formal sessions, acting as an honest broker between business and IT (translating rather than taking sides), crediting stakeholders visibly for their contributions, and warning them privately before anything unwelcome lands publicly.

## Context & Usage

Front-loaded at project initiation, when goodwill is cheapest to build, and drawn upon for the whole engagement — a stakeholder with a working relationship will tell you about the spreadsheet workaround, the real reason the last project failed, and the requirement everyone is afraid to raise. Re-invest whenever the cast changes (reorganisations, new sponsor) and after any conflict; a damaged relationship left unrepaired converts a difficult stakeholder into an opponent.
