# Process modelling and levels of detail

## Overview

Process maps live in a hierarchy, and most mapping confusion comes from mixing its levels. At the top, a level-0/organisation view shows the end-to-end value chain and where the process in question sits. Level 1 maps a single process end to end — major steps, key decision points, one page, readable by an executive. Level 2 drops into procedure detail with swimlanes assigning each step to a role or system, showing handoffs and exceptions — the workhorse view for analysis and requirements. Level 3 reaches task/work-instruction depth (keystrokes, screens, rules) for training and system specification. Decompose consistently: each step at one level should expand into its own map at the next, and a single diagram should never blend a strategic step with a keystroke. Choose the level by audience and purpose before drawing, not after.

## Context & Usage

Used whenever current-state understanding or future-state design is on the table — process maps are the BA's primary shared artifact with operational stakeholders, who will correct a diagram far more readily than a document. The level discipline matters practically: executives shown level-3 detail disengage; developers given level-1 maps invent the detail themselves. Start at level 1 to agree the frame, then decompose only the areas the project will actually change.
