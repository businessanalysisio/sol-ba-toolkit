# Preparing an organization for business agility

## Overview

Business agility is an organisational property — the ability to sense change and redirect quickly — and it cannot be installed by teaching delivery teams Scrum while everything around them stays annual and hierarchical. Preparation means working on the surrounding system: shifting funding from one-off project budgets to stable product teams with outcome targets; building fast feedback loops (short release cycles, real usage data, direct customer contact) so decisions are informed by evidence rather than by last year's plan; devolving decision authority to the teams closest to the information; slimming governance from stage-gate document approvals to frequent, lightweight steering on outcomes; and — hardest — changing leadership behaviour from directing solutions to setting intent and removing obstacles. A readiness assessment (culture, structure, funding, governance, technical practices) tells you where the organisation actually is, and adoption itself should be agile: pilot in one value stream, learn, expand — a big-bang "agile transformation programme" is self-refuting.

## Context & Usage

Taken up when the gap between market speed and organisational speed becomes a board-level concern, or when team-level agile adoption has visibly plateaued against unchanged funding and governance walls. The BA's role shifts accordingly: from specifying scope up front to continuous discovery, framing hypotheses and outcome measures, and helping leadership see systemic blockers — the mismatched incentive, the quarterly funding gate — that no delivery team can fix from below. Expect the resistance to concentrate in middle management, whose coordinating roles agility redistributes; preparing them is most of the work.
