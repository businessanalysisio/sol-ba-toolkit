# Enterprise-level business analysis

## Overview

Above the single project sits a different kind of BA work: analysing the organisation itself so that the right initiatives get started at all. Its activities include assessing business capabilities against strategy (which capabilities are weak, missing, or over-invested), mapping value streams end to end across departmental silos, shaping a target operating model, and translating strategic objectives into a coherent, sequenced portfolio of change — including recommending which proposed projects should *not* run because they duplicate others or serve no strategic need. The enterprise BA works alongside enterprise architects (who own the technology landscape view) and portfolio managers, supplying the business logic: capability maps, cross-project dependency views, and benefit overlap analysis. The unit of analysis is the organisation and its strategy horizon, not a requirement catalogue and a delivery date.

## Context & Usage

Engaged before projects exist — during strategy execution planning, annual portfolio shaping, post-merger integration, and whenever leadership suspects the change portfolio is a pile of local optimisations rather than a strategy. It is also where project-level pathologies get fixed at their root: five projects independently building customer-data capabilities is invisible to each project BA and obvious on a capability map. For the practitioner, this is the senior career track — the influence shifts from "requirements are right" to "the organisation is investing in the right change."
