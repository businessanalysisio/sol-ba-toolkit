# Handling stakeholder review feedback

## Overview

Review comments arrive as a mixed pile — typos, genuine corrections, opinions, and stealth scope changes — and the discipline is to process them as data, not as verdicts on the author. Consolidate everything into a single comment log with source, section reference, and classification: editorial (fix silently), substantive (analyse, respond, amend), and scope-changing (route to change control, not into a quiet edit — a review comment is not an approved change request). Where two reviewers contradict each other, do not privately pick a winner: get them together, or frame the conflict and take it to the decision-maker. Record a disposition for every comment — accepted, rejected with reasons, or deferred — so each reviewer can see what happened to their input; nothing kills future review effort faster than comments vanishing into a void. Re-issue with change marks or a change summary so re-reviewers read only what moved.

## Context & Usage

Follows every formal review cycle (see 211 for getting the reviews at all) and repeats until the document converges. Two failure modes to manage: feedback that keeps reopening settled decisions (answer from the decision log rather than re-arguing) and the endless-polish loop, where each re-issue attracts new cosmetic comments — cap review rounds and reserve later rounds for material issues only. Emotionally, the skill is treating a heavily marked-up draft as engagement, which it is; the dangerous reviewer is the one who returned it clean.
