# Requirements-elicitation method selection

## Overview

Each elicitation technique has a sweet spot, and picking by habit rather than fit wastes stakeholder goodwill. Interviews buy depth, candour, and safety for political topics, but scale badly and capture opinions one at a time. Workshops build shared understanding and resolve conflicts fast, at the price of scheduling pain and groupthink risk. Observation and shadowing reveal what people actually do — including the workarounds and exceptions they would never think to mention. Surveys reach large, dispersed populations cheaply but only answer the questions you thought to ask. Document analysis mines existing specs, regulations, and reports, indispensable for legacy and compliance detail. Prototyping draws out requirements stakeholders cannot articulate until they see something. Selection weighs the information sought (breadth vs depth, facts vs opinions), stakeholder availability and geography, cultural formality, and budget — and a competent plan always sequences a mix, e.g. document analysis first, interviews for depth, a workshop to confirm and resolve.

## Context & Usage

Decided during elicitation planning and revisited whenever a technique underperforms — which is expected, not a failure (see 184). Match method to stakeholder as well as to information: senior executives get short interviews, not two-day workshops; frontline staff who "can't describe it" get observed instead of interviewed. Record what was elicited from whom by which method, so gaps and single-source requirements are visible.
