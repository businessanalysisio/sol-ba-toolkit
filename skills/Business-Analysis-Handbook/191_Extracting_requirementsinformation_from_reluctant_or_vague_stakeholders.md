# Extracting requirements/information from reluctant or vague stakeholders

## Overview

When open questions produce shrugs ("it just needs to work like it does now, but better"), stop asking harder and change the mechanics. Offer a straw man — a draft process map, wireframe, or list of assumed requirements — because people who cannot articulate what they want can usually tell you what is wrong with what you've drawn. Switch elicitation format: a stakeholder who freezes in workshops may open up one-to-one, in writing, or via a short survey. Replace asking with watching — observe or shadow them doing the actual work, where the real requirements (and workarounds) are visible. Use closed and scenario-based questions ("what happens when a refund exceeds £500?") instead of abstract ones. Diagnose the reluctance itself: fear that the project threatens their job, past projects that wasted their input, or simple time pressure each call for a different fix. As a last resort, engage their manager to legitimise and prioritise their participation — spending that political capital knowingly.

## Context & Usage

Reached for when scheduled elicitation is technically happening but yielding nothing usable — short answers, cancelled sessions, "whatever you think is best". Persisting with the same failing technique wastes the calendar and hardens the reluctance. The stakes are asymmetric: the vague stakeholder is often the one with the deepest operational knowledge, and their unstated requirements are precisely the ones that surface as defects in UAT.
