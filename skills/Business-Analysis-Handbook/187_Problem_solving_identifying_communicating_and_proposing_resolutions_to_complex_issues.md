# Problem solving (identifying, communicating, and proposing resolutions to complex issues)

## Overview

When a project hits a complex issue — conflicting requirements, a technical constraint that breaks the design, a stakeholder impasse — the BA's discipline is to work it systematically instead of reacting. First separate symptom from cause (five whys, fishbone analysis) and size the impact in business terms. Then generate more than one resolution option, including "do nothing", each with pros, cons, cost, and risk — decision-makers deserve a choice, not a fait accompli. Communicate the issue and recommendation together, tailored to the decision-maker's altitude, and log it with an owner and target date so it cannot quietly stall. The BA rarely owns the decision, but almost always owns making the decision easy to take.

## Context & Usage

Triggered whenever progress is blocked by something nobody planned for: mid-build discoveries, requirement conflicts surfacing in review, integration surprises, benefits assumptions collapsing. Distinguish problems the BA should solve directly (analysis gaps, documentation conflicts) from those the BA should frame and escalate (funding, priority calls, political disputes). Escalating early with well-structured options is a strength; sitting on a known issue to avoid awkwardness is the cardinal failure.
