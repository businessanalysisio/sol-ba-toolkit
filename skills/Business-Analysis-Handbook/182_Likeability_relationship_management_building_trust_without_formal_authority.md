# Likeability / relationship management (building trust without formal authority)

## Overview

BAs hold no line authority over the people whose time, knowledge, and sign-off they depend on — cooperation has to be earned. The working tactics are mundane but decisive: invest in small talk and genuine curiosity about the person before diving into business; remember details from previous conversations; deliver quickly on small promises ("I'll send you that map by Friday") to bank credibility before asking for big favours; represent stakeholders' concerns accurately in writing so they see themselves fairly reflected; prefer a desk-side visit or coffee over a calendar invite for sensitive topics; and never blindside a stakeholder in front of their manager — raise problems with them privately first. Likeability here is not popularity-seeking, it is deliberately lowering the cost for busy people of helping you.

## Context & Usage

Matters most at project start (first impressions set the tone for months of elicitation) and whenever the BA must ask for unpopular things: workshop attendance, document reviews, admissions that a process is broken. A stakeholder who trusts the BA volunteers the awkward truths — the workarounds, the political landmines — that never appear in formal sessions. Rebuilding trust after it is damaged costs far more than maintaining it, so protect it even when delivering unwelcome analysis.
