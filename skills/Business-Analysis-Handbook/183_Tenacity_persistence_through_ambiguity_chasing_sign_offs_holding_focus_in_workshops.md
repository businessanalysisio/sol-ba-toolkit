# Tenacity (persistence through ambiguity, chasing sign-offs, holding focus in workshops)

## Overview

Much of BA work is politely refusing to let things drop. Concretely: chase reviews and sign-offs with dated follow-ups and an escalation ladder (reminder → manager copy → sponsor) rather than open-ended "when you get a chance" emails; book a 30-minute walkthrough slot in a reviewer's calendar instead of waiting for them to read a document unprompted; break large documents into chunks so each ask is small enough to say yes to; in workshops, restate the unanswered question until it is answered, and move diversions to a visible parking lot instead of letting them derail the agenda. Tenacity also means staying functional amid ambiguity — when nobody can yet say what the scope is, keep producing draft artifacts that force the ambiguity into the open where it can be resolved.

## Context & Usage

Most needed in the unglamorous middle of a project: requirements half-validated, three sign-offs outstanding, a key SME repeatedly cancelling. The failure mode tenacity prevents is silent drift — documents "under review" for six weeks that everyone later claims never to have agreed to. Calibrate persistence to the relationship: firm on the outcome, flexible and courteous on the method, and escalate through governance rather than nagging when the ladder is exhausted.
