# Requirements prioritization

## Overview

Prioritisation only works when it forces trade-offs, so the mechanics exist to defeat the universal instinct that everything is essential. MoSCoW remains the workhorse — Must (without it the solution is not viable on day one), Should (important, painful to omit, but survivable), Could (desirable if capacity allows), Won't-this-time (explicitly deferred, which reassures its advocates far more than silent omission) — but it needs teeth: cap Musts (a common rule keeps them under ~60% of delivery effort so there is contingency room), and interrogate each claimed Must with "what actually happens if this is missing at go-live?" Prioritise against declared criteria — business value, regulatory necessity, risk reduction, dependency order (a low-value item may enable three high-value ones) — and make the trade-offs visible in a facilitated session rather than harvesting priorities by email, where inflation is free. Business stakeholders own the value ranking; the sponsor arbitrates ties; the BA owns the integrity of the process.

## Context & Usage

Applied when the requirements set first stabilises, at every planning cycle in agile delivery (backlog order *is* prioritisation, continuously re-decided), and urgently whenever time or budget shrinks — a pre-agreed priority scheme turns a descoping crisis into the mechanical removal of Coulds. Re-prioritise when facts change: a regulation lands, a competitor moves, a dependency slips. A priority list from six months ago describes a project that no longer exists.
