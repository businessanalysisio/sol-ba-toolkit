# Comprehensive stakeholder identification

## Overview

The stakeholders who sink projects are the ones nobody thought to invite. A systematic sweep goes well beyond the sponsor's initial list: walk category checklists (customers, suppliers, regulators, auditors, unions, IT operations, service desk, legal, security, finance, marketing, downstream process owners); trace the org chart around every affected function; read process maps and system interface diagrams for actors and receiving systems; and snowball — end every early interview with "who else is affected by this, and who will disagree with what you've just told me?" Include the negative stakeholders (those the change disadvantages) and the invisible ones (support teams who inherit the solution at go-live). Then classify the resulting list by power and interest so engagement effort goes where it matters.

## Context & Usage

Done at initiation, before elicitation planning, and refreshed at each phase boundary and reorganisation — stakeholder lists rot quickly. The cost of a missed stakeholder scales with how late they surface: at scoping they add a requirement; at UAT they add a workstream; at deployment they can veto go-live. Regulators and security/compliance functions are the classic late discoveries, and the most expensive.
