# Redirecting stakeholders who jump straight to solutions

## Overview

Stakeholders routinely arrive with an answer — "we need a new CRM", "just add a dropdown" — rather than a problem. Dismissing the idea creates an enemy; accepting it uncritically bakes a possibly wrong solution into scope. The redirect works like this: acknowledge the suggestion and record it as one candidate option so the stakeholder feels heard; then ask what problem it would solve ("if you had that tomorrow, what would be better?") and walk the why-chain back to the underlying need; restate the result as a solution-agnostic requirement ("the ability to see a customer's full contact history", not "a dropdown on screen 4"); and finally test the proposed solution against that need alongside other options. Often the stakeholder's idea survives — but now it is justified by a need, and alternatives were considered.

## Context & Usage

Constant during early elicitation, and acute when the solution-jumper is senior — a sponsor who has already promised the board a specific product. There the technique is diplomatic: frame analysis as protecting their investment ("let's make sure this delivers the benefit you've committed to") rather than challenging their judgement. The discipline pays off when the stated solution would not actually fix the problem; catching that in a workshop costs an hour, catching it after go-live costs the project.
