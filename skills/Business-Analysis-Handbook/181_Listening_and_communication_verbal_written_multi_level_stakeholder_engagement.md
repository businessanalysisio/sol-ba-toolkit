# Listening and communication (verbal + written, multi-level stakeholder engagement)

## Overview

The foundational BA skill: hearing what stakeholders actually mean, not just what they say, and communicating back at the right altitude for each audience. In practice this means active listening — summarising back in the stakeholder's own vocabulary, probing the gap between the stated request and the underlying need, and noticing what is *not* said (hesitations, topics avoided, body language in workshops). On the outbound side it means shifting register deliberately: a one-page outcome summary with a clear decision ask for executives, screen-and-field-level detail for operational staff, precise models for developers. Written communication carries the audit trail — minutes with actions, owners and dates, decision logs, and playback notes sent after every elicitation session so misunderstandings surface within days rather than at sign-off.

## Context & Usage

Exercised constantly, but most consciously during elicitation interviews, workshops, and when the same message must land with the board, middle management, and frontline users in the same week. A BA who transmits well but listens poorly documents the wrong requirements fluently; a BA who listens well but writes vaguely loses the value of everything they heard. Use deliberate playback ("so what I'm hearing is...") whenever a stakeholder's request sounds like a solution, a complaint, or a contradiction of something another stakeholder said.
