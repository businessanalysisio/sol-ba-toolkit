# Business case justification (benefits and costs)

## Overview

A defensible business case sets several options — always including "do nothing" as the baseline — against the full cost and benefit picture. Costs span build (development, licences, hardware), transition (training, data migration, parallel running, backfill for seconded staff), and run (support, maintenance, subscription renewals) — projects that only cost the build routinely blow their case in year two. Benefits split into tangible (quantified in money or time, each with a named business owner accountable for realising it) and intangible (morale, brand, risk reduction — argued honestly rather than given invented numbers). Standard appraisal maths (payback period, NPV/ROI) makes options comparable, and a sensitivity check shows which assumptions the case lives or dies by. Treat the document as living: re-validate it at each stage gate, because a case that was true at approval can be false by build.

## Context & Usage

Produced at initiation to win funding, and wielded throughout delivery as the arbiter of scope decisions — "does this change strengthen or weaken the case?" is the cleanest scope-creep filter available. The BA's distinctive contribution is honesty under pressure: benefits are chronically inflated to get approval, and a BA who quantifies conservatively, names benefit owners, and documents assumptions gives the organisation a case that survives audit and a baseline that benefits realisation can actually be measured against.
