# Identifying transformational enterprise initiatives

## Overview

Transformation differs in kind from improvement: improvement makes the current business model work better (faster process, lower cost, happier users), while transformation changes the model itself — new markets, new revenue mechanics, a restructured operating model, digitisation that retires whole process families. Identifying genuine transformation candidates starts with scanning the drivers: external forces (regulation, technology shifts, competitor moves, changing customer behaviour — PESTLE territory) and internal signals (strategy–capability gaps, burning platforms, growth ambitions the current model cannot carry). Frame each candidate around business outcomes rather than systems ("halve customer acquisition cost" not "replace the CRM"), test it for genuine strategic alignment, and be honest about classification — relabelling an upgrade programme as "transformation" buys executive attention and then discredits it. True transformation demands multi-year roadmaps with staged value delivery, active CEO-level sponsorship, and organisational readiness assessment, because its risk profile and change burden dwarf a project's.

## Context & Usage

Performed at enterprise level (see 216) during strategic planning cycles, after leadership changes, or when disruption makes the status quo untenable. The BA's analytical contribution is the evidence base: quantifying the driver, mapping which capabilities and value streams the initiative would rebuild, and stress-testing the outcome logic before hundreds of millions are committed. Expect to spend as much effort killing weak candidates as championing strong ones — a portfolio of ten "transformations" is usually one transformation and nine upgrades competing for its funding.
