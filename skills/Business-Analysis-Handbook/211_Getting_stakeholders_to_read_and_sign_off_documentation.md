# Getting stakeholders to read and sign off documentation

## Overview

Emailing a 70-page document with "please review by Friday" produces silence, followed by sign-offs from people who skimmed the summary — approvals that dissolve the first time the document is tested by a dispute. What works instead: run walkthrough sessions where the BA presents the document section by section and captures reactions live, so reviewing becomes attending rather than homework; split the review load, sending each stakeholder only the sections they own plus the executive summary; set explicit deadlines with a stated consequence, using "no response by the 14th will be taken as acceptance" sparingly and with warning, since it manufactures consent rather than agreement; chase individually — a personal "can I get 20 minutes to walk you through your section?" outperforms any group reminder; and tell signatories exactly what their signature commits them to, because informed sign-off is the only kind that holds.

## Context & Usage

The bottleneck moment at every baseline: BRD approval, scope confirmation, design acceptance. Diagnose non-response before escalating — some reviewers are buried (offer the walkthrough), some are avoiding a disagreement they haven't voiced (draw it out one-to-one), and some don't believe their sign-off matters (explain the downstream reliance on it). Persistent refusal after that is a governance issue for the sponsor, escalated with a factual record of every chase (see 183).
