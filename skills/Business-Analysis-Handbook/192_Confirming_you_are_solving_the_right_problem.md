# Confirming you are solving the right problem

## Overview

The most expensive analysis failure is solving the wrong problem well. Guard against it before solutioning starts: write a problem statement covering what is happening, who is affected, how big the impact is, and since when — then play it back to the sponsor for explicit confirmation ("this is the problem we're solving; agreed?"). Test whether the brief describes a symptom or a cause: "sales are down" is a symptom; root-cause analysis may reveal a pricing issue, a competitor move, or a broken fulfilment process, each demanding a different project. Validate the problem with data (volumes, costs, complaint counts) rather than accepting anecdote, and check the problem actually connects to a business objective — a real but strategically irrelevant problem does not deserve the budget.

## Context & Usage

Belongs at project initiation and whenever the BA inherits a pre-scoped brief, a pre-chosen solution, or a project already in flight that feels aimless. Revisit it when scope arguments keep recurring — chronic scope conflict is usually a symptom that stakeholders never agreed on the problem. Pushing back on a sponsor's framing takes nerve, but a two-week problem-definition exercise is cheap insurance against a two-year misdirected delivery.
