# Meeting facilitation (running productive meetings/workshops toward a clear outcome)

## Overview

Every session the BA runs should be able to answer "what will be different when this meeting ends?" The mechanics: circulate an agenda in advance with a stated purpose and expected outcome per item; invite only people who contribute to that outcome; timebox each item and enforce it; open by confirming the objective and ground rules. During the session, balance airtime — round-robins and directed questions to draw out silent attendees, and "let's hear from someone we haven't yet" to contain dominant ones; park off-topic issues on a visible list with owners; drive to explicit decisions rather than vague agreement ("so we are deciding X — any objection?"). Close by reading back decisions and actions with owners and dates, then issue minutes within 24–48 hours while memory is fresh.

## Context & Usage

Applied to requirements workshops, scope-setting sessions, design walkthroughs, and review meetings — any gathering where the BA is accountable for the output rather than a mere attendee. Facilitation earns its keep hardest when stakeholders disagree: the facilitator's neutrality (managing the process, not advocating a position) is what allows conflict to surface safely and be resolved in the room instead of in corridor politics afterwards.
