# Requirements change management

## Overview

Once requirements are baselined at sign-off, change stops being editing and becomes governance. The cycle: capture the change request formally (what, why, who is asking, what it is worth); assess impact using the traceability web — affected requirements, design, build, test assets, cost, schedule, and the business case; put the assessed request to the agreed decision authority (change control board, sponsor, or product owner depending on scale and delivery method); then, on approval, update the baseline, re-version the documents, propagate the change through the RTM, and communicate to everyone building or testing against the old truth. Keep a change log — it explains at project end why delivery differs from the original BRD, and its patterns are diagnostic (a flood of changes in one area means the original analysis there was thin). One judgement call recurs constantly: clarification versus change. Restating an ambiguous requirement more precisely is a clarification; anything that alters scope, cost, or behaviour goes through the process, however small it looks.

## Context & Usage

Active from baseline to delivery. The process must be proportionate to survive: a change regime so heavy that stakeholders route around it (the "quiet word with a developer" channel) is worse than a light one that gets used. Agile reframes rather than removes this — the backlog absorbs change continuously and re-prioritisation is the decision mechanism — but sprint scope, agreed contracts, and the business case still need explicit change decisions. The BA's core contribution is the honest impact assessment: "yes, it's a small field — it also touches the interface spec, two reports, and the migration mapping."
