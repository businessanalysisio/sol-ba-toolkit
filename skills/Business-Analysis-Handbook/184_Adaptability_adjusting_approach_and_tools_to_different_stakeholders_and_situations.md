# Adaptability (adjusting approach and tools to different stakeholders and situations)

## Overview

No single elicitation or documentation technique survives contact with every stakeholder, so the practised BA carries alternatives and switches without ego. When a workshop stalls because two managers dominate, swap to one-to-one interviews for the quieter attendees. When a stakeholder cannot engage with a written specification, show a wireframe or prototype instead. When the organisation is informal and document-averse, trade the 60-page BRD for a wiki and story map; when it is regulated, do the opposite. Adaptability also spans methodology — moving fluently between waterfall-style up-front specification and agile just-in-time refinement as the delivery approach demands — and re-planning the analysis approach itself when scope, sponsor, or timeline shifts mid-project rather than executing an obsolete plan.

## Context & Usage

Invoked at two moments: up front, when choosing the analysis approach for a new engagement (culture, geography, delivery method, stakeholder mix), and mid-flight, when a chosen technique is visibly failing — attendance dropping, feedback superficial, artifacts unread. The trigger to adapt is evidence, not boredom: change the approach when the current one stops producing information or agreement, and be explicit with stakeholders about what is changing and why so the switch reads as responsiveness, not chaos.
